
戶籍志
- Section from the Wanli edition says that Anping consists of 4 tu (40 jia) within Jinjiang. “安海屬晉江八都之統圖，各統甲十，甲各統十花戶。今多少不一，四圖各十甲，共四十班，排年應班錢糧之役，上供國賦，中應差役。其戶籍，丁力，差役各色在府縣冊籍，茲不復贅。” A note appended in Kangxi 31 explains that the registers were thrown into disarray after the coastal ban; when the residents of the region were allowed to return, the old tax levies (which resumed in the 6th year after a tax reprieve) were too burdensome for their reduced numbers and wealth; therefore in Kangxi 30 a 泉州府高公，晉江縣李公 （知府 知縣？）performed a resurvey (清丈) which presumably lowered tax quotas, finally allowing the locals to 知有室家之好，永無失所之患也. 
	- The author of this note was 陳夢弼/子夔

公署志
- In Jiajing 巳末 an office for visiting officials (公館) was built in Anping next to the 石井書院. (Connected to military needs or to the growth of commerce?). It became a yamen in Wanli 35 and came to be used for legal cases even from neighboring prefectures. In Wanli 34 a 駐鎮 was established in Anhai.
	- Includes the text of two stone stelae dug up from the site of the Ming garrison office, detailing its construction. The first, written by 黃汝良 in the Wanli years, explains that an office was necessary to oversee the troubles resulting from Anhai’s growth due to maritime trade after the sea ban was lifted. The 縉紳父老 of Anhai had requested that either an 邑 or a 鎮 be established at the site of the Jiajing era fort (connection between the wokou era fortification wave and a renewed push of „urbanization,“ in the sense of establishing corporate towns in the dense sea of villages). A zhixian named 顧士琦 requested to the throne that Anhai be made a xian following the example of Haicheng. 
	- Huang Fengxiang‘s inscription states that Anping is a 巨鎮 comparable in culture to a 郡城 (thanks in part to the influence of Zhu Xi), and that its riches collected from the ocean trade are second to none. 

選舉志
- The first jinshi from Anping were two brothers and a son from the same family who passed in the same year (Song Yongxi 乙酉): 高鑌，高鎰，高涥 (鑌‘s son). Throughout the Song the Gaos of Anping produced 55 (!!) jinshi, of whom at least 9 were the direct descendants of the first 3. A 高惠連 who passed in 咸平 became Bingbu Shangshu and 開國侯, and had a son and two grandsons become jinshi. 
- Ming era jinshi on 181-184. Before the Hongzhi reign Anping produced 3 jinshi, compared to 21 in the Jiajing through Wanli years. Most prominent included 黃汝良, Libu Shangshu; 陳亮采, Jiangxi Anchashi; 蘇琰, Guizhou Yushi. 

人物志
- First biography is 高惠連. His father and uncle settled in Anping to watch over their ancestral grave, of a forefather who had served in office in Quanzhou during the Tang. Mostly notes that when he was stationed in Quanzhou, many locals passed the exam and credited him for it. Includes an epitaph written by Wang Anshi. 
- Biographies on merchants were entirely added by the 1987 editors. 
- 253 biography of 貞烈陳英娘 tells the story of a wife whose husband and brother were killed in the Manila massacre of 1603. A footnote quotes 李光縉，景璧集 as saying that in the Manila massacre, “安平無一人得脫.” Compare to the Anhayes of 1639!
- Another (烈女黃世) account of the wife of 陳憲章, who commits suicide after his death in the Manila massacre. “安海以商為業，雖文身赤髮之國亦到。呂宋比諸夷較近。但此禽獸，狡猾無常性。萬曆癸卯間，無故殺華人在其國者三萬餘人。“ Her husband, son, brother-in-law, and four servants are killed. 
	- This is from Huang Fengxiang


Juan 10 has an essay listing Anhai Huaqiao based on gathered information from genealogies (meaning that it mostly lists people who died abroad), starting page 434. The first group is those from Luzon; I counted 18 killed in the 1603 massacre, plus one listed as dying in 1604, who may have died the same way or died as a prisoner/slave. 4 killed in the “夷變” of 1639. 
- Almost all of these people were surnamed 黃，陳，顏; common surnames in Anping, but also a sign of the limited genealogical sources this information was drawn from.
