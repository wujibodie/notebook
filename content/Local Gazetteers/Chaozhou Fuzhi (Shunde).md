- The section as a whole contextualizes Lin Feng as part of a long-running maritime-piratical network tracing back to the 1550s: 
1. **Xu Dong (許棟):** The **foundational figure**. A major pirate leader active in the 1540s-1550s. He operated from bases like Nanao (南澳) and had connections to Japanese traders/pirates (倭).
    
2. **Wu Ping (吳平):** Originally a servant or follower of Xu Dong. After Xu Dong's decline, Wu Ping inherited his forces and became the **preeminent pirate chief** of the 1560s. Defeated Yu Dayou in 1565, but was then beaten and killed in 66.
    
3. **Lin Guoxian (林國憲):** A Raoping bandit who "勾倭入寇" (colluded with the Japanese). The text states: **"林鳳、林達塢皆其侄孫, 吳平共匿婿也"** — This is the crucial link. It identifies Lin Feng and another captain (Lin Dawu) as **nephews/grandnephews of Lin Guoxian**, and states they were **both sons-in-law ("匿婿") of Wu Ping**. This creates a direct familial and political bond between Wu Ping and Lin Feng.
    
4. **Lin Feng (林鳳):** The most famous international actor. As a nephew and son-in-law within the network, he rose to lead a major faction after Wu Ping's fall.
    
5. **Zeng Yiben (曾一本):** Explicitly called a **"吳平黨也" (a partisan/follower of Wu Ping)**. He became a major leader in his own right after Wu Ping's demise.
    
6. **Other Associated Captains:** The network included numerous other captains like **Hong Dizhen (洪迪珍), Li Guozhu (李國助)**, and many who are only named by their nicknames (e.g., "Eagle Nose"). They operated in a fluid federation.
