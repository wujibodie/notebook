- Inspiration for writing on Jiao Hong is search for a "transitional" figure between Ming xinxue and Qing kaozheng; Jiao Hong is representative of this transition, as well as of the sanjiao heyi context in which it was accomplished. 3
- Jiao Hong was a student of Geng Dingxiang's, and served as the student-principal of Geng's academy in Nanjing, the 崇正书院. 
 - Wang Daokun wrote a muzhiming for Jiao Hong's father, which traces his ancestry back to an early Ming general, 焦源, from Langya, Shandong. Consider the idea of Wang as a vocal proponent for military men, as well as of merchants; his extensive comparisons of the vocations of wen and wu, positive biographies of officers, etc.
 - Demonstrates that Jiao Hong was a student of major Taizhou School figures like Lu Rufang and Geng Dingxiang, and maintained lifelong friendships with a number of Taizhou figures (as well as Chan/Daoist thinkers). Dai's proof that Jiao believed in "考据" is far weaker, basically just assuming that since he took 7 keju exams, he must have read a lot of books… 7
 - Summarizes scholarship on Jiao Hong as focusing either on his place in late Ming sanjiao heyi thinking, or 实学 (see 陈鼓应，明清实学思潮史). Yu Ying-shih argued that Jiao Hong's xinxue and kaoju tendencies were fundamentally independent, against Qian Qingzu who saw them as constituting a comprehensive break from Cheng-Zhu orthodoxy.
     - The two main "problems" in Jiao Hong studies: 1. The Ch'ien-Yu debate on whether Jiao was a pivotal figure in the turning point of late Ming thought towards empiricism, or merely a "sensitive soul" who had little creative impact. 2. The various aspects of Jiao's career and thinking – xinxue, literature, historiography, evidential study, and sanjiao heyi thinking have been treated independently, and rarely connected to one another.
