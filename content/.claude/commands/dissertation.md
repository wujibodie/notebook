## Core Thesis

Minnan was a net food importer from the 11th century onward yet flourished as a leading commercial center—breaking the Ming fiscal system's agrarian assumption. This forced deeper commercialization to pay taxes, making the region a "laboratory for fiscal reform." The dissertation traces how maritime commerce, the wokou crisis, fiscal reform, and social transformation incorporated this region into Ming imperial systems while maintaining distinct regional characteristics.

## Analytical Frameworks

- **Exteriorization** (李之琇): state and society moving progressively outward toward the seas
- **Fiscal-military deadlock**: money needed to secure coast; security needed to generate money
- **Equalization** (均平/均徭): iterative, administrator-driven local implementation—not top-down policy
- **Bureaucratic vs. charismatic authority**: Liu Yaohui (reform existing structures) vs. Yu Dayou (embedded local networks)
- **Legibility**: the maritime economy operating across and escaping administrative categories
