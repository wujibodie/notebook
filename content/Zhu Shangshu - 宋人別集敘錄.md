- Su Shi's works were traditionally compiled into 7 parts: _Dongpo (Qian) Ji_ (东坡集/前集, 40 vols), _Hou Ji_ (后集, 20 vols), _Zouyi_ (奏议, 15 vols), _Neizhi_ (内制, 10 vols), _Waizhi_ (外制, 3 vols), _He Tao Shi_ (和陶诗, 4 vols), and then the _Yingzhao Ji_ (应诏集, 10 vols) which was only added posthumously. 
	- The Dongpo Ji was compiled by Su Shi himself, while the 後集 was put together from his post-Hangzhou works, by one Liu Mian 劉沔, though with Su's knowledge and approval. 
	- Another collection published in Su's lifetime but not part of the later canonical set was the 眉山集, which Su Zhe found was being read in Yanjing under the Liao. Another circulating collection, the 續添錢塘集, was submitted as evidence in Su's trial (the 烏臺詩案). 
	- The politically-focused essays of the Yingzhao Ji started to circulate with major collections of Su's writings in the Southern Song, though it's not clear exactly when it entered into the 七集 ("應詔集究為何人何本所補，尚待研究." 406). Its addition seems to track Su's canonization as a model of good writing, including for students. 
	- There were numerous circulating editions of the 六集: a 杭/臨安本，a 蜀本，a 吉州本，and a 建安本. By the Ming, most prints of Su's works followed the 七集 format. By the Qing, most original Song prints of the 七集 had been lost. Various partial copies survive of a late northern Song edition printed in 黃州，but only 4 of its 集 survive anywhere, and only the 和陶詩 is fully intact. Then there are three versions printed in the reign of 宋孝宗 held in Japan, one with 18 characters per line and one with 12 (per Fu Zengxiang, probably a Hangzhou print) (414-416). There is another Xiaozong edition, the 小字本, taken to be a cheap and error-ridden commercial copy from a time when Su was coming back into fashion.
	- Another edition inspected by Liu Shanrong, from a Sichuan printing, seems to be the 眉山功德寺 print attested by 洪邁 in the Southern Song. 
	- 422-3: Concludes that the Song editions, while scattered and incomplete, are highly valuable for checking mistakes in later prints and developing the modern critical editions.
- Su's highest political post achieved was 兵部尚書.
- Su is credited with ~4,800 prose works, ~2,300 poems, 300 詞. 
- Southern Song collections of Su's work expanded beyond the "canonical" 六/七集，with one Ming reprint of a 南宋 volume listing 24 different 集, including thematic poetry collections and biji. In general, the earliest good and complete editions of the 七集 are based on the 成化本. However, this version contained a number of errors, particularly the inclusion of falsely attributed poems in the 續集. Fu Zengxiang has a passionate denunciation of these poems' inauthenticity. 424
- The Chenghua 七集 was reprinted in Jiajing 13 (1534) in an edition sponsored by the Jiangxi 布政司. This version has a very different 續集, as the Jiajing editors cut dozens of poems and essays, mostly where they simply repeated ones appearing earlier in the collection (especially the 和陶詩); thus Fu prefers this edition to the Chenghua one. 
- Another popular Ming edition was the 蘇文忠公集, probably of the Chenghua/Hongzhi period; this was largely the same material as the Chenghua collection, but arranged by genre (類編). A similar effort called the 東坡全集 was printed in Wanli 37. This 全集 is fairly common now and seems to have been the basis for the major Kangxi edition of 蔡士英. 2 more late Wanli prints on 429-30:
	- The Wanli 34 蘇文忠公集 edited by Mao Wei 茅維. Mao notes that he could not find a good complete copy of Su's works in 楚，越, so was inspired to print an edition based on a 閣本 provided him by Jiao Hong (!), but incorporating extra materials from various other sources, ending with a 75 volume 全集. Liu Shangrong is not impressed by the printing quality, but considers it a very complete and well-arranged edition. 429
		- Note that Mao Wei is also responsible for an important Ming commentary edition, the "新王本" of the 百家注.
        - I suspect the "Chu" above might refer to Anhui, per Wang Daokun's usage. 
		- Mao Wei was son of the official Mao Kun, and appears at the end of his Mingshi biography: 茅坤，字順甫，歸安人。[嘉靖十七年](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Category:1538%E5%B9%B4_\(%E6%8F%90%E5%8F%8A\) "Category:1538年 (提及)")進士。歷知青陽、丹徒二縣。母憂，服闋，遷禮部主事，移吏部稽勛司，坐累，謫廣平通判。屢遷廣西兵備僉事，轄府江道。坤雅好談兵。瑤賊據鬼子諸砦，殺陽朔令。朝議大征，總督應檟以問坤。坤曰：「大征非兵十萬不可，餉稱之，今猝不能集，而賊已據險為備。計莫若雕剿。條入殲其魁，他部必襲，謀自全，此便計也。」檟善之，悉以兵事委坤。連破十七砦，晉秩二等。民立祠祀之。遷大名兵備副使，總督楊博嘆為奇才，特薦於朝。為忌者所中，追論其先任貪污狀，落職歸。時倭事方急，胡宗憲延之幕中，與籌兵事，奏請為福建副使。吏部持之，乃已。家人橫於里，**為巡按龐尚鵬所劾**，遂褫冠帶。坤既廢，用心計治生，家大起。年九十，卒於萬曆二十九年　坤善古文，最心折唐順之。**順之喜唐、宋諸大家文，所著文編，唐、宋人自韓、柳、歐、三蘇、曾、王八家外，無所取，故坤選《八大家文鈔》**。其書盛行海內，鄉里小生無不知茅鹿門者。鹿門，坤別號也。**少子維，字孝若，能詩，與同郡臧懋循、吳稼竳、吳夢陽，並稱四子。嘗詣闕上書，希得召見，陳當世大事，不報**。
	- The 萬曆 36 edition by 康丕揚 of 揚州, the 重編東坡先生外集, has a preface by Jiao Hong. The editor was one 毛九苞. This edition is valuable because it includes certain texts not found elsewhere, but it also includes some false attributions and uses low-quality materials. This edition seems to draw on otherwise lost Song predecessors using the 外集 name. 
		- Note that the printing of this text was probably the occasion for Li Zhi's 書蘇文忠公外紀後
- There were also numerous smaller selected editions of Su's poetry and other writings in the mid-late Ming, which Zhu doesn't cover in this book. 431
- Two versions of Su's works were common in the Qing: reprints of the Chenghua edition, and Cai Shiying's 東坡全集, a 115 volume 類編全集. Cai's version was missing various texts and is considered 殆非善本. 432
- A 1908-9 reprint of the Chenghua 東坡七集, while considered of low quality by Fu, served as the basis for later modern reprints (which checked it against the Jiajing version and 錢 critical edition).
- Zhu's comment on the Ming-Qing editions: Although the 七集 form was traditionally more respected, in part because it tended to be printed in better quality, the 類編 versions are invaluable for modern scholarship because by breaking up the standard format they allowed for additional works to be brought in, and because their structure makes them easier to consult.
- The 1986 中華書局 edition is based on the Ming era  蘇文忠公全集 by Mao Wei, but was edited in consultation with many other editions, and includes a 佚文彙編 for works not fitting into the 七集. 


- Overall reflections on this section: the two most flourishing periods of Su collections after his own lifetime seem to have been the reign of Song Xiaozong and the late Wanli reign. Speculations about why (ask class for ideas first): these are both periods of widespread monetization and cheap printing, and rise of a strong "middle stratum" of both urban and rural literati. However, they may also be periods when Su Shi was particularly fashionable: Zhu explicitly says that cheap commercial prints abounded in Xiaozong's reign because the emperor himself extolled Su's writings, while it may be that Su fit the taste of late Ming elites, as exemplified by a tastemaker like Jiao Hong. We can speculate about where Su Shi fit into polemics about good writing in both eras. 
	- Also note the differences between commercial printing house editions and state- or gentry-sponsored ones (e.g. the 1534 Jiangxi 全集 vs. the Wanli 外集). 

王狀元集百家注
-  A compilation of various commentaries on Su's poetry which was printed in Southern Song Fujian. Of the earlier commentary traditions, only the 五注，十注 survive. Most of the various commentary selections include the 注 of 趙夔. The Wang 百家 edition seems to have drawn mainly from the 八，十注. 
- Since the 百家注 was printed at Jian'an in an atmosphere of fierce commercial competition, there are doubts that 王十朋's name may have been falsely added. It was also clearly based on a number of earlier 類注 which had been popular in the Song and Yuan eras (listed on 438-448.) Basic history of these editions was that 類編 with commentaries were printed in the Song, then collated and edited in the Yuan, with the most complete revised version being the Yuan 盧陵本. Then in the Ming there were two common 類注: the Chenghua 汪氏 edition and the Jiajing 劉氏 version.
	- Oldest and most prestigious 百家注 print was the Jian'an 黃善夫 edition. 
	- #2 was printed by the 泉州市舶司!
	- There was also a Ming "新王本," which was actually edited by Mao Wei, but copied and reprinted by a Wang Yongji of Wuxi in the Chongzhen reign. 
注東坡先生詩
- Annotated Poems of Master Dongpo," by **Shi Yuanzhi (施元之), Gu Xi (顾禧), and Shi Su (施宿)**.
- Poems are arranged **chronologically**. It features two layers of annotation: **interlinear notes** (citing allusions) by Shi Yuanzhi and Gu Xi, and **prefatory notes** (detailing historical context, occasions, and persons) by Shi Su, which were highly praised.
- Qing synthetic commentaries (463-5):
	- **Feng Yingliu (冯应榴)**: Created _Su Wenzhong Shi Hezhu_ (苏文忠诗合注), combining the strengths of the Wang (classified), Shi (chronological), and Zha (supplemental) annotations, with extensive research.
	- **Wang Wengao (王文诰)**: Compiled _Su Wenzhong Gong Shi Bianzhu Jicheng_ (苏文忠公诗编注集成), which, while based on Feng's work, streamlined the annotations and added a detailed chronicle (_Zong'an_ 总案). This later served as the base text for the modern _Su Shi Shiji_ (苏轼诗集) punctuated edition.
