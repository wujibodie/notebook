Theobald, Ulrich. "Control the Uncontrollable: The Monetary Policy of the Ming and Qing Empires." In *Money in Asia (1200-1900): Small Currencies in Social and Political Contexts*, edited by Jane Kate Leonard and Ulrich Theobald, 15-46. Leiden: Brill, 2015.

Ulrich Theobald's chapter on Ming-Qing monetary policy provides crucial context for understanding the silverization of Fujian's fiscal system during the 16th century. Theobald emphasizes that "in China seigniorage played a very marginal role in monetary politics" because Confucian ideology held that money production and issuance should be "beneficial to the people" (利民) rather than profit-seeking (p. 23). This ideological commitment meant that production costs for copper coins consistently surpassed face value, as the government prioritized high-quality cash over profitable minting. The only periods when the Chinese government profited from seigniorage were the very late Ming—when new troops fighting the Jurchens required financing—and the early Qing decades before treasury consolidation.

This ideological framework illuminates why silver emerged as the solution to Ming fiscal pressures despite the state's inability to control silver supply. As Theobald notes, citing Kuroda Akinobu, the production cost of small-denomination copper coins was particularly high relative to larger coins, creating structural incentives for monetary substitution. For Fujian's fiscal reforms documented in this dissertation—from the 1520 *bafen fa* to the Single Whip reforms of the 1570s-1580s—Theobald's analysis suggests that the turn toward silver was not simply a pragmatic response to commercialization but was shaped by ideological constraints on state monetary policy. The state's inability to profit from copper coinage while maintaining ideological commitments to "benefiting the people" created openings for silver—imported through Fujian's maritime trade networks—to become the medium through which labor and grain levies were commuted, ultimately transforming the relationship between state extraction and market mechanisms in one of China's most commercially developed regions.
