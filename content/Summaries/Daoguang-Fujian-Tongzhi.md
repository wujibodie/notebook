《道光福建通志》(Daoguang Fujian Provincial Gazetteer)

The Daoguang era (1821-1850) Fujian Provincial Gazetteer preserves a crucial memorial by Haifang Tongzhi Shen Zhi (海防同知沈植) to Governor Liu Yaohui (劉堯誨) regarding the establishment of maritime customs in Wanli 2 (1574), documenting the institutional framework for overseas trade taxation that emerged from Liu's governorship. The passage records Shen's objections to prohibiting ships from wintering overseas, arguing that navigational knowledge (using the compass/指南針) and regular trade routes had become routine, with ships traveling to the Eastern and Western Oceans, Taiwan (雞籠淡水), and Guangdong's coastal ports. Shen articulated the rationale for differentiated treatment of Japan-bound trade: ships returning from Japan necessarily waited until the ninth or tenth month for wind patterns, and since Japan offered only gold and silver rather than goods, late-year returns without cargo clearly indicated intermediary trade with Japanese pirates. Ships with permits engaging in such activity would be prosecuted for "communicating with Japanese pirates" (通倭罪), while crew members and harbor masters were authorized to report violations for rewards.

The memorial documents the fiscal infrastructure of Liu Yaohui's maritime customs system: trading permits for Eastern and Western Oceans, Taiwan, and Guangdong ports were issued by the Coastal Defense Office (*Haifang Guan*), with taxes graded by destination—3 taels for Eastern/Western Oceans, 1 tael for Taiwan and Guangdong ports, later increased to 6 and 2 taels respectively. Wanli 18 (1590) saw the abolition of commercial fishing permits, transferring authority to coastal counties. The passage reveals the sophisticated understanding of maritime navigation, seasonal wind patterns, and commodity flows that underpinned Ming coastal administration. By Wanli 2, the system generated 6,000 taels annually for military expenses, institutionalized through seventeen regulations (*Haishui Jinyao* 海稅禁約). This documentation demonstrates how Qing provincial gazetteers preserved detailed records of late Ming fiscal innovations, providing crucial evidence for understanding the institutionalization of maritime trade taxation as a response to both fiscal crisis and the recognition of Fujian's commercial maritime economy.
