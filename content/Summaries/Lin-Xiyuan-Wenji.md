Lin Xiyuan 林希元. *Tong'an Lin Ciya Xiansheng Wenji* 同安林次崖先生文集 [Collected Works of Lin Ciya of Tong'an]. Qianlong 18 edition (1753). Reprinted in *Siku Quanshu Cunmu Congshu* 四庫全書存目叢書, Jibu 75, 409-761. Jinan: Qilu Shushe, 1997.

Lin Xiyuan's (ca. 1480-1560) collected works represent a crucial primary source for understanding the perspective of a prominent Minnan literatus navigating the tensions between Confucian orthodoxy, maritime commerce, and coastal defense during the Jiajing reign. A jinshi from Zhengde 12 (1517), Lin was dismissed from office due to controversies over Annam policy and spent his subsequent twenty-year retirement engaged in scholarly pursuits, local history compilation, and what the sources describe as "selling his writing services"—evidenced by the substantial volume of prefaces, tomb inscriptions, and elegies in the collection. The works demonstrate Lin's position within Cheng-Zhu orthodoxy and opposition to Wang Yangming's teachings, following the same intellectual trajectory as contemporaries Zhang Yue and Chen Chen.

Particularly valuable for Minnan maritime history is Lin's "Discussion of Maritime Bandits" (*Haikou Yi* 海寇議, Juan 11), which identifies pirates' mobility as their key advantage: "Setting sail with the wind, coming and going up and down—when large forces assemble, they gaze at the ocean and flee far away; when pursuit relaxes slightly, they take advantage of emptiness and return" (Juan 11, 320-321). Lin recommends recruiting toughs from Dongguan who share pirates' maritime skills to force them into traps, and argues against "pacifying" (*fu* 撫) pirates when they request it, since this indicates precisely the moment when their resources are depleted. The collection also documents Lin's relationship with Yu Dayou (俞大猷), whom Lin claims to have tutored early in his career and praises as Fujian's only effective commander against the wokou. Brook's research reveals that Lin personally benefited from the 16th-century dissolution of Buddhist monasteries when an admiring local magistrate granted him land from an abolished temple, which became the foundation of his lineage estate.
