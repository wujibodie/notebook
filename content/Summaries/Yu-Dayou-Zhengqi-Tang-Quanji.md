Yu Dayou 俞大猷, *Zhengqi Tang Quanji* 正氣堂集 (Collected Works from the Hall of Upright Qi), compiled by Lin Fengyi 林鳳儀 et al., original compilation dated to late Ming dynasty (reprint, Beijing: Beijing chubanshe, 200?).

This extensive collection comprises the writings of Yu Dayou (1503-1580), one of the Ming dynasty's preeminent military commanders and strategists who played a central role in coastal defense against the *wokou* pirates during the Jiajing reign. The work spans multiple volumes including the main collection (*Zhengqi Tang Ji*), a supplementary collection (*Xuji* 續集), and additional compilations (*Yuji* 餘集), totaling over 900 pages of primary documentation. The collection encompasses memorials (*zouyi*), letters (*shu*), military memoranda (*jie*), and treatises on naval and land warfare, providing an unparalleled window into Ming military administration and coastal defense strategies during the mid-16th century crisis.

The collection is particularly valuable for understanding the Fujian-Guangdong coastal region's military operations, including detailed correspondence regarding the campaigns against Lin Feng's pirate fleet in 1574-1575. Yu's letters to Liu Yaohui and other officials document the strategic deployment of Fujian's distinctive "white junks" (*baicao* 白艚) and the challenges of inter-provincial coordination in maritime security. His *Xi Hai Jin Shi* (洗海近事) appendix provides contemporary documentation of amphibious operations and garrison management. The work reveals the tension between bureaucratic military structures and charismatic local leadership, as well as the fiscal pressures driving military professionalization through recruited troops (*mubing*) rather than hereditary *weisuo* garrisons. For historians of Minnan and Ming fiscal-military systems, this collection offers irreplaceable documentation of how regional commanders negotiated between central military directives and local resource constraints during an era of profound institutional transformation.
