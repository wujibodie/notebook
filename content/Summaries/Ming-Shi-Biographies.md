*Ming Shi* 明史 [History of Ming]. Compiled by Zhang Tingyu 張廷玉 et al. 1739. Reprint, Beijing: Zhonghua Shuju, 1974.

The *Ming Shi* (History of Ming), compiled during the early Qing Dynasty under Zhang Tingying, provides standardized biographies of major Ming officials that serve as essential reference points for understanding 16th-century Fujian and coastal defense history. This document contains notes on several key figures relevant to Minnan's military and fiscal transformation during the Jiajing-Wanli transition (1522-1620). Notable biographies include Geng Dingxiang (耿定向), a Wang Yangming follower who supported Zhang Juzheng during the Duoqing controversy; Guo Yingpin (郭應聘), who served under Liu Yaohui in Guangxi and later as Liangguang Zongdu; and Ling Yunyi (凌雲翼), who oversaw the latter phases of the Lin Feng campaigns in Guangdong and Fujian.

Particularly significant are the biographies of military reformers Tan Lun (譚綸) and Qi Jiguang (戚繼光), which document their campaigns against the wokou (倭寇) pirates in Fujian during the 1560s and subsequent transfer to northern frontier defense. The *Ming Shi* biography of Tan Lun emphasizes his collaborative relationship with Qi Jiguang and their joint importance to Zhang Juzheng's reform program, as noted in the editorial comment (*zan*) that these figures represent Zhang's cultivation of military talent. The biography of Qi Jiguang provides detailed accounts of his tactical innovations, military training methods, and the campaigns that pacified Fujian's coastal region. However, the *Ming Shi* treatment of Wang Daokun—relegating him to an appendix of Wang Shizhen's biography—illustrates the text's privileging of certain categories of official achievement over others, particularly literary versus military or administrative distinction.
