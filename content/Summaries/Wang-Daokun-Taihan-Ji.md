Wang Daokun 汪道昆. *Taihan Ji* 太函集. Various editions, compiled during the Ming Dynasty.

Wang Daokun's *Taihan Ji* (Collected Works from the Great Enclosure) is a massive collection of literary works by this prominent 16th-century figure, best known as a literary associate of Wang Shizhen and member of the archaist (*fugu*) movement. The collection contains approximately 1,700 poems plus extensive prose, including prefaces, tomb inscriptions, and official documents. The work is particularly significant for its documentation of Wang's relationships with key military and political figures of the Jiajing and Longqing reigns, including Qi Jiguang (戚繼光), Tan Lun (譚綸), and Zhang Juzheng (張居正). Several notable pieces document the transfer of military commanders between southern and northern frontier postings, including a preface for Qi Jiguang's transfer to the northern frontier that quotes Qi's assessment of the comparative difficulties of defending against maritime versus steppe threats.

From a historiographical perspective, the *Taihan Ji* presents complex interpretive challenges. Wang Daokun appears in the *Ming Shi* (History of Ming) only as an appendix to Wang Shizhen's biography, reflecting his reputation as primarily a literary rather than political figure. However, his collected works suggest deeper engagement with military affairs and court politics than this official assessment acknowledges. The collection includes a substantial birthday encomium for Zhang Juzheng's father that provides insight into the reform network's patronage relationships. The discrepancy between Wang's literary reputation and his political connections raises important questions about how the Ming state categorized and remembered its officials, and how modern scholars should weigh literary sources against official historiography when reconstructing political history.
