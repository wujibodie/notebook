《萬曆泉州府志》(Wanli Quanzhou Prefectural Gazetteer)

The Wanli era Quanzhou Prefectural Gazetteer (compiled circa early 17th century under Xunfu Yang of Meicheng, with Huang Fengxiang as chief editor) represents a comprehensive revision of prefectural records following significant demographic and fiscal disruption during the Jiajing wokou crisis. The editors consciously followed the organizational model of the Longqing (1567-1572) gazetteer, which reduced the 17 categories of the Jiajing edition to 8 more manageable divisions and controversially eliminated the traditional *Shihuo Zhi* (食貨志, Treatise on Food and Money), folding fiscal information into the *Banji Zhi* (版籍志). This organizational choice reflected a conceptual shift viewing population as the source of wealth (有人斯有土), demonstrating how late Ming gazetteer compilation embodied evolving understandings of political economy. The Wanli edition notably excised detailed records of lands seized from Buddhist monasteries that had appeared in the Longqing edition, citing concerns about sparking disputes now that these lands had been properly surveyed and integrated into regular tax registers.

The gazetteer's demographic and fiscal sections document the catastrophic impact of the wokou crisis and subsequent recovery. Following Jiajing era raids, household and population registers dropped by 60-70% (戶口十損六七), though the editors note that forty years later, general prosperity had recovered "without consulting the registers." Detailed statistics trace this recovery: from Jiajing 10 (1531) totals of 42,337 households and 212,903 persons, to Jiajing 41 (1562) figures of 48,243 households but only 169,935 persons (reflecting wartime population loss), recovering to Wanli 36 (1608) figures of 48,704 households and 190,349 persons. The editors document how local officials interpreted regulations differently: in Jinjiang, Nan'an, Hui'an, and Dehua, officials reduced ding numbers while slightly increasing households; in Tong'an, Anxi, and Yongchun, uncontrolled corruption among village clerks led to inflated household and population numbers that harmed the people. The gazetteer provides detailed land survey records from the Wanli 10 (1582) Fangtian (方田) survey, noting dramatically different implementation across counties: Jinjiang's official Peng Guoguang conducted careful three-tier land classification that increased registered land by two-fifths, while Hui'an and Anxi merely redistributed "floating tax" burdens evenly among existing taxpayers.
