《正德漳州府志》(Zhengde Zhangzhou Prefectural Gazetteer)

The Zhengde era (1506-1521) Zhangzhou Prefectural Gazetteer represents the earliest comprehensive administrative record of Zhangzhou during the Ming dynasty, compiled before the major transformations of the Jiajing era including the wokou crisis and the rise of Yuegang as an international trade port. The gazetteer's preface to the *Shanchuan* (Mountains and Rivers) section articulates a fundamental premise of Ming local gazetteer compilation: that geographic environment shapes human character (山川者，其人物所自生乎), with different landscapes producing distinctive personalities through the interaction of yin and yang forces. This environmental determinism justified detailed geographic documentation as a tool for understanding regional distinctiveness and predicting official success. The gazetteer documents the prefectural leadership of Chen Hongmo (陳洪謨), who features prominently as the sitting prefect responsible for multiple initiatives including expelling Buddhists who had occupied land originally intended for a Zhu Xi academy, and building it as a Confucian school instead—reflecting the neo-Confucian revival agenda of early sixteenth-century local administration.

The gazetteer preserves crucial evidence for the pre-wokou commercial development of the Zhangzhou coast, particularly the earliest documentary reference to Yuegang (月港) as a commercial center: "In the county's southeast, fifty li distant, commonly called Yuegang, named for the moon-shaped creek. Dense population, merchants gather like spokes on a wheel, ocean vessels and shallow boats all collect here—it is a great market town of southern Zhangzhou" (入煙繁盛，商賈輻輳，海艘淺舶皆集于此，為漳南一大市鎮). This passage documents Yuegang's commercial prominence before its formal opening to foreign trade in 隆慶元年 (1567), demonstrating that the port's rise resulted from organic commercial development rather than simply state policy. The gazetteer also records the rededication of landscape features to Confucian scholarly achievement, such as Tenglong Mountain and Qi Mountain renamed during Chenghua era school reconstruction to reflect aspiration toward examination success. The documentation of Wenshan (文山), a substantial settlement of four to five hundred households with an academy and notable Ru families including jinshi degree-winners from the Lin lineage, illustrates the pre-Jiajing distribution of education and elite status in the Zhangzhou region. These passages provide essential baseline documentation for understanding how the Jiajing-Wanli crises and commercial expansion transformed this prefecture.
