# Pang Shangpeng in the Ming Shilu

## *Ming Shenzong Shilu*: Records of Pang Shangpeng's Fujian Governorship

*Ming Shenzong Shilu* [Veritable Records of Emperor Shenzong of Ming], juan 65–72, Wanli 5 (1577) (Beijing: Zhongyang Yanjiuyuan Lishi Yuyan Yanjiusuo, 1962).

The Veritable Records for the Wanli reign contain crucial documentation of Pang Shangpeng's tenure as Fujian Governor (1576–1578), providing the primary source basis for understanding Single Whip implementation in Minnan. The records open with Pang's August 1577 memorial requesting remission of over 226,000 taels in accumulated military expenses from the Longqing period, justified on grounds that "the region had become somewhat peaceful and military supplies were adequate" (*difang shao ning, bing shi po zu*). This initial entry establishes the fiscal context—military debt relief preceding structural tax reform—that characterized Pang's approach.

The most significant entry covers Pang's leap eighth month memorial on currency reform, reproducing fourteen detailed regulatory measures for establishing provincial coinage mints. These regulations reveal practical adaptation to Fujian's commercial realities: converting abandoned military camps into minting facilities, establishing cost structures (0.98 taels per 1,000 coins), creating public-private exchange partnerships with reputable shopkeepers, and permitting silver-coin bimetallism for civilian transactions while mandating official coinage for tax payments. The records show central Ministry approval (*bu fu yunxing*), demonstrating Zhang Juzheng's support for Pang's monetary experiments despite their ultimate failure.

Administrative conflicts emerge in ninth month entries, including Pang's impeachment of Zongbing Hu Shouren for corruption and attempted reassignment of the military command from Fuzhou to Funing—a move blocked by the Ministry of War's insistence that "military officials have their own kind of履历" requiring different standards than civil officials. The records conclude with Pang's Wanli 5 (1577) self-criticism and resignation request (*zi chen buzhi, qi ba*), denied by the throne, foreshadowing his 1578 dismissal for supporting duoqing controversy critics. These documents are essential for understanding how provincial reform operated within central-local administrative tensions and why Pang's Fujian reforms proved more durable than his earlier Zhejiang innovations.
