# Yang Peina 楊培娜

**Citation:** Yang Peina 楊培娜. *Shengji yu Zhidu: Ming-Qing Min-Yue Yanhai Yumin de Huji Guanli yu Haishang Huodong 生计与制度：明清闽粤沿海渔民的户籍管理与海上活动* [Livelihood and Institutions: Household Registration Management and Maritime Activities of Coastal Fishermen in Ming-Qing Fujian and Guangdong]. Beijing: Shehui kexue wenxian chubanshe, 2019.

**Summary:**

Yang Peina's comprehensive study examines the intersection of state registration systems, maritime livelihoods, and local social organization in the coastal borderland between Chaozhou and Zhangzhou during the Ming and Qing periods. Drawing on both the economic history tradition of Fu Yiling and Liang Fangzhong and the social history approaches of Zheng Zhenman and Chen Chunsheng, Yang demonstrates how the weisuo (military garrison) system served as the foundational node for controlling coastal populations, extracting salt and fishing levies, and managing the complex household registration status of maritime households. The work traces the transformation of weisuo from purely military installations into hybrid administrative units that managed civilian populations, enrolled students through wei schools (*weixue*), and competed with county (*zhouxian*) administrations for control over surplus households and tax revenues.

The study is essential for understanding the fiscal-military context of 16th-century Minnan. Yang documents how early Ming efforts to register and control "Dan" boat households through fishing tax stations (*hebosuo*) largely collapsed by the Jiajing reign, with fishing populations shifting into weisuo or lijia registration. She analyzes the silverization of salt taxes in Fujian (completed by 1508) and the subsequent enclosure of coastal resources by powerful elites who claimed tidal flats as private property, a process accelerated by the state's need for military funding. Yang's analysis of the jurisdictional conflicts between Guangdong and Fujian military authorities over strategies of "pacification" (*fu*) versus "extermination" (*jiao*) during the Lin Feng campaigns provides crucial context for understanding Liu Yaohui's administrative challenges. The work establishes that the mid-Ming period (Chenghua through Jiajing reigns) was when the fundamental patterns of coastal commercialization, resource enclosure, and state-local elite cooperation that characterized the late Ming were consolidated.
