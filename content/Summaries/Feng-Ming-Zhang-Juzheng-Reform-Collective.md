Feng Ming 馮明, "Zhang Juzheng Gaige Qunti Goucheng" 張居正改革群體構成 (The Composition of Zhang Juzheng's Reform Collective), unpublished research notes.

This analytical study examines the personal and political networks that sustained Zhang Juzheng's reform administration (1572-1582), challenging both Zhang's own self-presentation as a solitary reformer and contemporary accusations that he built a self-serving faction. Feng distinguishes between Zhang's personal "party" (*dang*) and the broader "reform collective" (*gaige qunti*)—overlapping but distinct networks of officials who shared reform objectives even when their personal relationships with Zhang varied. The work traces the formation of these networks through key events including Zhang's support for Yin Zhengmao's campaigns in Guangxi (1569), the Altan Khan peace negotiations (1570), and the promotion of Tan Lun and Qi Jiguang to northern frontier commands.

The analysis reveals the crucial role of Fujian officials within the reform collective, noting the appointments of Chen Rui and Chen Sheng from Changle, Fujian as Huguang Provincial Governors—a pattern suggesting that Zhang's network-building extended to incorporating capable administrators from the southeastern coast. The work documents the consolidation of Zhang's power during the "coup" of Wanli 1 (1573), when alliances with Empress Li, Feng Bao, and key officials including Wang Guoguang, Tan Lun, and Wang Zhigao established the institutional foundation for reform implementation. Feng's analysis of the posthumous "reckoning" (*qingsuan*) period (1582-1587), during which 28 high officials were removed from office, demonstrates both the fragility of reform networks and the lasting impact of the institutional changes they implemented. For historians of Minnan's place within late Ming reform movements, this study illuminates how provincial officials from the southeast coast—including those who served in Fujian like Liu Yaohui and Geng Dingxiang—connected to broader reform networks, and how the military-fiscal crises of the Jiajing period created both the necessity and the personnel for the transformative reforms of the Wanli era.
