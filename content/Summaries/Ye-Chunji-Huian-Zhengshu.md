Ye Chunji 葉春及, *Hui'an Zhengshu* 惠安政書 (Administrative Records of Hui'an)

Ye Chunji (葉春及), a native of Huizhou in Guangdong, compiled this comprehensive administrative manual during his tenure as magistrate of Hui'an County (circa 1570s), synthesizing his reformist governance philosophy with detailed local data. The work represents a practical application of the "reformist trilogy" (鄉約, 保甲, 社學) advocated by contemporary thinkers like Geng Dingxiang, combining moral community reconstruction with fiscal and administrative reform. Ye's methodology centered on empirical investigation: upon arrival in Hui'an, he convened thirty reputable local informants for a banquet interview to gather basic facts, which became the foundation for his *Tugaowen* (圖告問). From his taxation and accounting duties emerged the *Banji Kao* (版籍考), documenting systematic efforts to address the severe undercounting of population—he estimated actual numbers were understated by at least two-thirds due to excessive levies associated with registration.

The *Hui'an Zhengshu* critically engages with structural problems of the Ming fiscal-military system, including private land transactions enabling hoarding and inequality, garrison systems designed for desert frontiers that left soldiers starving on fertile coastal lands, and insufficient salt production. Ye's fiscal tables (*Fushui Biao* 賦稅表) document the long-standing practice of silver commutation for taxes in Fujian (銀稅輪輸者易以粟, 自開之故), while complaining that maritime tax, farmland, and wastage registers had not been updated in forty years. The work includes innovative institutional proposals: the *Xiangyue Pian* (鄉約篇) for self-governance across the county's 41 *du* (都), *Lishe Pian* (里社篇) for regulating local religious customs, and *Baojia Pian* (保甲篇) addressing concerns that *bao* members themselves were turning to crime. The preface concludes with a defense of reforms as following Hongwu's original intent, comparing administrative restoration to retuning an old instrument, thereby positioning contemporary reforms within a framework of returning to foundational Ming principles while adapting to changed circumstances.
