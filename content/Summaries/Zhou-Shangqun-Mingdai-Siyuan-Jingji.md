Zhou Shangqun 周尚群, "Mingdai Siyuan Jingji" 明代寺院經濟 (Ming Dynasty Temple Economies), unpublished research notes.

This brief but significant analysis documents the dramatic decline in Buddhist and Daoist temple land ownership from the Song to Ming dynasties, with particular attention to Fujian's Zhangzhou region. The work synthesizes local gazetteer data and secondary scholarship to demonstrate that while temples owned substantial portions of regional landholdings during the Song dynasty—estimated at 17.2% of agricultural land in Fuzhou and perceived as holding up to 70% in Zhangzhou according to contemporary observer Chen Chun—their economic position collapsed dramatically by the Ming period. By the Wanli era, temples held only 4.23% of land in Changtai county, 1.86% in Haicheng, and 15.13% in Longxi county.

The analysis illuminates several crucial dimensions of Minnan's social and economic transformation. First, the data reveals the different trajectories of established metropolitan counties (like Longxi) versus newly created maritime counties (like Haicheng), where large-scale land reclamation from the sea occurred during the Ming period without temple involvement. Second, the work documents the shift in land management institutions from religious organizations to lineages—temples no longer organized the irrigation works, dike construction, and land reclamation projects that they had dominated during the Song. This transformation reflects broader patterns of social organization in Minnan, where lineage consolidation replaced religious institutions as the primary framework for property management and local governance. For historians of Minnan's fiscal and social history, this analysis provides quantitative evidence for understanding how the region's distinctive lineage-based social structure emerged through the displacement of alternative institutional forms, with significant implications for tax collection, land tenure, and the organization of commercial agriculture.
