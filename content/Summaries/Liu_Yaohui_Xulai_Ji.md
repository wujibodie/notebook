# Liu Yaohui 劉堯誨 (1522-1585)

**Citation:** Liu Yaohui 劉堯誨. *Xulai Ji 虛籁集* [Collection of Hollow Sounds]. Original edition includes collected personal writings; memorials (*zoushu*) discovered separately in descendant holdings and now available through digital archives. First memorial edition printed 1757 (Qianlong 22) with colophon describing manuscript discovery in Taihe, Jiangxi.

**Summary:**

The *Xulai Ji* preserves the writings of Liu Yaohui during his tenure as Fujian Provincial Governor (*xunfu*, 1572-1576), a critical period for Ming coastal defense and fiscal reform. The collection includes both personal literary writings and, more importantly for administrative history, his memorials to the throne which document his efforts to address the fiscal-military crisis precipitated by pirate fleets operating from Taiwan and the Guangdong-Fujian borderlands. Liu's memorials reveal the practical challenges of funding coastal defense when regular tax revenues proved insufficient: his detailed accounting shows how he cobbled together emergency military budgets from salt commutation silver, borrowed frontier funds, and temporary surcharges while seeking to avoid additional burdens on an exhausted populace. His writings document specific initiatives including the establishment of maritime customs at Haicheng, the coordination of Fujian-Guangdong joint operations against Lin Feng's pirate fleet, and proposals to permanently garrison Nan'ao Island as a forward defense position.

Beyond administrative specifics, the *Xulai Ji* illuminates the political dimensions of late Ming provincial governance. Liu's letters to Zhang Juzheng (which appear in both Liu's collection and Zhang's collected works) reveal a carefully cultivated patronage relationship, with Liu articulating his mission as simultaneously pacifying local officials (*neike*) and preparing military defenses (*waiyu*). His memorials on the Lin Feng campaign expose tensions with Guangdong authorities over jurisdictional boundaries, conflicting strategies (pacification vs. extermination), and competing claims for credit. Liu's later reflections, written after his dismissal on corruption charges, include recommendations for subordinates like Hu Shouren and reveal his continuing concern with coastal defense even in retirement. The collection thus provides essential documentation for understanding how Zhang Juzheng's reform network operated at the provincial level, the practical challenges of implementing military-fiscal reforms in a commercially dynamic but agriculturally deficient region, and the personal costs of service in the Ming bureaucratic system.
