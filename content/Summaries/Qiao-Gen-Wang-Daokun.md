Qiao Gen. *Wang Daokun Wenxue Yanjiu* [汪道昆文學研究]. Beijing: Unspecified publisher, n.d.

Qiao Gen's study of Wang Daokun (汪道昆, 1525-1593) represents one of the pioneering scholarly efforts to examine this important Ming literary figure beyond his reputation as a playwright or source for Huizhou merchant history. The work addresses several persistent weaknesses in Wang Daokun scholarship: the underutilization of literary materials from his collected works (*Taihanji*), insufficient attention compared to contemporaries like Li Panlong and Wang Shizhen, and the lack of close reading of his actual writings in favor of mining them for broader network data. Qiao proposes a more "objective" critical appraisal that moves beyond simply repeating the criticisms of Wang Shizhen and Qian Qianyi. Methodologically, the study employs *xi du* (細讀, close reading), *zhi ren lun shi* (知人論世, understanding the person to discuss their times), and Chen Yinke's concept of "understanding sympathy" (了解之同情).

The study categorizes Wang's extensive poetic corpus—comprising some 1,700 poems—into four types: gift/reply poems, military life poems, lyrical/ekphrastic poems, and remembrance poems. Unlike the "Later Seven Masters" (*Hou Qizi*), Wang showed greater influence from Tang poetry, particularly Du Fu. Qiao connects Wang's emphasis on "practical utility" (*shiyong*) to his merchant family background, arguing that his commercial origins shaped his literary sensibilities and thematic concerns. This connection between mercantile origins and literary production offers a valuable framework for understanding the relationship between Fujian's commercial economy and its cultural output during the late Ming period. For research on Minnan's 16th-century history, this study illuminates the cultural world of a key Fujian-connected literary figure who maintained relationships with military reformers like Qi Jiguang and political figures like Zhang Juzheng, even if his actual political role requires further examination.
