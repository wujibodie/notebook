Li Zhijun 李之琇. *Fengxia zhi Hai* 風下之海 [The Sea Under the Wind]. Beijing: Unspecified publisher, n.d.

Li Zhijun's *Fengxia zhi Hai* (The Sea Under the Wind) proposes a sophisticated conceptual framework for understanding Minnan's (southern Fujian) unique position in Chinese history through the dual processes of "interiorization" (*neixianghua* 內向化) and "exteriorization" (*waixianghua* 外向化). Interiorization refers to the conscious tendency toward "de-barbarization" (*tuo man yishi* 脫蠻意識) and assimilation to Central Plains (*Zhongyuan*) cultural norms, while exteriorization describes the shift of economic gravity beyond the boundaries of the Chinese state toward maritime networks. This framework proves particularly valuable for understanding how a region that was culturally "interiorizing" toward Confucian orthodoxy simultaneously became economically "exteriorized" toward Southeast Asian and global trade networks during the Ming Dynasty.

The work places particular emphasis on environmental factors, including the impact of the Little Ice Age on the subtropical monsoon region. Li notes that the boundary of viable tropical agriculture—marked by the limits of annual frost and lychee cultivation—shifted approximately two degrees of latitude southward during this period, with snow reported in Fuzhou during many years. The study also documents typhoon patterns and their relationship to El Niño cycles, as well as the sophisticated tidal knowledge reflected in Minnan local gazetteers. Geographically, the study focuses on the Jiulong River delta ports of Putou, Shima, Haicheng, and Xiamen, situating them within the broader Taiwan Strait region defined by currents, winds, and cloud patterns squeezed between the Wuyi and Taiwan mountain ranges. For 16th-century Minnan studies, Li's environmental and geographic framework provides essential context for understanding the agricultural deficits that drove commercialization, the maritime expertise that enabled trade, and the climatic fluctuations that shaped settlement patterns during the critical Jiajing-Wanli period.
