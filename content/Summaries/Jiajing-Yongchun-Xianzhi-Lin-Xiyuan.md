Lin Xiyuan 林希元 (compiler), 《嘉靖永春縣志》(Jiajing Yongchun County Gazetteer)

Lin Xiyuan (林希元), a prominent Tong'an scholar-official, compiled this county gazetteer during his tenure as Yongchun County magistrate in the mid-Jiajing period (dated to approximately Jiajing 5-6, 1526-1527, based on the degree-winner table). Lin's preface articulates an activist vision of local governance: "The Master said: When a county lacks a gazetteer, the magistrate fails his duty" (子曰：邑無志，宰弗職也), and declares that "a gazetteer is the tool of government; compiling a gazetteer establishes administration" (夫志，政之具也。脩志所以立政). Lin emphasizes the military-strategic importance of geographic knowledge for governing Yongchun, a mountainous region where "despite removal to narrow mountain passes, there are many bandits and thieves—though perhaps not produced here, the terrain provides what bandits rely on to block its territory" (永春疆除於萬山之隘，多寇賊。雖或非其產，險故盜賊所憑阻其疆也). His personal experience battling bandits five times in three years convinced him that study of geography and topography was vital for officials serving there, advocating that gentlemen must begin with literary cultivation and progress to administrative governance in order to master worldly affairs.

The *Banji Zhi* (Household and Land Records) provides crucial demographic and fiscal documentation for this mountainous interior county. Population records trace a catastrophic decline from Hongwu 24 (1391) figures of 4,041 households and 21,017 persons to Yongle 10 (1412) figures of merely 2,093 households and 6,670 persons—a loss of two-thirds in thirty years that Lin accepts as evidence of real decline rather than registration decay. Land registration similarly plummeted from 2,437 qing in 1391 to 1,631 qing in 1412, then remained virtually constant thereafter, which Lin attributed to land abandonment following population decline. The gazetteer records the court's mistaken perception of Yongchun as having empty land suitable for military colonies (*tuntian*), which "only caused strife and lawsuits with the locals"—an admission that vacant land statistics reflected registration failure rather than actual availability. Lin's commentary on fiscal policy reflects sophisticated engagement with Song dynasty debates between Wang Anshi and Sima Guang regarding monetary taxation versus labor service, advocating "neither exclusively labor service nor exclusively hired substitution, but appropriate use according to circumstance" (朝役法不專於差，不專於僱，因而用之存乎，變利而應之). He concludes with a comparative observation on regional variation: labor service in the Northwest derived from adult males, while the Southeast considered both males and land; in the Northwest, more land meant heavier burden, so service should be based on persons rather than land, while in the Southeast land indicated wealth distinctions, requiring consideration of both persons and land.
