# Chen Maoren 陳懋仁 (late 16th-early 17th century)

**Citation:** Chen Maoren 陳懋仁. *Quannan Zazhi 泉南雜誌* [Miscellaneous Records of Southern Quanzhou]. Original preface dated to author's service as Quanzhou Prefectural Manager (*jingli*) during Wanli reign. Modern editions available in various Ming-Qing biji congshu and digital archives.

**Summary:**

Chen Maoren's *Quannan Zazhi* offers a rare firsthand administrative perspective on late Ming Quanzhou, written by a low-ranking official (*jingli* or prefectural manager) from Jiaxing who served in the region during the late Wanli period. Unlike the more elevated perspectives of provincial governors or senior officials, Chen's miscellany records the practical challenges of local governance, the material realities of Minnan's maritime economy, and the author's own struggles with tax evasion, land disputes, and corrupt practices. The work documents the continued vibrancy of Quanzhou's commercial economy decades after Liu Yaohui's reforms: detailed descriptions of the Dehua porcelain industry (noting that early high prices had declined due to expanded production), the importation of rice from Zhejiang and Zhili to compensate for land converted to sugarcane cultivation, and the extensive trade in exotic maritime products including edible bird's nests imported from Southeast Asian traders. Chen's observations on the density of population and scarcity of land—where even five to ten *qing* constituted a substantial holding—confirm the structural agricultural deficit that had driven Minnan's commercial orientation since the Song dynasty.

Beyond economic documentation, the *Quannan Zazhi* illuminates the administrative challenges of governing a commercially dynamic region with inadequate fiscal resources. Chen records his discovery of granary shortfalls and unreported silver sales during an audit of Dehua county, his frustration with the practice of converting tax obligations to silver commutation which enabled embezzlement, and his handling of a land dispute on Egret Islet (*Lusi Zhou*) where powerful families had encroached on school lands. His description of counterfeit silver (*huoxi*) circulating in Quanzhou city and his methods for distinguishing it from genuine sycee provide material evidence for the monetary problems that plagued late Ming commerce. The work's inclusion of poems attributed to Ouyang Zhan (Fujian's first recorded jinshi) and its discussion of the Penghu islands' depopulation during the wokou troubles demonstrate how local officials used historical and geographical knowledge to contextualize contemporary administrative challenges. As a record of quotidian governance by a middling official without imperial commission, the *Quannan Zazhi* complements the grand strategic perspectives of Liu Yaohui's memorials, revealing how the fiscal-military reforms of the 1570s were implemented, adapted, and sometimes frustrated at the local level decades later.
