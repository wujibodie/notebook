# Tang Kaijian 唐開建

**Citation:** Tang Kaijian 唐開建. Manuscript study based on *Dufu Shouyi 督撫疏議* [Memorials of Governors and Grand Coordinators], 15 juan, held at Nanjing Library. Article focuses on nine memorials concerning Lin Feng's pirate campaigns.

**Summary:**

Tang Kaijian's research on the manuscript *Dufu Shouyi* (held at Nanjing Library) provides crucial documentary evidence for reconstructing the Lin Feng campaigns of 1574-1575, supplementing gaps in the published *Ming Shilu* and private collections. The manuscript contains nine memorials specifically addressing Lin Feng: reports on pirate incursions, joint suppression operations, strategies for post-campaign pacification, maritime intelligence reports, victory memorials, and detailed accounts of Lin's flight to foreign territories (labeled "Lin Zei Tong Fan"). Tang's study confirms Lin Feng's origins as a subordinate of the notorious pirate Zeng Yiben, whose death in the early 1570s led to the fragmentation of his fleet among competing leaders including Lin Daogan, Zhu Liangbao, and Lin Feng himself. The memorials document the complex relationships among these pirate bands, which both cooperated and competed while maintaining fluid allegiances to Ming authorities when advantageous.

Tang's work is methodologically important for highlighting the limitations of Spanish sources (which provide most information about Lin Feng's early activities but remain unverifiable against Chinese documentation) and for demonstrating the value of archival manuscripts for late Ming military history. The *Dufu Shouyi* memorials illuminate the operational details of coastal defense that official chronicles elide: the difficulties of coordinating naval operations across the Fujian-Guangdong border, the challenges of distinguishing between "pacified" pirates who accepted official positions and those who continued raiding, and the role of local informants and fishermen in intelligence gathering. Tang notes that while most information about Lin Feng's origins comes from Spanish sources, the Chinese archival record provides essential context for understanding how Ming officials conceptualized and responded to the threat he posed. The memorials concerning Lin's flight to Luzon and his eventual defeat by combined Chinese-Spanish forces represent the most detailed official documentation of this early encounter between Ming China and Spanish colonial authorities in the Philippines.
