Pang Shangpeng 龐尚鵬, *Baiketing Zhaigao* 百可亭摘稿 (Selected Drafts from the Pavilion of the Hundred Acceptables), originally compiled during the Ming dynasty.

Pang Shangpeng (1524-1581) was a pivotal figure in Ming fiscal reform whose administrative career exemplified the transition from local experimentation to empire-wide policy implementation during the late 16th century. His collected works, organized into memorials (*zouyi*) and correspondence (*shujian*), document his implementation of the *Junping fa* (均平法, Equalization Method) in Zhejiang and Fujian—reforms that anticipated and influenced the broader Single Whip (*yitiaobian*) reforms associated with Zhang Juzheng's administration. Pang's writings provide crucial documentation of how fiscal reform operated at the provincial and county levels, translating abstract principles of "equalization" into concrete administrative procedures.

The correspondence section reveals the personal networks that sustained reform efforts, including extensive exchanges with Zhang Juzheng, Tan Lun, Geng Dingxiang, and other members of the reform collective. Pang's letters document the challenges of implementing new fiscal assessments in the context of the *wokou* crisis's aftermath, when military expenditures strained local resources and required innovative approaches to tax commutation and service obligations. His memorials address practical administrative problems including salt administration, military provisioning, and the coordination of provincial resources. For historians of Minnan's fiscal transformation, Pang's writings are particularly valuable for understanding how the "laboratory for fiscal reform" functioned: his Zhejiang and Fujian experiments demonstrated that land-based taxation could be adapted to commercially developed regions, establishing precedents that would be extended empire-wide during the Wanli reign. The collection thus bridges local administrative practice and central policy formation, showing how provincial officials like Pang translated the pressures of military crisis into institutional innovation.
