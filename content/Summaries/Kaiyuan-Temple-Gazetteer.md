Shi Yuanxian 釋元賢, ed. *Quanzhou Kaiyuan Temple Gazetteer* (Quanzhou Kaiyuan Zhi 泉州開元志). Chongzhen era (1628-1644), compiled 1643. Preface dated 1643 (崇祯癸未孟春灯节日). Modern edition referenced from Zhonghuadiancang.com.

This temple gazetteer (方志) for the Kaiyuan Temple in Quanzhou provides crucial primary source material for understanding religious institutions, land tenure, and social change in Minnan during the late Ming period. The compilation, undertaken by the monk Shi Yuanxian at the behest of local gentry in the final years of the Ming dynasty, documents the history of one of Fujian's most important Buddhist institutions from its Tang dynasty origins through the Chongzhen era. The text is organized into four sections: construction history (建置), eminent monks (开士), collected writings (艺文), and land endowments/taxation (田赋). The latter section is particularly valuable for fiscal history, as it details the extensive landholdings accumulated by the temple over centuries and their subsequent confiscation or alienation during the Ming.

The gazetteer records the temple's dramatic decline from a peak of 120 subsidiary halls during the Song dynasty to its severely diminished state by the late Ming, when "Buddhist halls and monk's quarters were largely occupied by commoners" (佛宇僧舍强半民居) and even the ordination platform was occupied by gunpowder craftsmen for nearly thirty years. The text documents how the temple's recovery in the Wanli period (particularly 1594/甲午) depended on the intervention of wealthy lay donors (檀越) such as Vice Surveillance Commissioner Huang Wenbing, who used his authority to expel illegal occupants and restore temple property. This pattern illustrates broader themes of gentry-temple relations, the commodification of religious property, and the challenges facing Buddhist institutions in a period of fiscal pressure and commercial expansion. The editor's lament about the decline of Chan Buddhism and the dispersal of talented monks (高明之衲，云散四方) reflects wider concerns about religious institutional decline in late Ming Fujian.
