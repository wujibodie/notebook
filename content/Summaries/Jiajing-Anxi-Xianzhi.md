《嘉靖安溪縣志》(Jiajing Anxi County Gazetteer)

The Jiajing era Anxi County Gazetteer contains exceptionally detailed land registration records spanning 161 years from Hongwu 24 (1391) to Jiajing 31 (1552), providing one of the most comprehensive longitudinal datasets for examining Ming fiscal administration stability and change at the county level. The *Tutian* (Lands) section opens with a historical survey connecting land tenure to ancient precedents, noting that the *Tribute of Yu* (*Yugong* 禹貢) established taxes based on land quality, and that from Han through Tang dynasties, officials regarded land as fundamental to the state and worthy of careful attention. The Anxi data reveals remarkable stability in total registered land over a century and a half: from 1,393.47 qing in 1391 to approximately 1,401.05 qing in 1552—a net change of merely 7.58 qing (+0.5%). Government land remained virtually unchanged at 166.52-166.60 qing throughout, while private land gradually increased from 1,226.94 to approximately 1,234 qing.

The gazetteer's categorization system illuminates Ming land tenure classifications. Government land (*guantian* 官田) included several subcategories: long-standing government land (官), salary land for officials (職), confiscated land from criminals (沒官), former temple lands (廢寺), and school lands for prefectural and county academies (府學, 縣學). By Jiajing 1 (1522), former temple lands comprised 88.9% of government fields (134.32 qing), while school lands represented significant holdings (13.02 qing prefectural, 58 qing county). The data shows a notable jump in private land registration in Zhengde 7 (1512, +3.27 qing), suggesting possible survey or administrative changes during that period. The extreme stability of these figures—changing by just tens of *mu* across most decades—raises important questions about whether this reflects actual land use stability, consistent under-registration, or administrative inertia in updating records. This dataset provides crucial comparative evidence for understanding how coastal commercial counties like Jinjiang (which registered dramatic increases during Wanli surveys) differed from mountainous interior counties like Anxi in their fiscal administrative evolution.
