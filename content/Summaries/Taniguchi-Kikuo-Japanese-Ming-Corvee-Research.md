Taniguchi Kikuo 谷口規矩雄. "Nihon no Mindai Yōeki Kenkyū" [Japanese Research on Ming Corvée]. In *Nihon Gakusha* [Japanese Scholars on Ming-Qing Fiscal and Labor Service Institutions], edited by [collective volume], 1992.

Taniguchi Kikuo's literature review synthesizes pre- and post-war Japanese scholarship on Ming corvée systems, focusing on how the commutation of labor services affected social status distinctions within local communities. His central research question probes the stability of privileges conferred by liangzhang (grain tax captain), li, and jiazhang (household group head) status, asking how these positions changed as their responsibilities shifted, and whether lijia status could compare to lineage- and education-based status hierarchies in local importance. Taniguchi documents how the miscellaneous service (*zayi*) category varied considerably by region but was generally wealth-based, levied on estimates of adult males and land. The junyao reform theoretically aimed to make these assessments more consistent, with its primary effect being the establishment of fixed terms and amounts for *zayi*—levied decennially, five years apart from the regular lijia corvée (*zhengyi*). Unlike the regular corvée, land served as the main metric for junyao levies.

For research on sixteenth-century Minnan fiscal reform, Taniguchi's synthesis provides essential historiographical context for understanding the institutional frameworks Fujian officials manipulated. His summary of Yamane Yukio's seminal 1966 work (*Jūgo, Jūroku Seiki Chūgoku*)—which established that junyao initially was not silver-based despite what the *Ming Shi* claims—clarifies that Hongzhi and Zhengde period reforms commuted these obligations to silver primarily to lessen the *zayi* burden. Taniguchi's documentation of how in North China silver was collected based on household rank in a nine-step system as part of junyao levies from at least the Chenghua reign demonstrates the regional variation that Fujian reformers had to navigate. His analysis of the Ten-Section Method as a response to perceived inequalities opened by silverization of junyao—which imposed payments based on potentially outdated census records—illuminates why Fujian officials developed increasingly sophisticated surveying techniques. Taniguchi's review of Iwai Shigeki's work on Jiangxi's Single Whip implementation by Xunfu Liu Guangji, and his observation that "Jiangxi's situation was somewhat different—reforms were implemented after understanding central government intentions," provides crucial comparative perspective for evaluating how Liu Yaohui's Fujian reforms balanced local adaptation with central compliance.
