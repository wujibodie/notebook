Geng Dingxiang 耿定向, *Geng Dingxiang Ji* 耿定向集 (Collected Works of Geng Dingxiang), originally compiled during the Ming dynasty.

Geng Dingxiang (1524-1596) was a prominent Neo-Confucian scholar-official who served as Provincial Education Commissioner in Fujian and later as Grand Secretary during the Wanli reign. His collected works provide crucial insight into the intellectual and administrative world of late Ming Fujian, particularly regarding the intersection of moral philosophy and governance. The collection includes personal letters, prefaces to examination records, and philosophical reflections that illuminate how Ming officials understood their roles as moral exemplars and administrators.

Particularly significant for understanding Minnan history are Geng's observations on regional character and his correspondence with key reformers including Zhang Juzheng and Liu Yaohui. His preface to the Fujian Provincial Examination Record (*Fujian Xiangshi Lu Xu*) engages directly with the characterization of Fujian as "Zoulu by the sea" (海濱鄒魯), tracing the region's cultural legitimacy through its transmission of Zhu Xi's learning while acknowledging its distinctive maritime character. Geng's letters reveal the personal networks that sustained reform movements, including his advisory correspondence with Zhang Juzheng regarding moral cultivation and governance. His documented dismissal of a Fujian county magistrate for purchasing a "beautiful boy" in the 1580s (noted in Timothy Brook's *Confusions of Pleasure*) illustrates the intersection of moral enforcement and administrative discipline in provincial governance. The collection thus offers valuable perspective on how Neo-Confucian moral discourse shaped administrative practice in 16th-century Fujian, complementing the fiscal and military records found in other primary sources.
