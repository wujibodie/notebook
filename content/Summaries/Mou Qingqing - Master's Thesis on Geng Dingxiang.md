
## English Summary

This Master's thesis, "A Study on the Thought of Governing the World and Practice of the Ming Dynasty Confucian Scholar Geng Dingxiang," examines Geng Dingxiang (1524-1596) as a pivotal figure in the mid-to-late Ming Dynasty who exemplified the practical dimension of Wang Yangming's school of thought. The study argues that Geng Dingxiang's career demonstrates a robust commitment to statecraft (*jingshi*), challenging the traditional view that Wang Yangming's philosophy was solely focused on abstract inner cultivation. Geng's academic tenets, such as "uncontrollable truth" (*zhenji buyi*), "mediocre words and deeds" (*yongyan yongxing*), and the "benevolence of the integration of all things" (*wanwu yiti zhi ren*), provided the theoretical bedrock for his pragmatic approach to governance.

The thesis traces Geng Dingxiang's political and administrative career, highlighting his efforts to improve academic and social atmospheres in Nanjing through lectures and educational reforms. Critically, it details his tenure as Fujian Provincial Governor (*Xunfu*), where he supported Zhang Juzheng's reforms by implementing land surveys, the Single Whip Law, strengthening coastal defenses against pirates, and establishing the *Baojia* system for local order and disaster relief. During this period, he compiled *Muyyaobian* (Essentials of Being an Official) and *Mushi Moye* (Final Discussion on Official Duties and Responsibilities) to offer systematic governance guidance, emphasizing moral cultivation and practical administrative skills for local officials. Furthermore, the study explores Geng's significant contributions to his hometown of Huang'an, including facilitating its establishment as a county, compiling the *Huang'an Chusheng* (Huang'an Chronicles), and promoting village compacts, *Baojia*, and educational lectures, thereby embodying the Confucian ideal of social transformation through practical action.

## Novel Claims, Theories, or Methodologies

*   **Re-evaluation of the Yangming School's *Jingshi* Dimension:** The thesis's primary novel claim is to demonstrate that the Wang Yangming school, particularly in the 16th century, possessed a strong and often overlooked *jingshi* (statecraft) dimension. It argues against the common perception that this school was solely concerned with abstract mind-nature theories.
*   **Geng Dingxiang as a Model of Practical Mind-Learning:** It positions Geng Dingxiang as a representative figure whose life and work illustrate how mind-learning served as both a theoretical foundation and a practical guide for active engagement in governance. Conversely, Geng's practical statecraft provided a crucial arena for verifying and refining his philosophical understanding.
*   **Integrated Analysis of Thought and Practice:** The methodology involves an integrated analysis of Geng Dingxiang's philosophical thought and his concrete administrative and local governance activities, showing a consistent underlying principle of pragmatic effectiveness that guided his actions across different contexts.
*   **Focus on Local Governance and Social Impact:** The study highlights Geng Dingxiang's significant, yet often underappreciated, contributions to local administration and social reform, such as county establishment, gazetteer compilation, and the implementation of community self-governance systems (*xiangyue* and *baojia*).

## Key Sources/Schools of Thought

*   **Schools of Thought:**
    *   **Yangming School (阳明学):** Geng Dingxiang is presented as a leading scholar of this school, drawing upon its core concepts like "integration of all things" (万物一体) and "uncontrollable truth" (*zhenji buyi*).
    *   **Confucian Statecraft (*Jingshi* 经世):** The central theme, exploring the practical application of Confucian ideals to governance and societal well-being.
*   **Key Figures:**
    *   **Geng Dingxiang (耿定向):** The central subject, whose philosophical development and practical actions are thoroughly examined.
    *   **Wang Yangming (王阳明):** The founder of the school that influenced Geng.
    *   **Zhang Juzheng (张居正):** A prominent Grand Secretary whose reforms Geng supported as Fujian Provincial Governor, though he later critiqued Zhang's methods.
    *   **Li Zhi (李贽):** Geng's intellectual contemporary, with whom he engaged in significant philosophical debates.
    *   **Xu Jie (徐阶) and Gao Gong (高拱):** Other influential Grand Secretaries whose political dynamics intersected with Geng's career.
*   **Geng Dingxiang's Own Works:**
    *   *Shuofubaojian* (硕辅宝鉴): A historical compilation intended to guide prime ministers.
    *   *Xianjin Yifeng* (先进遗风): A collection of exemplary conduct for officials.
    *   *Muyyaobian* (牧要编) and *Mushi Moye* (牧事末议): Treatises offering practical administrative guidance for local officials.
    *   *Huang'an Chusheng* (黄安初乘): The first local gazetteer of Huang'an County, compiled by Geng.
    *   *Guansheng Ji* (观生纪): Geng's autobiography, providing personal insights into his life and intellectual journey.
*   **Historical Records and Scholarship:**
    *   *Mingru Xue'an* (明儒学案) by Huang Zongxi: Used as a critical reference point for Geng's intellectual lineage.
    *   *Ming Shi* (明史) and *Siku Quanshu* (四库全书): Standard dynastic histories and encyclopedic collections.
    *   Local gazetteers (地方志): Essential for reconstructing Geng's local governance activities and their impact.
    *   Contemporary academic works on Ming history, Confucian thought, and *jingshi* studies.
