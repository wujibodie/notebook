Brook, Timothy. *The Chinese State in Ming Society*. London: RoutledgeCurzon, 2005.

Timothy Brook's seminal work examines the multifaceted interactions between the Ming state and local society, providing essential theoretical and empirical grounding for understanding 16th-century Minnan's fiscal transformation. Brook demonstrates that the township (*du* 都) rather than the village served as "the principal unit by which a county magistrate organized the administration of taxes" (p. 29), with registration numbers assigned to land plots organized by township in non-repeating series. This structural arrangement had profound implications for land transactions: purchasing land outside one's home township required registering as a dependent under the vendor's household, creating ambiguous title that was "constantly flouted" (p. 30)—a practice that contributed to the "frozen registers" problem central to understanding Ming fiscal crisis.

Chapter 2's detailed study of Ye Chunji (葉春及), magistrate of Huian County (惠安縣) in Quanzhou Prefecture from 1571, offers a crucial case study for Fujian's Single Whip reform implementation. Brook shows how Ye's attempt to survey taxable land through mapping was actively resisted by local gentry who submitted maps that "traced what was already in the land registers, not what actually lay on the landscape" (p. 187). Despite Ye's innovations—including mobilizing local elders to create 155 village-level maps and 27 township maps—his methods "did not circulate widely" and the "accuracy he strove for... was not continued beyond that time" (p. 59). Chapter 7's analysis of Buddhist institutions documents the 1391 "hundred-day edict" that closed smaller monasteries and transferred their property to larger institutions, and notes Fujian-specific interventions in the 1570s-1580s when "the state needed emergency funds to meet military costs" (pp. 148-149). Brook's conclusion that Single Whip reforms "grew up out of local state practice rather than descending from the court" (p. 187) provides crucial framing for understanding Fujian's fiscal exceptionalism.
