Xu Xiaowang 徐曉望. "On the Development of Port Cities Around Xiamen Bay during the Ming Dynasty" [論明代廈門灣周邊港市的發展].

This specialized study examines the evolution of maritime port cities around Xiamen Bay during the Ming dynasty, providing crucial evidence for understanding the commercial infrastructure that supported Minnan's integration into global trade networks. Xu draws heavily on the Haicheng County gazetteer compiled by Zhang Xie and others, reprinted in the "Japan Collection of Rare Chinese Local Gazetteers" (日本藏中国罕见方志丛刊), as well as the Zhangping County gazetteer, which records how "those coming from the southeast rivers and streams via Yuegang through Huakou and various passes to reach Jianning and Yanping—all are smugglers seeking profits, creating disturbances." The study documents how Haicheng County's strategic location made it a natural transshipment point for foreign goods entering Fujian's interior, despite official restrictions on maritime trade.

Xu's analysis incorporates key contemporary statements on the region's commercial importance. Li Ying's memorial requesting the establishment of Haicheng County observed that "of Fujian's eight prefectures, Zhangzhou is the most remote; of Zhangzhou's eight counties, Yuegang is the border corner. In terms of population density it is like bees and lice, while goods and treasures gather here—it is the trade center of southern Fujian." Zhou Qiyuan's famous description of the Longqing-era legalization of trade captures the transformation: "In our Muzong's reign, the laws against trading with foreigners were removed, so that merchants from all directions came in joy to the water country, dividing into eastern and western routes. The precious and exotic goods they carried need not be described, but the money they traded annually was certainly several hundred thousand. Both public and private relied on this—it was indeed the Son of Heaven's southern treasury." This quotation, drawn from Zhang Xie's *Dongxi Yangkao* (东西洋考), encapsulates the fiscal significance of maritime trade for the Ming state and local society, providing essential context for understanding how coastal Fujian's commercial prosperity created both opportunities and tensions within the Ming fiscal system.
