《嘉慶同安縣志》(Jiaqing Tong'an County Gazetteer)

The Jiaqing era (19th century) Tong'an County Gazetteer preserves valuable Ming-era institutional history regarding coastal defense and the evolution of Xiamen (廈門) as a military and commercial center. The gazetteer's maps include a depiction of the Wenmiao (Confucian Temple) with a "Lin Gong Ci" (林公祠) in pride of place just outside the main temple—presumably honoring Lin Xiyuan (林希元), the prominent Jiajing-era scholar-official and compiler of the Yongchun County Gazetteer. This placement alongside a temple to Su Song (蘇頌)—a Song dynasty scholar-official honored by Zhu Xi—reflects the elevation of locally-rooted intellectuals as models for educational achievement, representing an expression of "intense localism and intellectualism" among later Ming-Qing scholars who valued Lin's undistinguished official career, early retirement, and devotion to education as equally valuable alternatives to Su Song's empire-wide bureaucratic prominence.

The gazetteer's military geography section documents the relocation of coastal defense installations from the Ming to Qing periods, tracing the movement of the Wuyu Water Fortress (浯嶼水寨) from its original position on Dayu Island to Xiamen (廈門), and eventually to Liaoluo (料羅). These relocations reflected ongoing debates about coastal defense strategy: the original Wuyu position "held the peril between Dayu and Xiaoyu Islands, cut off maritime connections to Haemen and Yuegang, and coordinated with Fuzhou Fenghuo, Xiaocheng, Xinghua Nanri, and Zhangzhou Tongshan"—a comprehensive system for defending all Fujian. However, the editors document criticism that moving these installations inland represented "not defending the doorway but guarding the inner chamber, losing the strategic advantage" (不禦門戶而守堂奧，自失其險). The account traces the progressive withdrawal of coastal defenses through the late Ming wokou crisis and subsequent threats: the original Wuyu fortress, relocated to Xiamen by Wanli times, was eventually moved to Liaoluo with a seasonal garrison system that became increasingly nominal. The passage reveals the tension between strategic coastal positions and logistical sustainability, as well as the impact of successive maritime crises (wokou, Dutch, Zheng) on Fujian's coastal defense geography.
