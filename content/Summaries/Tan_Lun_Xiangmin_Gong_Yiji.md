# Tan Lun 譚綸 (1520-1577)

**Citation:** Tan Lun 譚綸. *Tan Xiangmin Gong Yiji 譚襄敏公遺集* [Collected Works of the Late Lord Tan Xiangmin]. Edited collection of memorials, correspondence, and military writings. Original Ming edition; modern references cite various reprints and manuscript holdings.

**Summary:**

The collected works of Tan Lun (posthumously titled *Tan Xiangmin Gong Yiji*) constitute a crucial primary source for understanding mid-16th century military administration and coastal defense policy during the Jiajing wokou crisis. As a military commissioner who rose from supervising coastal defense in Zhejiang to becoming Minister of War (bingbu shangshu), Tan's correspondence documents the practical challenges of coordinating anti-piracy campaigns across provincial boundaries, the implementation of "discipline and regulation" (*jiezhi*) military reforms, and the intricate patronage networks linking field commanders to central authorities. The collection contains extensive exchanges with key figures including Yu Dayou (whom Tan consistently championed as a "minister for the altars of state"), Qi Jiguang (with whom Tan developed close collaborative relationships), and memorials documenting their joint campaigns against coastal raiders and inland bandits in Zhejiang, Fujian, and Guangdong.

For the study of Minnan in the 1570s, Tan's writings are particularly valuable as they establish precedents and networks that Liu Yaohui would inherit and adapt. The correspondence reveals tensions between military professionals and civil officials, the challenges of coordinating Fujian-Guangdong operations against mobile pirate forces like Lin Feng's, and the importance of Zhang Juzheng's patronage in protecting military reformers from civilian bureaucratic opposition. Tan's memorials on the establishment of coastal garrisons, his advocacy for Yu Dayou's appointment to high command despite civilian opposition, and his detailed discussions of military finance and logistics provide essential context for understanding the fiscal-military challenges that Liu Yaohui would confront as Fujian governor. The collection also preserves Yu Dayou's memorial text for Tan, offering rare insight into how military commanders themselves conceptualized their shared mission.
