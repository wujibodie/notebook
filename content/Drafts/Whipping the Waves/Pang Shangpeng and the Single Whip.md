No doubt Pang implemented reforms that went under the moniker of "Single Whip" in Fujian. But what exactly was this "Single Whip"? Was it the same in Fujian as in other provinces? What problems were Pang's reforms intended to resolve, and to what extent did they really do so? What connection did the Fujian version of the Single Whip have to the development of the province's silver economy?
It will be my contention that, while Fujian's taxpayers may well have found the reforms implemented under Pang "convenient," it is unlikely that they were as satisfying to Zhang Juzheng. Judged from Beijing, Fujian's Single Whip failed to meaningfully increase revenues to the central treasury. Moreover, the impact of these reforms on the fiscal and private silverization of Fujian's economy was probably minimal, in part because that process was already so advanced (as we saw in previous chapters), but also because of the limited nature of these reforms themselves. In sum, the Single Whip of Pang and Zhang did not have (and could not have had) the sorts of effects in Fujian attributed to it in other provinces or for the empire as a whole. Indeed, the difficulties revealed by the attempts to increase stable revenues through Pang's reforms led directly to the next major round of centrally-directed reform, namely the comprehensive land resurvey tasked to Geng Dingxiang.

Comparative Military Manuals

Pang Shangpeng emerges as a consistent opponent of interaction with foreigners of any kind across three different coastal provinces, in spite of the actual differences in these provinces' situations. Pang viewed the presence of outsiders with unknowable intentions on Ming territory as an unacceptable strategic risk and envisioned meeting it via setpiece land battles in which control of the terrain was the key determinant of victory. I have found no indication that he ever envisioned proactive naval defense a workable strategy against the Portuguese or Japanese threats.
