Nested scopes: Southeast China, Fujian, Minnan, Chao-Zhang, Quanzhou and Zhangzhou, Jinjiang and Tong'an (Longxi and Haicheng), Quanzhou City and Anhai. Focus on Minnan as the low, narrow, humid, sandy coastal region ringing Xiamen Bay, pressed on all sides by high mountains and deep ocean, defined by tides and sedimentation, and reaching up into the Wuyi mountains by way of the various turbulent streams and rivers. Connected to but distinct from Chaozhou and Xinghua, and largely cut off from the mountainous interior (except Longyan).
- Focus on the process of "exteriorization" identified by Li Zhijun, the general tendency across the period for the state and society to move outwards into the seas. Starts with smuggler-pirate strongholds on outlying islands, moves towards the "forward bases" of coastal weisuo, ends with the takeovers of Penghu and Taiwan, mass Hokkien migration to Southeast Asia, and rise of Xiamen. Contrast this with the strategies and fates of PRD (establishment of foreign outposts at the "outskirts," local society and state stay centered inland) and Zhejiang (successful suppression of foreign trade at Ningbo and Zhoushan archipelago, orientation inland towards Jiangnan).
