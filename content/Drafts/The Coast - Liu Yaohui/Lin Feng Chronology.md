### Complete Chronology of Lin Feng (林鳳, Limahong)*\* 

#### Phase 0: Emergence
- Pre-history: emerges as one of the successors to Wu Ping's pirate network operating from Nan'ao; Lin is a "grand-nephew" of Wu. Fights under Zeng Yiben until the latter is killed in Longqing 3. 
  
#### Phase I: Consolidation on Nan'ao (1571–1573)

- **Longqing 5–6 (1571–72):** Operating from Raoping and Nan’ao on the Fujian-Guangdong border, Lin Feng emerges as a major pirate leader in Chaozhou prefecture. He raids Shenquan Town (神泉鎮) and Chenghai (澄海), commanding a mixed fleet of Fujianese and Guangdong vessels.
- **July 1572:** Ming General Yu Dayou assesses Lin’s fleet at \*\*43 ships\*\* (38 \*Baicang\* 白艚 ships of 2,000–3,000 shi and 5 \*Wuwei\* 烏尾 ships of 3,000–4,000 shi). Yu notes that Lin’s crew includes Japanese (\*wokou\*) and men from Leizhou, Lianzhou, and Hainan, and predicts he will retreat westward rather than attack Fujian directly. 

#### Phase II: The Taiwan Interlude (1573)
**April–May 1573 (Wanli 1):** Anticipating a major Ming offensive in Guangdong, Lin Feng flees north into the Taiwan Strait with 50+ ships, occupying Penghu (澎湖). 
**June 1573:** Pursued by Fujian naval forces under Regional Commander Yu Dayou, Lin Feng finds his large Wuwei (烏尾) ships unsuited for the shallow waters of Taiwan’s southwest coast. He scuttles 8 large Wuwei ships (3,000–4,000 shi) and transfers his forces to 82 smaller boats, fleeing to Wanggang (魍港) at the mouth of the Xingang River (新港溪, modern Tainan).
**Late 1573:** Lin Feng clashes with rival Chaozhou pirate Lin Daoqian (林道乾) in Taiwanese waters. Defeating Lin Daoqian, he seizes 55 of his ships, temporarily swelling his fleet to 95 vessels (the peak of his naval power). Returns to Chaozhou.

#### Phase III: The Luzon Expedition (1574–1575)\*\* 
- **March, 1574:** Ministry of War proposes joint Fujian-Liangguang campaign against Lin Feng, anchored at Nan'ao. Court approves request for coordinated 夾剿 operations under Yin Zhengmao and Liu Yaohui.

* \*\*November 3, 1574:\*\* After attrition from the Taiwan campaigns, Lin Feng sails south with \*\*70 ships\*\* (predominantly \*Baicang\*-type vessels of 150–200 Spanish toneladas) and approximately \*\*3,000 men\*\*. Capturing a Fujianese merchant vessel returning from Manila, he learns of Spanish wealth.
- **November 30, 1574:** Lin Feng’s fleet arrives in Manila Bay. His Japanese captain Sioco leads **700–800 men** ashore in a dawn raid, killing Spanish Master of Camp Martín de Goíti and burning the town, but failing to penetrate the Spanish palisade. \*
- *\*December 2, 1574:\*\* Second assault on Manila repelled by Spanish reinforcements under Juan de Salcedo. Lin Feng withdraws to Cavite, then sails north to \*\*Pangasinan\*\* (玳瑁港/Agno River), where he constructs fortifications. \*
- *\*March 1575:\*\* Spanish Governor Guido de Lavezaris dispatches Juan de Salcedo with \*\*250 Spaniards and 2,500–3,000 Luzonese warriors\*\* to blockade the Agno River. \* \*\*March 30, 1575:\*\* Spanish-Luzonese forces attack Lin’s fortified camp. The Ming navy, under Squad Leader \*\*Wang Wanggao\*\* (王望高), arrives in the Lingayen Gulf, inaugurating the first Sino-Spanish military cooperation. \* \
- *\*April–August 1575:\*\* Tripartite blockade (Spanish, Luzonese, Ming) traps Lin Feng. During the siege, Lin’s personal secretary defects to the Spanish, revealing that the pirates have sufficient supplies for several months but face deteriorating conditions. \*
- *\*August 1575:\*\* Lin Feng breaks the blockade. He escapes Pangasinan with \*\*33 newly built ships\*\* (reconstructed after the Spanish burned 35 of his vessels in March) and approximately \*\*1,000 men\*\*, sailing back toward Taiwan. \*\*Phase IV: Final Pursuits and Disappearance (1575–1576)\*\* \*
- *\*September 1575:\*\* Lin Feng’s fleet straggles back to \*\*Chaozhou waters\*\* (Haimen 海門). Fujian Grand Coordinator \*\*Liu Yaohui\*\* dispatches troops under Hu Shouren to coordinate with Guangdong forces for a final elimination campaign. \* \*\*October 1575:\*\* Ming forces pursue Lin Feng to \*\*Danshui\*\* (淡水洋)—likely the waters off northern Taiwan or the Taiwan Strait. Liu Yaohui reports that Lin’s forces have been reduced to remnants. \* \*
- **November 1575:** Lin Feng’s subordinates \*\*Ma Zhishan\*\* and \*\*Zhou Xiao\*\* attempt to negotiate surrender with Guangdong officials, but Lin Feng himself refuses. The Ming court debates whether to accept the surrender of his followers while hunting Lin himself. 
- **1576:** The \*Ming Shilu\* records the final suppression of his remnants in Wanli 4 (1576), noting that Wang Wanggao employed Luzonese auxiliaries to burn Lin’s remaining ships, after which Lin Feng “burst through the cordon and fled” (潰圍遁), never to be reliably heard from again. 1712 of his men surrender to the Guangdong authorities under Jin Zhe. 
- **Summary of Force Numbers** 
- 1572: 43 ships (Yu Dayou’s count)
- Late 1573: 95 ships (peak, after defeating Lin Daoqian)
- Nov 1574: 70 ships (Luzon invasion force)
- March 1575: 35 ships burned at Pangasinan
- Aug 1575: 33 ships escape Luzon
- 1576: Remnants destroyed or surrendered; Lin Feng disappears.
