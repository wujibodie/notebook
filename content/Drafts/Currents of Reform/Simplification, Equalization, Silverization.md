Idle thought: Part of what I want to show in this dissertation is how and why the "Single Whip" differed in Fujian from other provinces. I hope to set up a problem of why Fujian wasn't *already* seen as being on board with Single Whip policies by Wanli 1, despite having heavily silverized local finances, and how Pang and Geng's attempts to bring it into line with the other commercialized southeastern provinces ultimately reinforced the Minnan sonderweg. Part of the explanation, in my view, is precisely the "outward-facing" duties and opportunities provided by Fujian's involvement in ocean trade; merchant-pirates are the chief drain on the military budget, but legalized trade to Manila is a lifeline (virtually silver coming out of thin air to solve the hole in the budget!), and the two feed on each other and on a totally opaque and thus nigh-untaxable local economy which the Zhang Juzheng era reforms never manage to bring to heel. Thus, I hope that I can conclude my Liu chapter having demonstrated exactly why Fujian wouldn't be easily amenable to the package of reforms Pang brought over from experiences in Guangdong and Zhejiang. The *maritime* contrast (taxation of out-going Fujianese ships vs. the Macao compromise in Guangdong and suppression of foreign commerce in Zhejiang) reinforces and explains the *fiscal* contrast (lack of centralized and simplified "single whip" program in Fujian); both also feed into the *land* issue which will be dealt with in Chapter 1 (agrarian deficit Minnan, already dependent on grain imports by boat, has totally different dynamics than net-surplus Liangguang and Zhejiang). (In my current plans, Chapter 2 is not focused on Fujian at all, but on the empire-wide background of fiscal reform leading up to the Single Whip synthesis and the formation of the "reform group" around Zhang Juzheng, of which Liu was an early and important member; but it might be ambitious to cover all that ground in just one chapter.)

Instead of a broad empire-wide overview, focus it tightly on **creating a direct comparator for Fujian**. Structure it around answering the question: "What did successful Single Whip reform look like in the other commercialized southeastern provinces?"

- **Section 1: The Pearl River Delta (Guangdong) Model.** Focus on the "equalization" (均平/均徭) tradition, the role of local gentry, and the _Macao compromise_—how a controlled, gateway model of foreign trade allowed for a more manageable fiscal integration.
    
- **Section 2: The Zhejiang-Jiangnan Model.** Focus on the "Single Whip" synthesis proper, its origins in anti-wokou logistics, and how the _suppression of foreign commerce at Ningbo_ oriented the economy inward, making land and internal trade the clear bases for taxation. - **Section 3: The Zhang Juzheng Reform Collective.** A tighter focus on the network of officials (like Pang Shangpeng) who developed this playbook in the regions above and were then deployed to tackle the "Fujian problem."
