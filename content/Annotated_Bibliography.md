
## PRIMARY SOURCES

### Collected Works and Memorials

Chen Maoren 陳懋仁. *Quannan Zazhi 泉南雜誌* [Miscellaneous Records of Southern Quanzhou]. Original preface dated to author's service as Quanzhou Prefectural Manager (*jingli*) during Wanli reign. Modern editions available in various Ming-Qing biji congshu and digital archives.

Chen Maoren's *Quannan Zazhi* offers a rare firsthand administrative perspective on late Ming Quanzhou, written by a low-ranking official from Jiaxing who served in the region during the late Wanli period. Unlike the elevated perspectives of provincial governors, Chen's miscellany records the practical challenges of local governance, including tax evasion, land disputes, and corrupt practices. The work documents the continued vibrancy of Quanzhou's commercial economy decades after Liu Yaohui's reforms, including detailed descriptions of the Dehua porcelain industry and extensive trade in exotic maritime products. Chen's observations on the density of population and scarcity of land confirm the structural agricultural deficit that drove Minnan's commercial orientation since the Song dynasty. The work illuminates administrative challenges including granary shortfalls, the practice of converting tax obligations to silver commutation, and the circulation of counterfeit silver in Quanzhou city.

Geng Dingxiang 耿定向. *Geng Dingxiang Ji* 耿定向集 [Collected Works of Geng Dingxiang]. Originally compiled during the Ming dynasty.

Geng Dingxiang (1524-1596) was a prominent Neo-Confucian scholar-official who served as Provincial Education Commissioner in Fujian and later as Grand Secretary during the Wanli reign. His collected works provide crucial insight into the intellectual and administrative world of late Ming Fujian, particularly regarding the intersection of moral philosophy and governance. Particularly significant are Geng's observations on regional character and his correspondence with key reformers including Zhang Juzheng and Liu Yaohui. His preface to the Fujian Provincial Examination Record engages with the characterization of Fujian as "Zoulu by the sea," tracing the region's cultural legitimacy through Zhu Xi's learning while acknowledging its distinctive maritime character.

Gu Yanwu 顧炎武. *Tianxia Junguo Libing Shu* 天下郡國利病書 [The Strategic Advantages and Disadvantages of the Prefectures of the Empire]. In *Gu Tinglin Shiwen Ji* [Collected Works of Gu Yanwu]. Beijing: Zhonghua Shuju, 1959.

Gu Yanwu's magisterial survey of Ming provincial administration provides the most comprehensive analysis of Fujian's fiscal and military institutions during their late Ming transformation. The work's Quanzhou section opens with a devastating critique of weisuo military colony collapse, documenting how soldiers pawned land to local elites and became rent-collectors vulnerable to famine. The analysis of Quanzhou land taxation reveals the complexity that frustrated reform efforts, including detailed documentation from Wanli-era gazetteers on land categories and the "floating grain" problem. For understanding the Single Whip's impact, Gu's Zhangzhou section attributes the reform's invention to Wang Zongmu's lost treatise *Jun Shu*, documenting Pang Shangpeng's implementation through direct quotation. The work's coverage of maritime taxation provides essential documentation of Liu Yaohui's 1572 customs establishment and its subsequent evolution, distinguishing between tribute trade and merchant shipping while documenting the three-tier tax structure.

He Qiaoyuan 何喬遠. *Min Shu* 閩書 [Book of Fujian]. 154 juan. Fujian: Ming Wanli period manuscript; modern editions available.

He Qiaoyuan's monumental *Min Shu* stands as the most comprehensive provincial history of Fujian compiled during the Ming dynasty, providing indispensable documentation of Minnan's fiscal, military, and commercial transformation during the Jiajing-Wanli periods. As a Quanzhou literatus who served as Minister of Rites, He drew upon extensive personal networks to compile detailed accounts of maritime trade, the wokou crisis, and relations with overseas Chinese communities. The work contains crucial memorials regarding coastal defense reorganization and systematic documentation of institutional reforms under successive Fujian governors. The *Min Shu* provides quantitative benchmarks for maritime commercial taxation and preserves unique details about the Manila massacre's impact on Quanzhou merchants.

Huang Fengxiang 黃鳳翔. *Jiajing Dazheng Leibian* 嘉靖大政類編 [Classified Compilation of Major Jiajing Reign Policies]. Juan on "Southern Wo" (*Nan Wo*), pp. 471-485. *Siku Quanshu Cunmu Congshu* *Shibu* vol. 55. Jinan: Qi Lu Shushe, 1996.

Huang Fengxiang's classified compilation provides crucial narrative coverage of the wokou crisis that reshaped Minnan's fiscal-military institutions. The "Southern Wo" section traces the fifty-year evolution of piracy, suppression campaigns, and administrative reform from the 1523 Ningbo Incident through the 1560s campaigns that "exterminated the pirates." The compilation documents the wokou's southward movement into Fujian from winter of iajing 36 (1557), providing harrowing accounts of attacks on Xinghua and Quanzhou. Huang's narrative culminates in the Jiajing 42-43 (1563-1564) campaigns, preserving contemporary assessments of official conduct including Zhu Wan's aggressive anti-piracy enforcement.

Huang Fengxiang 黃鳳翔. *Tian Ting Cao Ershi Juan* 田亭草二十卷 [The Twenty-Chapter Tian Pavilion Collection]. Wanli 40 (1612) edition. Reprinted in *Xuxiu Siku Quanshu*, vol. 1356. Shanghai: Shanghai Guji Chubanshe, 2002.

This collected work by the late Ming Quanzhou literatus Huang Fengxiang contains 98 prefaces and numerous temple inscriptions, many focused on the Quanzhou region. The most historically significant section is the "Anping San Liezhuan" (Three Biographies of Anping Martyrs), documenting female suicide following the 1603 Manila massacre that devastated the overseas Chinese merchant community. The collection offers invaluable grassroots perspective on the social consequences of maritime commercialization, demonstrating the risks overseas traders faced despite their integration into global silver flows.

Lin Xiyuan 林希元. *Tong'an Lin Ciya Xiansheng Wenji* 同安林次崖先生文集 [Collected Works of Lin Ciya of Tong'an]. Qianlong 18 edition (1753). Reprinted in *Siku Quanshu Cunmu Congshu*, Jibu 75, 409-761. Jinan: Qilu Shushe, 1997.

Lin Xiyuan's (ca. 1480-1560) collected works represent a crucial primary source for understanding the perspective of a prominent Minnan literatus navigating tensions between Confucian orthodoxy, maritime commerce, and coastal defense. Particularly valuable is Lin's "Discussion of Maritime Bandits" (*Haikou Yi*), which identifies pirates' mobility as their key advantage and recommends recruiting toughs from Dongguan. The collection documents Lin's relationship with Yu Dayou, whom Lin claims to have tutored early in his career and praises as Fujian's only effective commander against the wokou.

Liu Yaohui 劉堯誨. *Xulai Ji* 虛籟集 [Collection of Hollow Sounds]. First memorial edition printed 1757 (Qianlong 22) with colophon describing manuscript discovery in Taihe, Jiangxi.

The *Xulai Ji* preserves Liu Yaohui's writings during his tenure as Fujian Provincial Governor (1572-1576), including memorials documenting efforts to address the fiscal-military crisis. Liu's memorials reveal practical challenges of funding coastal defense, including detailed accounting showing how he cobbled together emergency military budgets from salt commutation silver and temporary surcharges. His writings document initiatives including the establishment of maritime customs at Haicheng and proposals to permanently garrison Nan'ao Island. Liu's letters to Zhang Juzheng reveal a carefully cultivated patronage relationship, while his memorials on the Lin Feng campaign expose tensions with Guangdong authorities over jurisdictional boundaries and competing strategies.

Pang Shangpeng 龐尚鵬. *Baiketing Zhaigao* 百可亭摘稿 [Selected Drafts from the Pavilion of the Hundred Acceptables]. Originally compiled during the Ming dynasty.

Pang Shangpeng (1524-1581) was a pivotal figure in Ming fiscal reform whose administrative career exemplified the transition from local experimentation to empire-wide policy implementation. His collected works document his implementation of the *Junping fa* (Equalization Method) in Zhejiang and Fujian. The correspondence section reveals personal networks sustaining reform efforts, including exchanges with Zhang Juzheng, Tan Lun, and Geng Dingxiang. For historians of Minnan's fiscal transformation, Pang's writings demonstrate how the "laboratory for fiscal reform" functioned, establishing precedents that extended empire-wide during the Wanli reign.

Pang Shangpeng 龐尚鵬. *Junzheng Shiyi* 軍政事宜 [Matters Concerning Military Administration]. Written during Pang's tenure as Fujian Provincial Governor.

Pang Shangpeng's *Junzheng Shiyi* represents a systematic effort to reform Fujian's military administration, articulating principles of "regulation and economization" that influenced subsequent reformers including Liu Yaohui. The treatise addresses practical challenges of maintaining effective coastal defense, emphasizing clear hierarchy and rigorous training. Pang's core principle—that military forces must be organized so that "controlling many is like controlling few"—reflects his synthesis of classical military thought with practical experience against wokou raiders.

Qi Jiguang 戚繼光. *Zhizhi Tang Ji* 止止堂集 [Collected Works from the Hall of Stopping]. Originally compiled during the Ming dynasty.

Qi Jiguang's collected works, centered on the *Minhai Jishi* section, provide detailed autobiographical accounts of his campaigns in Fujian between 1562 and 1567. The work documents Qi's pivotal relationship with Wang Daokun, the Fujian surveillance commissioner who championed Qi's strategy of recruiting local troops. Qi's writings reveal how successful campaigns depended on personal relationships between commanders and local officials, the professionalization of military labor through silver wages, and the gradual eclipse of the *weisuo* system.

Tan Lun 譚綸. *Tan Lun Zouyi* 譚綸奏議 [Memorials of Tan Lun]. Originally compiled during the Ming dynasty.

Tan Lun's memorials from Fujian provide authoritative documentation of coastal defense reform during the critical 1562-1564 period. The collection documents collaborative operations that broke pirate power, including the decisive victory at Pinghaiwei in April 1563. More significantly, Tan proposes twelve fundamental reforms that restructured Fujian's coastal defense system, revealing sophisticated understanding of maritime strategy and explicitly connecting military institutional decline to the broader fiscal crisis.

Wang Daokun 汪道昆. *Taihan Ji* 太函集 [Collected Works from the Great Enclosure]. Various editions, compiled during the Ming Dynasty.

Wang Daokun's massive collection contains approximately 1,700 poems plus extensive prose. The work documents Wang's relationships with key military and political figures including Qi Jiguang, Tan Lun, and Zhang Juzheng. Several notable pieces document the transfer of military commanders between southern and northern frontier postings. From a historiographical perspective, the *Taihan Ji* presents complex interpretive challenges, as Wang appears in the *Ming Shi* only as an appendix to Wang Shizhen's biography, yet his collected works suggest deeper engagement with military affairs.

Xie Zhaozhe 謝肇淛. *Wuzazu* 五雜組 [Five Miscellanies]. Originally published in the late Ming dynasty (ca. 1610s).

Xie Zhaozhe's encyclopedic compendia represents one of the most comprehensive portraits of late Ming social, economic, and cultural life. Born into a prominent Fujian merchant family, Xie brought particular sensitivity to regional economic variation. The work provides invaluable documentation of Minnan's commercial exceptionalism, including discussion of regional market terminology and comparison of Nanjing and Fuzhou. Xie's observations on the dismantling of the *tuntian* system and fiscal consequences of military professionalization align with broader trends in administrative sources.

Xu Fuyuan 許孚遠. *Jinghe Tang Ji* 敬和堂集 [Collection of the Respectful Harmony Hall]. Late Ming woodblock edition.

This collected work by Xu Fuyuan, a Ming official who served in Fujian, contains philosophical correspondence and temple inscriptions. The collection contains relatively little direct reference to Xu's tenure in charge of Fujian administration, distinguishing it from more explicitly policy-focused collected works. For researchers examining tensions between commercial realities and Confucian moral discourse, this collection exemplifies how officials expressed their public identities through philosophical rather than policy writings.

Yu Dayou 俞大猷. *Zhengqi Tang Quanji* 正氣堂集 [Collected Works from the Hall of Upright Qi]. Compiled by Lin Fengyi 林鳳儀 et al. Beijing: Beijing chubanshe, 200?.

This extensive collection comprises the writings of Yu Dayou (1503-1580), one of the Ming dynasty's preeminent military commanders. The collection encompasses memorials, letters, military memoranda, and treatises on naval and land warfare. Particularly valuable are detailed correspondence regarding campaigns against Lin Feng's pirate fleet in 1574-1575, documenting the strategic deployment of Fujian's distinctive "white junks" and challenges of inter-provincial coordination. The work reveals tension between bureaucratic military structures and charismatic local leadership, as well as fiscal pressures driving military professionalization.

Zhang Juzheng 張居正. *Zhang Juzheng Ji* 張居正集 [Collected Works of Zhang Juzheng]. Edited by Zhang Shizhe 張舜徽. 4 vols. Wuhan: Hubei renmin chubanshe, 1987.

Zhang Juzheng's collected correspondence, particularly letters to Liu Yaohui and Geng Dingxiang, constitutes essential documentation for understanding the late Ming reform network. The letters reveal Zhang's strategic vision for coastal defense, his assessment of military personnel, and management of inter-provincial coordination during the Lin Feng campaigns. Beyond military matters, the letters articulate philosophical and practical foundations of the reform program, defending Single Whip reforms against charges of "harshness."

### Official Records

*Ming Shilu* 明實錄 [Veritable Records of the Ming]. Compiled by the Ming History Bureau. Modern editions include Academia Sinica searchable database and Zhongyang yanjiuyuan editions (1961-1966).

The Ming Veritable Records constitute the official court chronicle, providing authoritative documentation of central government perspectives. Entries concerning Liu Yaohui's career illuminate his rise from junior censor to Fujian Provincial Governor. For the Lin Feng campaigns of 1574-1576, the *Shilu* provides official chronology and the court's understanding of developments, documenting interventions in provincial disputes and formal protocols structuring center-province relations.

*Ming Shi* 明史 [History of Ming]. Compiled by Zhang Tingyu 張廷玉 et al. 1739. Reprint, Beijing: Zhonghua Shuju, 1974.

The *Ming Shi* provides standardized biographies of major Ming officials. Notable biographies include Tan Lun and Qi Jiguang, documenting their campaigns against the wokou pirates. The biography of Qi Jiguang provides detailed accounts of tactical innovations and campaigns that pacified Fujian's coastal region.

### Local Gazetteers

《安平志》*Anping Zhi* [Anping Town Gazetteer].

The Anping Town Gazetteer documents the history of Anhai, a major commercial port on the Quanzhou coast. The Wanli era *Huji Zhi* records Anhai's administrative structure, while the *Gongshu Zhi* traces institutional evolution from the Jiajing defense crisis through the Wanli commercial boom. The *Renwu Zhi* documents Anhai's commercial diaspora and the devastating impact of the 1603 Manila massacre, including accounts of women widowed by the massacre.

《嘉靖安溪縣志》*Jiajing Anxi Xianzhi* [Jiajing Anxi County Gazetteer].

The Jiajing era Anxi County Gazetteer contains exceptionally detailed land registration records spanning 161 years from Hongwu 24 (1391) to Jiajing 31 (1552). The data reveals remarkable stability in total registered land over a century and a half, providing crucial comparative evidence for understanding how coastal commercial counties differed from mountainous interior counties.

《海澄縣志》*Haicheng Xianzhi* [Haicheng County Gazetteer].

The Haicheng County Gazetteer documents the establishment and development of Haicheng County, created in Longqing 1 (1567) from coastal territories formerly part of Longxi County. The gazetteer traces the evolution of coastal defense infrastructure from the Jiajing crisis through the Tianqi period, documenting key moments of maritime threat response.

《嘉靖惠安縣誌》*Jiajing Hui'an Xianzhi* [Jiajing Hui'an County Gazetteer].

The Jiajing era Hui'an County Gazetteer contains valuable household registration data from the Jiajing 1st year (1522) yellow registers. The data shows 1,833 civilian households versus 1,360 military households—a remarkable inversion where military households averaged significantly more adult males per household than civilians.

《萬曆泉州府志》*Wanli Quanzhou Fuzhi* [Wanli Quanzhou Prefectural Gazetteer].

The Wanli era Quanzhou Prefectural Gazetteer represents a comprehensive revision following demographic and fiscal disruption during the Jiajing wokou crisis. The demographic and fiscal sections document catastrophic impact and subsequent recovery: household and population registers dropped by 60-70% following Jiajing raids, recovering over forty years. The gazetteer provides detailed land survey records from the Wanli 10 (1582) Fangtian survey.

《正德漳州府志》*Zhengde Zhangzhou Fuzhi* [Zhengde Zhangzhou Prefectural Gazetteer].

The Zhengde era Zhangzhou Prefectural Gazetteer represents the earliest comprehensive administrative record of Zhangzhou during the Ming. The gazetteer preserves crucial evidence for pre-wokou commercial development, including the earliest documentary reference to Yuegang as a commercial center before its formal opening to foreign trade in 1567.

Ye Chunji 葉春及. *Hui'an Zhengshu* 惠安政書 [Administrative Records of Hui'an].

Ye Chunji compiled this comprehensive administrative manual during his tenure as magistrate of Hui'an County (circa 1570s). The work represents practical application of reformist governance philosophy, combining moral community reconstruction with fiscal reform. Ye's fiscal tables document the long-standing practice of silver commutation for taxes in Fujian.

## SECONDARY SOURCES

### Western Language Scholarship

Brook, Timothy. *The Chinese State in Ming Society*. London: RoutledgeCurzon, 2005.

Timothy Brook's seminal work examines interactions between the Ming state and local society, providing essential theoretical grounding for understanding 16th-century Minnan's fiscal transformation. Chapter 2's study of Ye Chunji, magistrate of Huian County from 1571, offers a crucial case study for Fujian's Single Whip reform implementation. Brook shows how Ye's land survey attempts were resisted by local gentry. Chapter 7's analysis of Buddhist institutions documents Fujian-specific interventions in the 1570s-1580s when "the state needed emergency funds to meet military costs."

Grove, Linda, and Christian Daniels, eds. *State and Society in China: Japanese Perspectives on Ming-Qing Social and Economic History*. Tokyo: University of Tokyo Press, 1984.

This edited volume translates seminal Japanese scholarship on Ming-Qing social and economic history. The collection includes essays on large landownership in the Jiangnan delta, popular uprisings and rent resistance, and rural control in the Ming dynasty. Yamane Yukio's contribution traces reforms in the service levy system, documenting how the equalized labor service was introduced to Fujian by Administration Commissioner Zhu Ying.

Rawski, Evelyn Sakakida. *Agricultural Change and the Peasant Economy of South China*. Harvard East Asian Series 66. Cambridge: Harvard University Press, 1972.

This foundational study examines agricultural development in Fujian and Guangdong during the Ming and Qing periods. Rawski demonstrates that Zhangzhou prefecture experienced explosive market growth, expanding from 11 periodic markets in 1491 to 65 by 1628. Her analysis of tenancy systems documents the "one field, two lords" system widespread by the sixteenth century, enabling tenant farmers to absorb productivity gains while landlords received fixed rents.

So, K. L. (Su Jilang). *Prosperity, Region, and Institutions in Maritime China: The South Fukien Pattern, 946-1368*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Asia Center, 2000.

K.L. So's magisterial study examines Minnan's economic transformation from the Tang through Southern Song, establishing the region's exceptional maritime-commercial development. The work documents how Southern Song Minnan developed unusual social formation characterized by interpenetration of merchant wealth, lineage organization, and state institutions. So's analysis of the ceramics industry, Buddhist temple landholding, and Quanzhou-Nan'an urban structure provides essential background for understanding Minnan's later fiscal exceptionalism.

Szonyi, Michael. *The Art of Being Governed: Everyday Politics in Late Imperial China*. Princeton: Princeton University Press, 2017.

Michael Szonyi's study of Ming military households provides critical context for understanding coastal defense organization in 16th-century Fujian. Szonyi demonstrates that coastal *weisuo* basic role was policing rather than confronting major forces. Particularly relevant is the discussion of Zhu Wan, who "made a good start at restoring order" but antagonized powerful local interests, including his identification of Lin Xiyuan as "the worst of the Fujian literati in league with pirates and smugglers."

Theobald, Ulrich. "Control the Uncontrollable: The Monetary Policy of the Ming and Qing Empires." In *Money in Asia (1200-1900): Small Currencies in Social and Political Contexts*, edited by Jane Kate Leonard and Ulrich Theobald, 15-46. Leiden: Brill, 2015.

Ulrich Theobald's chapter provides crucial context for understanding the silverization of Fujian's fiscal system. Theobald emphasizes that "in China seigniorage played a very marginal role in monetary politics" because Confucian ideology held that money production should be "beneficial to the people" rather than profit-seeking. For Fujian's fiscal reforms, Theobald's analysis suggests the turn toward silver was shaped by ideological constraints on state monetary policy.

### Japanese Scholarship

Iwai Shigeki. *Chūgoku Kindai Zaiseishi Kenkyū* [Studies in Modern Chinese Fiscal History]. Kyoto: Kyoto University Press, 2004.

Iwai Shigeki's seminal work examines the fundamental tension between "fixed quotas" and "floating assessments" in Chinese tax policy. For research on Minnan fiscal reform, Iwai's work provides crucial theoretical grounding for understanding structural pressures facing reformers. His analysis of how the lijia system's original design created friction when revenue collection imperatives were imposed illuminates the broader context of Fujian's fiscal crisis.

Kishimoto Mio 岸本美緒. *Fengsu yu Lishiguan* 風俗與歷史觀 [Custom and Views of History]. Beijing: Shangwu Yinshuguan, 2018.

Kishimoto Mio's collection of essays presents methodological intervention in the study of Chinese social and economic history. Particularly valuable is Kishimoto's analysis of *fengsu* as both descriptive and normative concept in Chinese political thought. For 16th-century Fujian fiscal history, Kishimoto's framework illuminates how contemporaries understood social transformations they witnessed.

Kishimoto Mio 岸本美緒. "Wan Ming de Baiyin Beiliu Wenti" (晚明的白银北流問題) [The Problem of Silver Flowing North in Late Ming]. *Zhongguo Jingjishi Yanjiu* (中国经济史研究), no. 1 (2020): 5-15.

Kishimoto's article traces the intellectual history of debates surrounding silver transfers to northern frontier garrisons during the late Ming. She documents how "silver flowing north, going without return" (白銀北流，往而不返) became fiscal common sense, with Beijing-sourced military allocations growing from 700,000 taels annually in the early 16th century to 4 million by the early 17th (p. 8). Crucially for Minnan studies, Kishimoto analyzes He Qiaoyuan's 1630 proposal contrasting northern "exit holes" (出孔) with maritime "entry holes" (入孔)—explicitly framing Fujian's overseas trade as solving empire-wide silver shortages. The article provides essential context for understanding how Liu Yaohui's maritime customs establishment represented a structural response to the fiscal pressures documented in northern debates.

Kuroda Akinobu 黑田明伸. "Zhongguo Huobishi shang de Yongyin Zhuanbian" (中国货币史上的用银转变) [Transformation of Silver Usage in Chinese Monetary History]. *Zhongguo Jingjishi Yanjiu* (中国经济史研究), no. 1 (2020): 17-26.

Kuroda's magisterial synthesis traces China's distinctive monetary evolution from silk supplementary to silver-by-weight dominance. For Minnan, his analysis of land contracts from Nanping, Fujian is decisive: while early Ming deeds used paper notes, contracts from 1501-1638 specified weighed silver (p. 19), confirming silverization preceded maritime trade influx. Kuroda's "complementary structure" framework—copper coins for local transactions, silver for interregional trade—explains how Single Whip reforms operated within, rather than against, monetary pluralism. His concept of "imaginary silver units" (虛銀兩) for merchant bookkeeping illuminates the credit mechanisms Liu Yaohui's customs administration had to accommodate.

Matsumoto Yoshimi 松本善海. *Chūgoku Chihō Jichi Hatten Shi* [History of the Development of Chinese Local Autonomy]. Tokyo: Kyūko Shoin, 1977.

Matsumoto's foundational study traces evolution of Ming fiscal institutions from Hongwu land surveys through mid-Ming reforms. For Minnan fiscal reform, Matsumoto's work provides essential institutional background. His observation that Jiajing fiscal debates focused on simplification of overly complex systems helps contextualize why Fujian reformers emphasized transparency.

Oyama Masaaki 小山正明. "Fu, Yi Zhidu de Biange" [Changes in Tax and Corvée Systems]. In *Nihon Gakusha* [Japanese Scholars on Ming-Qing Fiscal and Labor Service Institutions], 1992.

Oyama Masaaki's chapter defines the Ming-Qing fiscal system as transitional between Tang-Song two-tax system and post-revolutionary system. Oyama's analysis of the "Ten-Section Method" identifies its core innovation as levying fixed silver fees on adult males and land registered in the *huangce*. For Minnan fiscal reform, Oyama's work provides insight into institutional pressures shaping Fujian's trajectory.

Taniguchi Kikuo 谷口規矩雄. "Nihon no Mindai Yōeki Kenkyū" [Japanese Research on Ming Corvée]. In *Nihon Gakusha* [Japanese Scholars on Ming-Qing Fiscal and Labor Service Institutions], 1992.

Taniguchi Kikuo's literature review synthesizes scholarship on Ming corvée systems, focusing on how commutation of labor services affected social status distinctions. For Minnan fiscal reform, Taniguchi's synthesis provides historiographical context for institutional frameworks Fujian officials manipulated.

### Chinese Scholarship

Chen Boyi 陳博翼. *Haifang zhi Dao: Mingdai Nanhai diqu de Haiyu, Junzheng, Shehui yu Zongjiao* [The Way of Coastal Defense: Maritime Domains, Military-Political Affairs, Society, and Religion in the South Sea Region During the Ming Dynasty]. Beijing: Zhonghua Shuju, 2023.

Chen Boyi's comprehensive study examines evolution of Ming coastal defense institutions, focusing on surveillance officials' acquisition of specialized authority. For Minnan fiscal and military institutions, Chen's work provides methodological foundations. His critique of over-reliance on military treatises supports the dissertation's use of local gazetteers.

Chen Chunsheng 陳春聲. *Cong Woluan dao Qianhai: Chaozhou yu Ming-Qing Haijiang Shehui de Yanbian* [From Wokou Troubles to Coastal Clearance: Chaozhou and the Evolution of Ming-Qing Maritime Society]. Beijing: Zhongguo shehui kexue chubanshe, 2017.

Chen Chunsheng's magisterial study of Chaozhou reconceptualizes wokou troubles and coastal clearance as continuous processes of socio-political transformation. For Minnan, Chen's study provides essential comparative context for the Fujian-Guangdong borderlands. His analysis of mutual recriminations between Fujian and Guangdong officials illuminates jurisdictional tensions structuring cross-provincial military coordination.

Deng Zhihua. "Pang Shangpeng" chapter in *Fengjiang Dali yu Shehui Biange* [Frontier Governors and Social Change]. Unpublished manuscript.

Deng Zhihua's biographical study provides the most detailed reconstruction of Pang Shangpeng's career trajectory and institutional innovations. The study's core contribution is analysis of Pang's Zhejiang reforms as cumulative syntheses of county-level experimentation. Deng's analysis of Pang's Fujian tenure emphasizes continuity with Zhejiang methods while noting distinctive features including remitting 500,000+ taels of accumulated military debt.

Ding Liang 丁亮. "Mingdai Zhejiang Caizheng Zhidu Biange Yanjiu—Yi Lijia, Junyao, Yichuan Weizhongxin" [Research on Fiscal Institution Reform in Ming Dynasty Zhejiang]. PhD diss., Tsinghua University, 2014.

This dissertation examines transformation of Zhejiang's fiscal system, challenging interpretations viewing shift toward hiring substitutes as "silverization." Ding argues true silverization only occurred with Pang Shangpeng's reforms. For Minnan fiscal reform, Ding's work provides essential comparative context for understanding Pang Shangpeng's subsequent Fujian tenure.

Feng Ming 馮明. "Zhang Juzheng Gaige Qunti Goucheng" [The Composition of Zhang Juzheng's Reform Collective]. Unpublished research notes.

This analytical study examines personal and political networks sustaining Zhang Juzheng's reform administration. The analysis reveals crucial role of Fujian officials within reform collective. For Minnan's place within late Ming reform movements, this study illuminates how provincial officials connected to broader networks.

Fu Yiling. *Ming-Qing Shidai Shangren ji Shangye Ziben* [Merchants and Commercial Capital in the Ming-Qing Period]. Beijing: Renmin Chubanshe, 1956.

Fu Yiling's foundational study examines development of Chinese commercial capitalism. For Minnan, Fu documents Fujian merchants' transition from domestic to overseas trade, citing Jiajing-era reports and Portuguese presence at Yuegang. His analysis of Haicheng *pushang* illustrates how commercial self-regulation emerged alongside official maritime taxation.

Ha Yiming 哈伊鳴. "Mingdai Junshi Zhidu Yanbian" [Evolution of Ming Military Institutions]. PhD dissertation.

This dissertation analyzes transformation of Ming military institutions from early dynasty through late 16th century. The work is particularly valuable for understanding Minnan's military transformation, documenting how Yu Dayou's troops received higher compensation than hereditary *weisuo* soldiers. Ha's analysis connects military professionalization directly to Single Whip reforms.

Huang, Ray (Huang Renyu). "Military Expenditures in Sixteenth Century Ming China." In *Chinese Ways in Warfare*, edited by Frank Kierman and John Fairbank, 35-68. Cambridge: Harvard University Press, 1974.

This foundational essay analyzes fiscal pressures driving Ming military reform, focusing on unsustainable budgetary pressures. Huang documents distinctive characteristics of southern military finance: funds raised locally rather than through Ministry of Revenue, and new revenues created during wokou campaigns never abolished afterward.

Li Bao 李寶. "Ming Zhongye Fujian Caizheng Shouru Tizhi Yanbian Yanjiu" [Research on the Evolution of Mid-Ming Fujian's Fiscal Revenue System]. Master's thesis.

Li Bao's thesis challenges prevailing narratives about Ming fiscal decline, demonstrating that government capacities expanded through *yifa* reforms. The thesis traces reform stages including the *bafen fa* (1520), *shiduan fa* (1537), and Single Whip method, arguing implementation was "smooth" in Fujian because earlier reforms prepared the ground.

Li Xiaoti 李孝悌. "Wan Ming Quanzhou Shidafu de Shenghuo yu Qingqu" [The Life and Sentiments of Late Ming Quanzhou Literati]. In *Zhongguo de Chengshi Shenghuo* [Urban Life in China], edited by Li Xiaoti, 1-47. Beijing: Xinxing Chubanshe, 2006.

This chapter examines cultural and social transformations among Quanzhou's literati class, focusing on figures including Li Zhi, Lin Xiyuan, He Qiaoyuan, and Li Guangjin. For research on Minnan fiscal institutions, this work provides essential social context for constraints facing reformers.

Li Zhijun 李之琇. *Fengxia zhi Hai* 風下之海 [The Sea Under the Wind]. Beijing: Unspecified publisher.

Li Zhijun's work proposes conceptual framework for understanding Minnan's position through dual processes of "interiorization" and "exteriorization." The study places emphasis on environmental factors including Little Ice Age impacts on subtropical monsoon region. For 16th-century Minnan studies, Li's framework provides context for agricultural deficits driving commercialization.

Qiao Gen. *Wang Daokun Wenxue Yanjiu* [Literary Study of Wang Daokun]. Beijing: Unspecified publisher.

Qiao Gen's study addresses weaknesses in Wang Daokun scholarship, proposing critical appraisal beyond criticisms of Wang Shizhen and Qian Qianyi. The study categorizes Wang's poetic corpus and connects his emphasis on "practical utility" to merchant family background.

Wan Ming 萬明. "Junpingfa Kao: Yi Qinyin Liangzhe Junpinglu Wei Zhongxin" [An Examination of the Equalization Method]. *Zhongguo Shehui Kexue* [Social Sciences in China], no. 2 (2015): 146-157.

Wan Ming's article provides comprehensive analysis of the 1566 *Qinyin Liangzhe Junpinglu*, characterizing it as "the most complete document of a Ming Dynasty fiscal reform." The study clarifies relationship between Junping reforms and Single Whip method. For Minnan fiscal reform, Wan's work is crucial for understanding institutional template Pang Shangpeng attempted to implement.

Wei Qingyuan. *Zhang Juzheng yu Wanli* [Zhang Juzheng and the Wanli Era]. Taipei: Yunlong Chubanshe, 2005.

Wei Qingyuan's comprehensive political biography provides crucial insights into Zhang Juzheng's economic thought. Wei's analysis of Zhang's essay "Preface Presented to Zhou Hanpu" articulates proto-mercantilist philosophy that farmers and merchants "mutually support one another." The work traces bureaucratic politics of Single Whip implementation.

Wu Xiu'an 吳修安. "Fujian's Early Development" [福建早期發展].

This comprehensive study traces Fujian's economic development from Six Dynasties through Song dynasty. Wu adopts Braudel's geographic-economic approach to examine coastal-interior divergence. By late Tang, Quanzhou already had double-cropping rice, and by Five Dynasties, Zhangzhou and Quanzhou had developed gold- and silverworking industries.

Xu Xiaowang 徐曉望. "Lun Mingdai Xiamenwan Zhoubian Gangshi de Fazhan" [On the Development of Port Cities Around Xiamen Bay during the Ming Dynasty].

This specialized study examines evolution of maritime port cities around Xiamen Bay, drawing heavily on Haicheng County gazetteer. The study documents how Haicheng County's strategic location made it a natural transshipment point for foreign goods entering Fujian's interior.

Yang Peina 楊培娜. *Shengji yu Zhidu: Ming-Qing Min-Yue Yanhai Yumin de Huji Guanli yu Haishang Huodong* [Livelihood and Institutions]. Beijing: Shehui kexue wenxian chubanshe, 2019.

Yang Peina's comprehensive study examines intersection of state registration systems, maritime livelihoods, and local social organization. The work is essential for understanding fiscal-military context of 16th-century Minnan, documenting how early Ming efforts to register fishing populations largely collapsed by Jiajing reign.

Zhang Simin 張斯珉. *Weidao zhi Xue: Geng Dingxiang Sixiang Yanjiu* [The Learning of Guarding the Way: Research on Geng Dingxiang's Thought]. Beijing: Renmin Chubanshe, 2019.

Zhang Simin's intellectual biography examines Geng Dingxiang's role as fiscal reformer and conservative Cheng-Zhu philosopher. For Minnan fiscal reform, Zhang's work provides crucial insight into post-crisis normalization of reform institutions.

Zheng Zhenman. *Ming-Qing Fujian Lijia* [Lijia Registration in Ming-Qing Fujian]. In *Ming-Qing Fujian Jiazu Zuzhi yu Shehui Bianqian* [Family Lineage Organization and Social Change in Ming-Qing Fujian]. Changsha: Hunan Jiaoyu Chubanshe, 1992.

Zheng Zhenman's pioneering study examines intersection of Ming fiscal institutions and lineage formation in Fujian. For Minnan fiscal history, Zheng's analysis of lijia system's hereditary transformation provides essential context for understanding how local power structures mediated Single Whip implementation.

Zhou Shangqun 周尚群. "Mingdai Siyuan Jingji" [Ming Dynasty Temple Economies]. Unpublished research notes.

This analysis documents dramatic decline in Buddhist and Daoist temple land ownership from Song to Ming dynasties. For Minnan fiscal history, this analysis provides quantitative evidence for understanding how region's lineage-based social structure emerged through displacement of religious institutions.

## ADDITIONAL SOURCES FROM DISSERTATION DRAFTS

### Western Language Scholarship

Andrade, Tonio. *The Gunpowder Age: China, Military Innovation, and the Rise of the West in World History*. Princeton: Princeton University Press, 2016.

Andrade's comparative study of Chinese and European military development provides crucial context for understanding the Ming military reforms in Fujian. His analysis of agrarian versus maritime revenue regimes illuminates how different geographic and economic contexts shaped military institutional development. For Minnan's fiscal-military transformation, Andrade's framework helps explain why coastal defense required fundamentally different approaches than northern frontier defense.

Chang Pin-tsun. "The Formation of a Maritime Convention in Minnan." In *From the Mediterranean to the China Sea: Miscellaneous Notes*, edited by Claude Guillot, Denys Lombard, and Roderich Ptak, 143-156. Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz Verlag, 1998.

Chang's seminal article examines the development of maritime legal and commercial conventions in Minnan during the Ming dynasty. The work documents how local merchants developed sophisticated contractual practices and risk-sharing mechanisms that enabled large-scale overseas trade despite official prohibitions. For understanding Minnan's fiscal exceptionalism, Chang's analysis reveals how commercial self-regulation emerged alongside—and sometimes in tension with—state maritime taxation systems.

Clark, Hugh R. *Community, Trade, and Networks: Southern Fujian Province from the Third to the Thirteenth Century*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1991.

Clark's foundational study traces the long-term development of southern Fujian's commercial and social institutions from the Tang through Song dynasties. The work documents how Minnan's distinctive lineage organization and merchant networks developed in response to geographic constraints and maritime opportunities. For 16th-century Minnan fiscal history, Clark provides essential background on the institutional foundations that shaped how the region adapted to Ming fiscal pressures.

Colin, Francisco. *Labor Evangelica: Ministerios Apostolicos de los Obreros de la Compañia de Jesus, Fundacion, y Progressos de su Provincia en las Islas Filipinas*. 3 vols. Barcelona, 1900-1902.

Colin's Jesuit chronicle provides crucial Spanish primary source documentation of Lin Feng's 1574 attack on Manila and the subsequent Ming-Spanish diplomatic contacts. The work preserves contemporary accounts of Chinese merchant communities in the Philippines and the Ming state's limited capacity to protect or control overseas traders. For understanding Minnan's integration into global trade networks, Colin documents both the opportunities and risks that maritime commerce entailed.

Kuroda Akinobu. *Global History of Money*. London: Routledge, 2020.

Kuroda's magisterial study of monetary history provides essential theoretical framework for understanding the silverization of Fujian's economy. His analysis of how multiple currencies could coexist within single political spaces illuminates why Fujian's commercial economy gravitated toward silver despite official Ming preferences for copper coinage. For 16th-century Minnan fiscal reform, Kuroda's work explains why monetary policy proved so difficult to implement and why silver became the de facto solution.

Ng Chin-keong. *Trade and Society: The Amoy Network on the China Coast, 1683-1735*. Singapore: Singapore University Press, 1983.

While focused on the Qing period, Ng's study of the Xiamen (Amoy) commercial network provides essential background on how Minnan's maritime trading systems evolved from Ming foundations. The work documents the institutional continuity between late Ming maritime customs systems and Qing commercial organization. For understanding 16th-century Minnan's commercial development, Ng illuminates how the "laboratory" of fiscal innovation established under Liu Yaohui provided templates for later commercial expansion.

Reid, Anthony. *Southeast Asia in the Age of Commerce, 1450-1680*. 2 vols. New Haven: Yale University Press, 1988-1993.

Reid's monumental study of Southeast Asian commercial development provides essential context for understanding Minnan's maritime trading partners. His analysis of maritime absolutist states including Aceh and Johor illuminates the political economy of the trading world into which Fujian merchants inserted themselves. For Minnan fiscal history, Reid's work reveals how the region's commercial prosperity depended on political developments beyond Ming state control.

von Glahn, Richard. *Fountain of Fortune: Money and Monetary Policy in China, 1000-1700*. Berkeley: University of California Press, 1996.

Von Glahn's comprehensive study of Chinese monetary history provides crucial context for understanding Fujian's silverization during the 16th century. His analysis of the transition from bronze coinage to silver bullion illuminates the pressures driving Pang Shangpeng's abortive coinage reform and the broader Single Whip reforms. For Minnan specifically, von Glahn documents how maritime trade connections accelerated monetary transformation in coastal regions compared to interior provinces.

Zelin, Madeleine. *The Magistrate's Tael: Rationalizing Fiscal Reform in Eighteenth-Century Ch'ing China*. Berkeley: University of California Press, 1984.

While focused on the Qing period, Zelin's study of fiscal rationalization provides important comparative context for understanding Ming reform efforts. Her analysis of how the Qing secret memorial system enabled more effective fiscal oversight offers contrast to the Ming system's reliance on routine memorials and public debate. For understanding Ming fiscal reform limitations, Zelin illuminates why Zhang Juzheng's reforms ultimately failed to achieve lasting institutional transformation despite their initial successes.

### Chinese Language Scholarship

Chen Jinghe 陳荊和. *Mingdai Zhongguo yu Feilübin zhi Guanxi* [Relations between Ming China and the Philippines]. PhD dissertation, Tokyo University, 1962.

Chen's dissertation provides comprehensive documentation of Sino-Philippine relations during the Ming period, including detailed analysis of the Lin Feng crisis of 1574-1575. The work draws on both Chinese and Spanish archival sources to reconstruct the multinational dimensions of the Manila trade. For Minnan fiscal history, Chen documents how overseas commercial expansion created new fiscal opportunities—and new security challenges—that Liu Yaohui's maritime customs system sought to address.

Huang Fengxiang 黃鳳翔 (historian). Studies on wokou pre-history and maritime violence in the Fujian coast.

Huang Fengxiang's historical scholarship on the origins and development of the wokou crisis provides essential background for understanding the fiscal-military pressures that drove reform. His work traces the evolution of piracy from scattered coastal raiding to organized maritime confederations capable of challenging Ming military forces.

Lin Renchuan 林仁川. *Mingmo Qingchu Siren Haishang Maoyi* [Private Maritime Trade in the Late Ming and Early Qing]. Shanghai: Huadong Shifan Daxue Chubanshe, 1987.

Lin's study of private maritime trade provides crucial documentation of how Fujian's commercial networks operated across the Ming-Qing transition. His work on Yuegang geography and trade patterns illuminates how the institutional foundations established under Liu Yaohui and subsequent reformers shaped long-term commercial development.

Liu Zhiwei 刘志伟. *Zai Guojia yu Shehui Zhijian: Ming Qing Guangdong Diqu Lijia Fuyi Zhidu yu Xiangcun Shehui* [Between State and Society: The Lijia Corvée System and Rural Society in Ming-Qing Guangdong]. Beijing: Beijing Shifan Daxue Chubanshe, 2021.

Liu's comprehensive study of the lijia system in Guangdong provides essential comparative context for understanding Fujian's fiscal institutions. His analysis of how registration systems shaped rural social organization illuminates the mechanisms through which Minnan's commercial economy both relied upon and transformed Ming administrative categories.

Tang Jianmin 唐建民. "Judao Lin Feng" [The Great Pirate Lin Feng]. Unpublished research article.

Tang's article provides detailed reconstruction of Lin Feng's pirate career based on archival sources including the *Dufu Shouyi* manuscript. Tang Jianmin 唐建民. "Guanyu Lin Feng" [Regarding Lin Feng]. Unpublished research article.

This companion piece to Tang's main Lin Feng study provides chronological analysis of the pirate's movements and the Ming military response. The work illuminates operational details of coastal defense that official chronicles elide.
