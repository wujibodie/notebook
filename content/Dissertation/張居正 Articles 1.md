9.7: 高拱，張居正的重商思想

* Cites a letter by [Zhou Chen] (於行在戶部諸公書, included in the 明經世文編): “耕稼勸，則農業崇，而棄本逐末者不得縱。由是賦稅**可均**而國用可足.” Wei sees this as a summation of the central goals of the “抑商” policy.
* Describes Zhang’s time in retreat to Jingzhou as the period in which he formulated his beliefs about the need to encourage merchants and restrict/simplify taxation, in part due to seeing the negative effects of overtaxing on the merchants of his home district. Extensively cites an essay Zhang wrote at this time, the 贈水部周漢浦榷竣還朝序. The key argument of this essay is that farmers and merchants mutually support one another, and that the goal of the state should thus be to maintain balance between them, not to push one down in an attempt to favor the other, or to bleed one dry to pay for short-term expenses. “故余以為欲物力不屈，則莫若省征發，以厚農而資商；欲民用不困，則莫若輕關市，以厚商而利民.” For Wei, the thoughts expressed in this essay formed the foundation of Zhang’s later reform efforts, and were substantially more developed than any similar ideas to be found among his contemporaries. 401
* Sees the beginning of practical fiscal reforms by the center as being signaled by a 1569 memorial by Gao Gong, in which he claimed that the the merchants of the capital had been reduced to misery by the outdated valuations which the court gave for the goods it requisitioned from them, often paying less than half of market value, the solution to which, Gao believed, was monetary reform to stabilize and rebalance prices. 403

張瑞威 - 足國與富民

* An under-discussed element of Zhang Juzheng’s monetary policy was his experiment with provincial smelting of copper coins, in order to provide for common currency while silver was dedicated for the government.
* The core of the essay comes from von Glahn’s analysis of Zhang turning to large-scale copper minting as a solution to the problem of instability in silver prices, which for Zhang Ruiwei proves that late Ming officials had developed a way of thinking about fiscal problems which went beyond the simply “conservatism” which Huang Renyu assigned to ZJG.
	* von Glahn cites Tan Lun as one of the key influences on Zhang’s economic thinking, and ZRW analyzes Tan Lun’s 理財疏 to argue that Tan Lun was focused on encouraging the widespread use of copper coin among the common people in order to reduce overall dependency on silver; he saw the continued use of copper as the main currency as profitable for the state. Based on Tan Lun’s writings and a letter of Zhang Juzheng’s to Pang Shangpeng, ZRW suggests that both thinkers were entirely concerned with the goal of 足國, to which the ideal of 富民 was a subsidiary piece, and neither actually analyzed the problem of silver’s price instability in any depth.
	* As a result of their focus on covering government expenses, Zhang Juzheng and other officials were of course concerned to find ways to make casting of coins profitable for the state, and the idea of opening provincial mints departed from the suggestion of a Yunnan xunan yushi, 郭廷梧 that it would be more cost-effective to mint coins in that province, close to the copper mines, than to ship copper from Yunnan to Beijing while buying cowries for local use.
