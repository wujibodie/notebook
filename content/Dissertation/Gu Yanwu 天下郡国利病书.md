泉州衛屯田
- At the start of the dynasty, the Quanzhou weisuo usually had 4/10 troops on duty and 6/10 farming at a given time: 泉州屯種軍士大約以四六爲率. This should have been sufficient, but the land was mountainous and the non-native soldiers unused to conditions, so when outside rations were cut the granaries quickly became empty: 但田在叢山之中者五分之四，時軍士皆非土著，水土異宜，病死迯亡者過半，田多荒蕪。正統三年，每軍止徴餘糧六石，免徴正糧一十二石，聽其自給，而屯倉廢。As a result the garrisons quickly abandoned or sold their lands and found other means of support, and the censuses became unreliable: 計今屯種軍士田畝所入，有賠輸者，有僅輸者，求其如　國初得正糧以自贍者，十無一二矣。
- Notes that salt had become much cheaper in Ming Fujian than in the Song due to the use of brine evaporation: 宋時鹽價，斤爲錢十，貴倍之。今日價極高，不過錢二文，以曬法無柴薪費故也。
- Notes that the old silk requisitions, formerly assessed on land, have been turned to silver levied equally across the lijia populations: 異時農桑具文無實數，每疋始徴銀一兩六錢三分有奇，均里甲丁糧科之，非舊制矣。
- For some reason chooses this chapter to give a basic overview of the lijia system; think about why. He notes here that there is a lizhang equivalent called fangzhang in the cities: 其在城郭爲坊長。Implication that (at least here) urban 坊 were equivalent to 里. 
- In the 潮汐總叙, notes that of the major streams feeding into the Quanzhou coast, only the East and West streams of Tong'an are not briny near their mouths: 同安東、西溪之潮，去海道遠，水淡可耕.
- Provides a long and detailed analysis of the basic land taxation structure of Ming Quanzhou, lifted in large part from a (Wanli?) gazetteer. Need to consider why he includes this in the Quanzhou section specifically: clearly he viewed Quanzhou as an area of particularly complex land ownership, with consequences for its political horizons. Note that he reproduces the story of Peng Guoguang being the only official to properly 方田. Especially calls out the problem of “浮糧,” productive land taken off tax registers through claims of destruction in floods.
	- 。泉州府新志田土　國朝凡天下田地、山林、海塘、海蕩等，悉書其名數于籍。其田之等有二：曰官，曰民。若職田、若學田、若廢寺、若没官、若官租，皆係之官。職田者，唐制職官所分之田也。學田者，府縣以贍學校之田也。廢寺田者，寺額廢而入官者也。没官田者，籍没之家入官者也。又有原没今没之别。官租田者，籍没之田而募人耕種者也。民田苦繇役重累，官田有折觧而無繇役，故豪家操鬻田者之急，或強冒官産，或减畝合劵，乃鬻田者亦久操之，懸産不推，迄于死徙，其子孫稚弱淪亡，竟無從究詰。間或有水漂沙壓，田去産存，饔飱無資，而追呼日逼。凡此二者，不得不號𥸤于官々司憐之，“浮糧”之名所由起矣。顧又有宿奸巨蠹，入錢里胥，飛詭旁射，以濱溪濱海崩陷爲詞，名曰“浮糧”，而皆實産也，其巧詐不可勝窮。邇歲田價騰踊，人争尋丈尺寸之利，近田閒土，𡻕有墾闢，揃削山麓，填夷溝岸，而界至相隣，彼此互争。舊日水漂沙壓之地，有田主未能墾復，而他人乘間營之者，有田主墾復如舊，而未及受産，爲他人首告者，訟端繁矣。萬曆十年，　朝廷下方田之令，泉惠安、安溪二邑，官民皆難與慮始，第均攤浮糧於業户而已。晋江令彭國光勵精集事，履畝定則，分上中下派産業户隨丘叚各授一紙，彙登簿籍，每畝派官米若干，民米若干，鈔米若干，寺田之已經拭買及見在寺者，一㮣丈量灑派，計溢額一千四百八十頃八十九畝有奇，而産米亦增五分之二，即灑浮糧而均攤之，不至是也。人始謂惠安、安溪之士民有遠慮先見焉。他縣行之，亦或有遺議，德化志具言之矣。然自是浮糧除免，疆理不淆，争訟爲之稍息，而獨寺僧之與民間相告言也，則積𡻕不休云。先是嘉靖中，有大工之役，費無所出，令民間拭買寺田。逮季年，閩中中倭，兵餉匱之，撫按復議“寺在僧在”者，内將四分給僧焚修，六分抽餉，行之已久。萬曆十九年，倭警復聞，先後巡撫復相繼清查助餉，乃有司奉行太過，而奸僧騰端起釁，告訐紛紛。既准行查報，府中申詳，請將庫收官帖盡行提查，無庫收者徴銀若干，無官帖者徴銀若干，捕繋置對，夢引株連，民甚病之。尚寶卿永春人李開藻貽書方伯范公淶，其書縷縷千餘言，大略謂“寺田奉　㫖變賣，業在嘉靖中年，拭買後既經丈量，已攤入黄册，𡻕辦糧差，與民業無異。七八十年間，時事遷改，其子孫不能守，鬻之他人，庫收官帖，安得一々分折？歷年既久，安能一々存留？而有司毛舉數𦊙，展轉追求，至比於民間貼契之説，猥鄙特甚。且令胥役居爲奇貨，黔黎日困陷穽，非仁人所忍聞也”。李尚寶素楗户不與外事，亦無寺業，其書侃直諄切，范方伯以白撫院，事遂寢。
	- Gu's summary of temple land appropriation: 先是嘉靖中，有大工之役，費無所出，令民間拭買寺田。逮季年，閩中中倭，兵餉匱之，撫按復議“寺在僧在”者，内將四分給僧焚修，六分抽餉，行之已久。
	- Fuyi: commutation of Quanzhou's rice tax to silver began early on (seems to connect to a memorial by Shen Zhuo), and was universalized later, to general relief. Only the lijia corvee was experienced as a serious burden by the mid-Ming: "而獨里甲之役，有甚爲民苦者。始坊里長在官，專掌催錢糧，勾攝公事而已。其後乃以支應官府諸費，若祭祀、鄉飲、迎春等事，皆責措辦，浸淫至於雜供私饋，無名百出，一𥿄下徴，刻不容緩，加以吏皂抑索其間，里甲動至破産，此其弊與宋時等矣." Refers to Shen Zhuo's Zhengde 15 reform as "丁四糧六". 
		- The original Shen Zhuo system fell unfairly (不均) on larger families, so the 十段法 was enacted to split the assessed burden evenly across each 甲. Later, Wang Daokun raised the tax on grain to support Qi Jiguang's campaigns, thus 丁四糧八. "又謂十甲輪差，遇有本甲丁米少者，則銀少而差輕，或本甲丁米多者，則銀多而差重，未免有不均之嘆。乃又令各縣將實差丁米分爲十叚派編，其法頗稱詳明，然民困猶未甚甦息。既而都御史汪道昆以總兵戚繼光蕩㓕倭寇，實藉金革兵之力，遂調金華兵戍守，議加派軍餉，丁四糧八，奉　㫖定賦，而民又增一役矣。"
		- Pang's Yitiaobianfa is an extension of the logic of the shiduan fa: not only will "extra" taxes for a given year be equalized across a jia, but the full corvee burden of a ten-year cycle will be equalized across  families and years. Especially liberating to be freed from the treasury-guard and postal service corvee. 至萬曆𥘉年，都御史龐尚鵬始議一條鞭之法，通府州縣十𡻕中夏税秋糧存留起運額若干，綱徭兵站加銀額若干，通爲一條。其曰“綱銀”，則院司郡邑之供應，祭祀之費用，生員之試賞，舉貢進士之盤纏牌坊皆統焉。其曰“均徭銀”，則諸司之祗𠉀、隸兵、庫子、門子、倉庫夫、壇夫、齋夫、膳夫、陂夫、館夫、獄卒、斗級、弓兵、舖司兵、巡欄之役皆統焉。**即庫子、驛站之徭差**，舊時最爲民所苦者，一㮣通融均派，供其入者民，司其出者官，而奸徒之仰勒需索，無所藉手，著爲令甲，民甚以爲便。蓋賦役之法，始也煩，繼也簡，始則賦役分爲二，今則合而爲一矣。
- 上供三辦: Main problem with these tributes was that the quantities weren't clearly fixed, and responsibility rested solely with the decennially-serving lijia groups, so petty lixu could abuse taxpayers. Solved by equalization and silverization under Shen Zhuo and unnamed JJ26 reformers. 正德十五年，沈御史行“八分法”，通融各縣應辦物料，就於八分銀兩支觧本府，僉長觧買辦本色觧部交納。嘉靖二十六年，議附由帖徴銀，觧布政司支應。
- Commercial tolls: 　商税課。宋諸縣各有税務，其在城者曰“都税務”，在外者“外税務”。政和、紹定間，盈縮不常，於是有侵　追賠之弊。至濮守斗南，乃取前十年月課，逐月定額税錢十分爲率，以其一納總制庫，其一納財計司，餘八分從州納。諸縣税務𥘉建後皆罷廢，丞簿兼掌之。其税目有“遺利錢”、“賠綱錢”、“糜費頭錢”等名。　國朝洪武𥘉，府置税課司，縣置税課局，令商税三十税一，巡欄者收所税，以季終交於局官。而民間田𡈽交易，官給工本墨，令自填冩，爲之印識以照之，亦收其税。永樂七年，遣官點視，按爲定額。至正統初，詔革税課司局，鈔不及三萬貫者，有司兼領之。後貨物弛不復税，課額倚辦於巡欄，𡻕編有力人户充之。其名目有商税課、門攤課、桐油課、果木課、𧊵蜜課、窑冶課、鑄瀉課、蠣房課、麪税課、契本工墨課。

漳州府志
- Starts by quoting Sima Qian that lands south of the Jiang eat rice and fish and are poor; what distinguishes Zhangzhou from Wu, Yue, Ou, Min? When Chen (Yuanguang) conquered Zhangzhou, he instituted confused taxes, which were criticized by Zhu Xi; in the Ming Dynasty things were no better "租税相诡，不均不实之弊，今犹昔尔." Because this region's soil cannot feed its people, they rely on salt and fishing, which bring great profits but also great risks. "土之所产，既不足食其人民，往往旁趋于山海鱼盐，其为利博而害亦随之。“ For this reason Gu has decided to write a 赋役志 for Zhangzhou.
	- Note that for Quanzhou he chooses instead to write on 卫屯田; Quanzhou is defined as a problematic military border region while Zhangzhou is defined by its poverty. 
- First recapitulates that Zhangzhou's situation only permits about 2 or 3 tenths of its land to be farmed. Goes on to give a general overview of land taxation in the Ming (the logic perhaps being that Zhangzhou is an obvious misfit to the general policy): land is divided between 官田 and 民田 (which includes temple land), then graded and assessed duties based on its resources. Notes that because transporting grain to the capital is inconvenient, Fujian has long had most of its grain tax commuted. **At first 3/10s of rice was commuted, then in the Zhengde reign the yushi 沈灼 memorialized that, since guantian taxes were higher, these lands should be permitted to send half of their tax as silver, with newly added and "floating" rice quotas being fully commuted.** He also requested that the 土贡物料 and 差徭 be monetized and paid for out of the 丁米. Gu rather rushes through a series of further commutations, at the end of which (because of different valuations for a dun of rice) the mintian actually had higher tax loads than guantian. 
- Description of the 三主 system: 其受田之家后又分为三主大凢天下田民得租而输赋税于官者为租主富民不耕作而贫无业者代之耕嵗输租于産主而其馀以自赡给为佃户所在皆然不独漳一郡尔矣惟是漳民受田者徃徃惮输赋税而濳割本户米配租若干石以贱售之其买者亦利以贱得之当大造年米入户一切粮差皆其出办于是得田者坐食租税于粮差㮣无所与曰小税主其得租者但有租无田曰大租主民间买田契劵大率记田若干畆嵗带某户大租榖若干石而已民间仿效成习乆之租与税遂分为二而佃户.
	- The most disturbing part of this system for Gu is that the "大租主," after keeping most of the rent for themselves, uses part of the rest to hire people to cover their tax responsibilities; for this they simply write contracts with paper exchanges of grain, without money actually changing hands. 
- The lands which have the most confused ownership situations and lead to the most lawsuits are monastery lands 僧田; while those that lead to the fiercest fighting and even killing are the dam lands 埭田。For example, at the border of Longhai and Haichen are three sand islets at the mouth of the Jiulongjiang; these islets can be reclaimed over time and made to farmland. Local people, seeking profit, try to claim new fields by sending their taxes in advance to the magistrates, who then duly draw legal boundaries; but at times the land is not made good and these men's sons and grandsons are left with tax burdens but no land. "然而沧桑之变，或不能待，至垂白长子孙而不得田者有之." These sons then sell the land to investors who, if they successfully reclaim it, might face lawsuits from the regretful original owners. 
- As for temple lands, Zhangzhou has long been called 佛国, with some 600 temples recorded from Tang Kaiyuan on; but now they have been demolished or collected into just five temples with a total tax quota of 2320 石. Earlier in the dynasty monks had certain tax exemptions which made it attractive for people to surreptitiously register their lands as belonging to monasteries, often with fees to the monks, but this changed later on. From the Chenghua reign on temple levies have been combined together with ordinary 民田 levies. In Jiajing 27 a temporary silver levy was enacted on temple lands; in Jiajing 43, under pressures of war, Tan Lun (as xunfu) had 6/10s of temple land confiscated as 官田 in what was called the 寺租四六之法. The next year (JJ 44) Wang Daokun levied 4 fen of silver per ding and 8 per 石, adding another burden to the monks. In Longqing 2 Tu Zemin again confiscated another 6/10s of temple land and enacted a further silver levy per mou of land. Finally in LQ 4 a monk from Kaiyuan Temple, 净慧, memorialized to the throne (look for this memorial). Simultaneously the Zhangzhou Zhifu 罗青霄 asked for the 丁四米八 to be cut in half, which was approved -- except for the monks, who were levied the old rate. After this it's not clear if the monastic lands recovered or declined on the whole, but then in the Wanli 20s a new round of exactions began; the low point came in WL25 when the Xunfu 金学曾 again confiscated 6/10s of land from monasteries with over 2000 mu along with high levies, with smaller temples just being assessed a double silver levy. 
- Another way Zhangzhou families can go under is by being overburdened in their years of corvee responsibility (whether 里甲, 均徭，驿传，苗当).
- When temples were troubled, there were often people who took advantage of them through predatory loans and then placed their tenants on the land thus usurped; later, after these tenants were established, conniving monks requested the lands back from the government, using various means to pressure the new tenants (even after land may have changed hands repeatedly) away ("寺田始则缁流以丛林为传舍，巨室以常住为奇货，或乘急称贷，以子母钱入其租，则民为僧病。久之，民已成业矣，或经转移数手，而僧徒动以豪强兼并为名，今年告入官，明年告输饷，以致重征横敛，相率欲弃田而不可得，则僧又为民病"). Concluding comment: at this points monks are no different from common people 夫僧之田地粮差既与民同，则亦民尔。
	- Notable that the monks' main weapon here is calling for resurveys and emergency levies! 
	- Note that the potential for monastic lawfare existed because the various rounds of exactions never fully wiped out the monasteries as institutions, nor did they erase their long-standing claim to some of their former temple lands; so there was room for dispute around the boundaries of those lands.
- Begins his praise of the Single Whip, which can avoid the harm caused by hasty government measures to address temporary military exigencies (with for some reason monks being blamed?): 今一髡抱牍，而当道遽然其信，请清丈则听，请加赋则听，盖此一时权宜赡兵之策，庸知民间剥肤及髓，有不可言者乎？故田土、税粮、徭役诸弊，非尽奸伪而不可诘，在上者平其政而已。By reducing corvee burdens and clearly registering lands, the Single Whip can avoid some of the worst abuses above. 
- Attributes the invention of the Single Whip to 王宗沐 in his book 均书 (!), Pang Shangpeng picking it up from Wang. Quotes the Junshu: 一条鞭法者，通府州县十岁中夏税秋粮、存留起运额若干，均徭、里甲、土贡、顾募、加银额若干，通为一条，总征而均支之也。
	- Gu effusively praises the Single Whip, largely for freeing people from the burden of physically carrying their tax grain and from the various intermediaries involved; they can simply hand in their cash and sleep soundly in their own beds. 诸役钱分给主之官，承募人势不得复取赢于民，而民如限输钱讫，闭户卧，可无复追呼之扰。
- 罗青霄 appears again memorializing on the 一田三主 system. He appears to suggest that land be taxed according to its market value in silver: 此则不论田则高下，止照价银分田，则田因银为多寡，无出贴承买之烦；粮因田而办纳，无有粮无田之病矣。
	- Here Luo‘s memorial clarifies that the 4/6 rule does not fully confiscate monks' lands, but earmarks the (full?) income from that land for military purposes, while presumably leaving the monasteries with legal title to it. He defends the monks from a proposal to seize 8/10ths of land this way, arguing that this would force many of them to flee and become servants or even bandits. Some wandering monks have taken to settling on temple lands and simply cultivating them for themselves, depriving the other monks of rental income.
- Corvee: Of the four categories of corvee, the postal service is the most harmful 而驿传之为民害尢甚。In the past, people were expected to serve one week out of every five years. In the Zhengde reign there was an experiment in letting people pay silver then used to hire grooms; in the Jiajing reign this was taken up again, with the burden of paying for the grooms rotating according to the junyao rolls; but it was expensive enough to immediately ruin the families assigned to cover it. A visitor to the local post station could reduce a man to selling his wife and children. Now, by merging the corvee duties and having local governments pay for their needs directly out of their treasuries, these burdens are not senselessly placed on individual households, but spread across the whole county. With a single stroke, the anxiety of centuries has been wiped away; this beautiful law can be kept for 10,000 years. 总纲机徭站名四差银，数百年民所疾苦，一蠲除之，小民荷国恩甚厚，良法美意，虽万世守之可也。
- Local tribute 土贡: In the past this was a part of each household's decennial lijia duties. In Zhengde 15 the 八分法 commuted this to a fixed silver tax from which the relevant goods would be purchased. In Jiajing 37 the role of buying these goods on the market was moved from the *fu* qianzhang 本府佥长 to the buzhengsi (prefectural to provincial treasury?). With the Wanli Single Whip, the step of buying local goods to forward to the capital was skipped, and the levied silver was merged with the autumn tax silver. 
- Return to temples: if you ask elders of the region how the 寺租 payments came to be, they give different answers; some say land which pays rents to monks started as state donations, others say it came from private donors, yet others say it was falsely registered for tax purposes back when monks had exemptions. Gu traces the phenomenon back to families pawning the tax liability of their land, while arranging for a son in a monastery to be the nominal rentee, not anticipating that they could be squeezed away from cultivation rights later on. Later on, under military pressures, the administration hastily listened to monks who urged higher taxes on lands which were, on paper, held by the "powerful," in order to preserve their right to the "middle" layer of rents (inherited by the sangha). In some places, like Longxi Nanjing, as much as 3 or 4 tenths of land pays 僧租; how could this all be held by the rich? Gives the example of Haicheng, where land sells at over 10 liang per mu, but only produces about 7/8 石 of rice per year, of which half goes to the tenant-farmers; after the monk-rent of 1 石 7 斗 is paid, the owners only keep a little over 2 石; no one would choose this business if they weren't born and raised in the area. For the state to then demand double this income (half of total yields?) to pay the soldiers is clearly intolerable. These days, when a mu of land might be worth 7-8 liang, but its tax assessment set at over 10 liang, it is fortunate that there haven't been more cases of people fleeing their land. On the other hand, when the people are incapable of paying their taxes, the state naturally turns to the monks, who suffer in turn. Reproduces Luo's proposed reform, which suggests grading land taxes not by their (fluctuating and falsifiable) rents but by grain yields (seems to go against Luo's other proposal above?). Another proposal suggests fixing limits to the number of monks per temple and keeping accurate records of which rents accrue to monks and which to commoners, not allowing any change thereafter, and confiscating the land of those who hide pawned or mediated rents, as well as punishing monks who destroyed or concealed records. 
	- Above is all quoting the 癸酉志原载寺租议, from the Longqing years (it mentions Luo's proposal above as "pending approval.")
- Salt （DeepSeek): 
1. **Fiscal Hybridity in Salt Taxation**
    - **Two Systems**:
        - **盐粮 (Salt Grain Tax)**: A per-capita levy (丁口税) converted from grain → paper money (天顺) → silver (弘治). By 嘉靖, merged into the _Single Whip_ system as part of the _丁料银_.
        - **盐课 (Salt Monopoly Tax)**: Administered by merchants via state licenses (_引_), with profits split between merchants and the state.
    - **Key Point**: The shift to silver taxation (e.g., 折征银) exemplifies Ming fiscal monetization—parallel to land tax reforms you’ve studied.
2. **Regional Disparities & Informal Markets**
    - **Zhangzhou’s Exception**: Unlike state-controlled salt fields (盐塲) in northern Fujian (上里, 牛田), Zhangzhou had:
        - **No official salt fields**: Only small-scale 晒盐坵盘 (tidal salt pans) in Zhangpu, taxed per unit area (_每方一丈征银三分_).
        - **No merchant licenses (_引_)**: Salt was traded freely (从便贸易), with minimal local taxes.
    - **Contrast**: State-run systems (e.g., 西路盐) generated higher revenue but were prone to corruption (e.g., 奸商贱买贵卖).
3. **Capitalization & Labor Exploitation**
    - **Salt Pans as Capital**:
        - Non-_灶户_ (non-state salt workers) **rented pans** (转佃盐埕) from wealthy owners, paying taxes in silver.
        - Output was meager (2 _shi_/worker/day) and vulnerable to weather—resembling **marginal land tenure** in Haicheng’s reclaimed fields.
    - **Merchant Monopolies**:
        - Licensed merchants (e.g., 江瑞) colluded with officials to corner markets, forcing small producers into debt (cf. monastic land abuses).
4. **Maritime Trade Links**
    - **Coastal-Downriver Networks**:
        - Salt was shipped from 浯洲/𬇞洲 to **Haicheng** (a key port) via small boats, then upriver to inland counties (龙岩, 宁洋).
        - **Smuggling Routes**: Private salt flowed into 延平/建宁, undermining state monopolies (西路盐).
    - **Conflict**: Officials feared maritime salt trade would "blockade" licensed routes (_阻塞西路盐政_), showing tension between **local subsistence** and **state revenue**.
5. **State Overreach & Resistance**
    - **Late Ming Extraction**:
        - 万历矿税使 (eunuch tax collectors) imposed new levies on salt, worsening poverty (_边海民盐无所售_).
        - Officials like 房寰 and 闵梦得 protested that over-taxation bred rebellion (citing 卢溪/三饶 uprisings).
    - Fang's proposal 
	    - quite interesting on mutual dependence of the mountain and coast ecologies: 知漳浦县事房寰议以为“漳浦东南滨海，西北负山，附海者鱼盐，居山者耕牧，川陵险窄，舟车不通，其民或负薪米至海以易鱼盐，或持鱼盐入山以求薪米，皆彼此自为相通。山间之民，数日食无盐则病，必商人领票肩盐入山行卖，势不能家给人足。匪惟深山之民终岁无盐，而商且自病矣。海滨之民，一日不卖盐则饥，若晒盐，家必积累亿万斤，求富商大贾而后售，不惟商人力有所不能，而民先告饥矣。且奸商江瑞、武益等皆市井游手，家无儋石，投靠宦门，把持中外，既不能尽所产之盐而买之，又不能徧一邑之民而食之，徒见老弱匍匐，穷日之力，辛勤负薪米，涉山谷，易盐数斤，辄要而夺之，甚且尽其身之所有，又持盐而恐吓卖者，必倾其家而后已。今县民告诉，鼓噪号啕，若漫不加恤，必激而为乱。漳州一府，盐税不满千两，以县计之，不满百两。　朝𢌜榷其税一，而奸商罔利百倍之，奸商得其利百，而小民受害千倍之。以百两之税病百里之民，必非司国计者之意。况以已然之害而将酿必然之祸，岂为民牧者计所敢出哉?” This proposal was triggered by a Wanli 4 policy which put Zhangzhou and Quanzhou under a new salt monopoly office.
    - **Policy Debates**:
        - **Pro-Market**: 房寰 argued for free trade (_彼此自为相通_), as heavy regulation hurt mountain communities (_深山之民终岁无盐_).
        - **Pro-Control**: Salt officials (盐法道) sought to restrict private trade to protect monopoly revenue (_万金之课_).
6. Zhang and Ning: Extended discussion of the combined salt circuits of Zhangping and Ningyang, both of which are dominated by outside merchants from Longxi (前此二县之商，率皆龙溪之人，夤缘投认，根深蒂固，莫可驱除。) The official Min Mengde 闵梦德 suggests setting up customs stations at the borders between the three levels of the salt circuits, in order to share the profit instead of letting more sophisticated inland merchants squeeze the producers and peddling 澳商 ， banning Longxi merchants entirely, increasing quotas in view of manifestly increased demand, and using newly increased quotas to lower per-bag taxes.

- 屯田考: The original weisuo system of 7 active soldiers to 3 cultivators had collapsed by the Zhengde reign, as soldiers fled their duties or pawned their land to local elites; registered soldiers tended to collect rents from cultivators (similar to monks), which made them vulnerable in times of famine, especially after commutation of taxes meant there was no need to actually handle grain. Empty weisuo granaries and low morale made the system virtually useless for actual defense; state pursuit of money for salaries only worsened the hollowing of the garrisons‘ actual functions. 
	- Attempts at resurveying were stymied by the opposition of local people, with soldiers rarely present to claim their own land: 又久则坵叚换移，而屯失本来之额，粮日以逋，军日以耗，虽履丈量而不可清，悬厉禁而不可革也。时有清理屯田之议，则亡赖又得因以为利，而豪巧猾者又从而阴阳其间，公举私首，肩摩踵接，断墨未乾，侵冒如故。

- Citing the 南靖志 on the One Field Three Lords system: 王田不经清丈，区𤱔税粮原无定则，奸民乘之，欺隐日滋。间尝核通县田𤱔不下三十万，其登赋税者十五万九千有奇耳，此外皆他邑豪所踞者也。且所谓一田三主之弊，尢海内所罕者，曰“大租主”，一日“业主”，一曰“佃户”。同此田也，买主只收税榖，不供粮差，其名曰“业主”。粮差割寄他户，抽田中租配之受业而得租者，名曰“大租主”。佃户则出赀，佃田、大租、业税皆其供纳，亦各一主，此三主之说也。又有一田而载官米若干在“赵甲户”，又载民米若干在“钱乙户”，不成四主乎？且贸易相承之时，更有以租田诡为税田，而减米求售者，初不过利买者之重价，久之粮无从办，则扳告买主收米矣。买者既费高值，妆额未兑租，无赖之辈往往持此以骗富室。或租入仕宦，则不敢扳告，而岁久遂为悬粮。故有田连阡陌而户米不满斗石者，有贫无立锥而户未至数十石者。版籍既不足据，流弊因而愈甚。即贤有司若心调停，亦治其标而已。盖经界不正，自紫阳治漳，日恨之矣。 南靖土壤，东连龙泰，西接王和，南界浦澄，北抵龙岩、漳平之间，𤱔多而壤沃，视他邑颇胜。**第兵燹之后，民多流离，境内田𤱔归他邑豪右者十之七八，土著之民大都佃耕自活，其他诡其田米，每米一斗，割租榖或数斗或一石以与豪。** 得田者惮于主户当差，则又飞诡寄之家，使之代纳粮差，名为配米大租，遂有“一田三主”之说。得租者不能常守，又或减米而卖其租，遂有“虚悬”之号。讼端纷纷，多从此起。**又卖田者见昔贱而今贵，则索买者之增价，或一索，或再索，或屡索，其名曰“洗业”。** 索而不遂，则告典借，告车估，缠讼不已。又势族豪门或欲夺人之产，则使卖者告赎，而彼从中主之，不论年月久近，不顾事理可否。盖漳俗缙绅日盛，则田价日高，田价日高则趋利者日众，而官民日益多事矣。又南靖欺隐之弊，视他邑尢不可言。盖靖地广饶，豪右视为利■薮，其风俗乔朴而气势又复琐尾，豪右殊无顾忌之心，故报赋不报赋，秪听其方便何如耳。未暇远引旁证，且如乙未筑城，计𤱔出费，本县但令约保各自报其约内田𤱔，旬月之间报𤱔二十四万三千七百有奇，查其登版征赋者，不过一十五万九千一百有奇耳。况访得势豪之田，约保所不敢报者尚十之三四也，犹为有法手。尝窃计之，向使南靖之田地得如他处之清丈，不数乾没于权豪，则不必如今之七科八科，即上者五升科之，次者三升科之，下者二升科之，可以岁足一万七千之额，官课裕而民困亦苏。惜租米为梗，影单百端，势日𫐖𮝺而不可，此紫阳所以有“遗恨”也。
    - Nanjing is a fertile county upriver from Zhangzhou city, which has made it attractive to outside land investors who become "大租主" and remove the land from local taxpayer rolls. The county's overall tax burden thus falls heavily on the remaining smallholders. Also notes that friction has arisen because of rising land prices, with former sellers pushing for compensation. As the newly minted "俗缙绅" see prices rising, they flock to make a profit, creating a typical real estate bubble. （盖漳俗缙绅日盛，则田价日高，田价日高则趋利者日众，而官民日益多事矣。）The obvious solution is a comprehensive land resurvey, but the local surveyors are overawed by the powerful gentry landholders.
    - **欺隱日滋**: 50% or so of land untaxed (30万田 vs. 15.9万登赋)
    - **他邑豪踞**: 70-80% of Nanjing land held by **outsider elites** (他邑豪右) while locals reduced to **tenant farmers** (佃耕自活) victimized by transformation of fertile land to a speculative asset. 
	    - The passage attributes the start of this phenomenon to military troubles, which caused many local people to sell or abandon their land, giving an opening to 他邑豪右.
