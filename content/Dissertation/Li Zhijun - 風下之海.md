Intro
- Proposes that Minnan, uniquely among Chinese regions, experienced processes of both "interiorization" 內向化 and "exteriorization" 外向化: the first being the conscious tendency to "de-barbarize" (脫蠻意識) and assimilate to (a version of) Zhongyuan norms, the latter referring to the center of economic gravity shifting outside the boundaries of the Chinese state. 
- Focuses on the relevance for a subtropical monsoon region of the "little ice age" of the Ming-Qing transition: the boundary of the "tropics" as defined by the limits of annual frost and (by extension) viability of plants like lychee shifts by about 2 degrees latitude from north of Fuzhou to south of it (during this period snow was reported many years in Fuzhou). Further, the typhoons became weaker, especially in El Nino years.
- Points out that even in ancient China the basic precipitation cycle and lunar-tidal connection were understood. Importance of tidal knowledge: Minnan gazetteers routinely include local tide charts. 12-13 (Recall also military discussions of tides in Taiwan Waiji.)
	- Zhang Xie described three main navigational tools: the compass ( 指南針), wooden markers to measure speed/distance ("測更法")，and anchors to check depth (重錘法).
- Discussion of the Jiulongjiang delta focuses on two related topics: problems of flooding (and shi-class explanations thereof), and the sedimentation of new sand flats, leading to “與海爭田，“ "以海為田" strategies (as well as as simple 與人爭田 conflicts). 19
- Basic geographic setting: the Jiulongjiang mouth ports of 浦頭，石碼，海澄，廈門, and the broader Taiwan Strait region, defined as the narrow channel where currents, winds, and clouds are squeezed between the Wuyi and Taiwan mountain ranges, with Penghu in the middle of these currents. 
- In his study of the Lin Shuangwen rebellion on Taiwan (1786), focuses on the logistical networks called on by Qing forces, especially the provision of grain from the Yangtze for both troops on Taiwan and the staging base (and the rebels' home region) of Minnan. 

Chapter One
- Defining Minnan: In the Song and Ming, "Minnan" could include all of Fujian, or all of coastal Fujian, e.g. 蔡獻臣 wrote: "夫閩南福，興，泉，漳四郡，其地濱海，其山海多而田地少，故餬口必資與糴粵，而生計必籍與販洋." Xie Zhaozhe, for one, identified his home of Fuzhou as "Minnan." 3-4
- Cultural 內向化: defined as the "脫蠻意識" and desire to approximate the "center." Notes survivals in the Ming of memories of She people becoming literate, as in a Minshu account of one 袞, a Tang-era She literatus.  8
