Officials
In Hongzhi 10, a Xunfu position was established over the border region between Tingzhou, Zhangzhou, Chaozhou, southern Jiangxi, and a bit of southern Huguang, called 提督南赣汀漳抚臣. It was abolished in JJ 45, when Chaozhou was returned to Guangdong oversight and Zhangzhou to Fujian, though Tingzhou stayed under the Nangan xunfu. Wang Yangming held this position.

巡視撫臣
Complicated set of institutional rearrangements between JJ26-36; at first Zhejiang and Fujian are put under a common 巡視撫臣, then Nan Zhili is added under a zongdu (Zhu Wan), and a subordinate 都御史 position for just Zhejiang and coastal Fujian is created in 33. Coastal Fujian is separated under its own yushi in 35, but still subordinate to the zongdu. Finally in 36 a xunfu is given authority over all of Fujian to deal with repeated piracy (以倭連歲寇東南) and this becomes permanent thereafter (1123).
- First official in this role is Zhu Wan. Zhu had first been the Nangan xunfu in JJ 25, which gave him authority over Ting/Zhangzhou. He was then moved to the new position of Censor for Zhejiang and maritime Fujian; he immediately strictly prohibited foreign trade and and burned all ships with more than two masts. He defeated "山寇" from Tong'an. Later, while going from Wenzhou to Funing, he captured Franks and other foreigners (white and black, and Chinese "nakhodas" (? not *fan* but *zei* ): 擒佛郎名王及黑白諸番喇噠諸賊甚衆。Zhu was thereupon accused of killing without due process; in the investigations, reporting censors Du Ruzhen and Chen Zongkui noted that the Malaccan *fan* had for some time been contacting ne'er-do-wells, but it was first in JJ 27 that they reached Yuegang and Wuyu. They anchored there and traded with the locals, while officials were bribed to turn a blind eye. "功過未明，竟優恐仰藥，公論惜之。"

巡撫 (提督軍務巡撫福建地方都察院御史)：Established in JJ 36, at first subordinate to the 巡視撫臣 of Zhejiang until that role was abolished, thereafter subordinate only to the throne.
- Ruan E 阮鶚: First person in the position of xunfu. Previously the xunfu of Zhejiang. "福建有巡撫自鶚始". Facing "tens of thousands" of pirates with insufficient funds and poor soldiers, he nonetheless fought bravely until his recall due to rumors of impropriety. 
- Liu Tao: Known for riding and shooting prowess; supposedly shot several bandit leaders to death personally. However, he fell ill and had to retire before fully extirpating the bandits, and the people of Fujian remembered him fondly. 
- Tan Lun: The first to be named Xunfu of the full province without a superior, in response to the wokou capture of Xinghua. 
- Wang Daokun: Spent a total of five years as a military official in Fujian. As a strategist he was primarily concerned with funding: 其畫軍事，首議餉，次議兵，三議責成，四議事任。Notes that he defeated Wu Ping among a few others.
- Tu Zemin: No comment.
- He Kuan: 舊列，賦入之外，丁取錢四，糧取錢八，以供軍。至寬，始蠲其十之六。Also attempted to implement higher standards for military recruits' fitness and inspection of corruption among the clerks.
- Yin Congjian: Following new wokou attacks (Zeng Yiben?), re-implemented military surcharges, the dingsi liangliu. (Not sure if this would be on top of the previously reduced Tan-era charges, or if he merely raised the schedule a bit.) However, he attempted to economize as much as possible, including in his own habits, not wearing elegant clothing or eating meat. He died in office and was found to have no wealth besides the 10 taels of his monthly salary; the people mourned him and built a temple at the West Lake of Fuzhou. 
- Liu Yaohui: "林鳳劫掠濱海郡邑，堯誨設方略合番漢兵攻之，遂有東番魍港，呂宋國玳瑁港二捷。策南澳一島介漳潮間，賊往往憑之爲患，奏設總兵居守。" Also requested to keep salt taxes in the province for military needs. As a result managed to create surpluses: "於是，軍有餘錢，倉有餘待。"
- Pang Shangpeng: Served as qiandu yushi under Yin Congjian. When he became Xunfu, the province was peaceful and the treasury had a surplus, so he removed certain military surcharges and enacted the simplifying measures of the Single Whip. HQY claims that when he opposed Zhang on the duoqing issue, he was removed in an audit which found minor discrepancies in the provinces' accounts and told to return home to await reassignment, which never came. 
- Liu Siwen: In his brief time as Xunfu, was apparently the person who memorialized requesting a qingzhang survey, due to finding tax rolls in disarray. 
- Geng Dingxiang: Says very little about his time in Fujian, except that he was known for "jiangxue," and while in Fujian he was more interested in lecturing than governing. 
- Xu Xueju: During a famine, Xu called together merchants and encouraged them to sell their wares, which piled up along the rivers, such that the people were not harmed by the famine. (Sounds like the story about Fan Zhongyan.) Proof of functioning food markets' role in political legitimacy in Minnan.

### [Page 28]

**風俗** - really has heavy focus on local economy & agronomy.

- Frequently points out women's role in work, as in Fuzhou: "閩則長婦并力而合作，幾多於男。"
    
- 古田 is a poor, mountainous district with bad soil, leading the men to hire themselves out as servants: "壯者往往傭四方，鄙朴而勤力。" The lack of merchants and artisans means silver is hard to come by, so to pay their taxes they have to sell rice at the port: "四民之中，工商闕二，里無所獲金鏹銀。輸賦之至，必運米出易於水口。"
    
- Mingqing is also mountainous, but the hills are more fertile due to springwater.
    
- 長樂: Coastal, sells fish, salt, and fruits to 四方, especially lychee & longan. "業俗儉而因怠"
    
- 福清: This coastal district earns wealth from trade (頗有海舶之利), which is invested in education: "學不遂則棄之習文法吏事，不則行賈於四方矣，比其財饒倍邑。"
    
- The mutually beneficial relationship between commerce and learning.
    
- Quanzhou: The fields are fertile and the lychees and longans are dried and sold all over 天下. The people rely more on island fisheries than rice farming. The mountain-dwellers boil sugar for sale across 天下. "地狹人稠，行賈客出疆，仰粟於外，上吳越而下廣東。"
    
- Their artisans are not the best in anything, but can imitate textiles from anywhere, even 國初龍袞. Notes that even among educated/official families, women will sit bare-faced selling things in the market; this is because "濱海小民，非是無所得食。"
    
- Long, praiseful description of Anping, where 入海為貿易 is no barrier to widespread education "是以縉紳先生翁盛於中原。"
    
- Nan'an: Fertile and scenic, with virtuous people who 皆有鄒魯之風.
    
- Hui'an: The soil is relatively abundant and fertile, and gives 2 harvests: 3/4 of land are rice, and on the rest they double-crop wheat and sweet potatoes. Sells livestock to neighboring regions.
    
- Dehua: Mountainous and cold, with just enough produce & fish to sustain itself but not to sell elsewhere; coastal products and salt have to be bought from Jinjiang, so are triple the price here.
    
- Anxi: Relatively fertile, so produces surplus grain for the 郡中 (but no further). Uses animal bone to meal as fertilizer. Their merchants go to 永安 or 古田 and bring back 盬鹽.
    
- Tong'an: "朱文公之所過化也，其韻句尚存。" Notes that bamboo tools are sold from "佳禾之嶼", surely 嘉禾.
    
- Yongchun: Cold and rugged but fertile, exports grain to Jinjiang. Notes that violent feuding is common in the west of the county, especially in protection of female virginity.
    
- Putian: enthusiastic praise for this 莆田, which has produced numerous high officials and scholars, where 3-4/10ths of men can read poetry, the merchants of which travel all over the empire (especially Jinling), and the crafts of which fill Fujian.
    
- Longxi: leads with terrifying reputation: "閩中諸郡，惟漳最悍剽。" They gather in large lineages and trade abroad: "族大之家，猶或數十，賴多入海通夷。" The less law-abiding go abroad to 奸蘭 or mint illegally 鼓鑄. Notes that Zhangzhou people like to 交合 with Quanzhou, and Quanzhouese is the prestige dialect preferred in plays etc.

**39 版籍志**

- 戶口: In Tang times, Mingzhou, as part of 揚州, was taxed in coin. Under the 偽閩, men between 16 & 60 were assessed a capitation tax in coin, but in Zhang-Quan this could be commuted to rice (!). When Zhenzong offered tax relief for the poll tax to the south, Xinghua, Quanzhou, and Zhangzhou were left out, as they paid in rice.
    
- Suggests that much of the loss in 戶 during the Ming came from attrition of military households, who at first had half as many 戶 as 民戶 despite being 1/3 as many 口. Notes that after Shen Zhuo's Zhengde 14 八分法, only the 差役 was still assessed decennially thru lijia. Notes that unregistered 丁 are called 私丁, and are normally counted and fined by yamen clerks (source of funds for local bureaucracies?)
    
- Statement on the falseness of the Yellow Registers: "有可編審之時，卒視奉行署量誆丁口，皆非實數矣。" However, in ancient times corrupt rulers/ministers sometimes falsely added people to the census to increase taxes, which was also bad. "夫如是也，何必實數，何必不實數哉？" 958-959
    
- Hongwu 26: 戶, 815,528. 口, 3,916,806.
    
- Hongzhi 4: 戶, 506,039. 口, 2,106,060.
    
- Wanli 6: 戶, 515,307. 口, 1,738,793.
    
- 賦役: The 偽閩 divided Fujian's land in three grades, giving the best to 僧道, second to natives, third to immigrants. The Min's taxes were heavy, so in Song 太平興國, officials were sent to 均福建田稅. The Song went back and forth between corvee and monetized hiring of substitutes. Over time the overlapping systems became confused and the censuses lost touch with rural reality, as decried by Zhu Xi, who called for a comprehensive land resurvey in Quan, Zhang, Tingzhou, in order to "酌產均稅".
- In the Ming, from early on Fujian commuted half its 官米 and 3/10ths of its 民米 to silver for transport to Beijing (the rest being kept locally). Shen Zhuo added fixed 耗米 to both 官 & 民 taxes to cover costs & shortages. 961
- Notes that after Shen Zhuo's reforms, the tax burden on 官田 became less than that on 民田. 962. This led to mass transfer of land to 官田 households that could bring it under 官田. This was the beginning of the 飛灑 & 浮糧 problems.
- At first the lijia was only intended for monetary payments and handling of local affairs, but it became the main support for county/prefectural administrations, leading to arbitrary charges, corruption, bullying, and an overall insupportable burden. This is why Shen Zhuo wanted to implement a simple silver tax for provincial admin.
- Goes through the various ills of the lijia as of the Jiajing reign, diagnosed by Zhang Juzheng as stemming from poor land surveys: "賦法弊矣，說者謂宜度畝方田。而萬曆初年，江陵相始議行之，相度高下墝腴，定為上中下三則，折為實畝。" As for the 役法, the categories began to be combined and levied evenly as annual fees in the late Jiajing years, with the 十段法. Qi Jiguang and Wang Daokun added new silver levies to pay for Qi's army.
- Finally, the 一條鞭法 eliminated the distinctions among 戶 entirely. This was actually Zhu Xi's initial intention ("其法蓋即朱文公輸納產錢分錙銖元彀之意" - it was carried out across the empire by Pang Shangpeng (but no specific mention of his time in Fujian).
    -> "法行而民若以息肩。蓋賦役之法，始甚煩，繼也簡；始則賦役分為二，今則合為一矣。" 964
    
- 土田: Drop from 146,000 頃 in the Hongwu census to 139,000 in Wanli 6.
    
- 屯田: By the Hongzhi years there were clearly not enough farmers in the 軍戶 to keep the garrisons self-sufficient, so attempts were made to impress new 屯田 tenants, but to little avail. The Wanli 10 清丈 was stymied by 豪民 who had been using 屯田. "蓋國初屯法至此壞盡矣。" The attempt to resurvey the garrison lands would first require restoring the old garrison armies, which was impossible: "然揆田畝迷失之由，軍士消乏之故，則欲行清屯，必先清軍；軍政既弛，即欲復國初屯種之舊，亦不可得，所謂其弊也。"
    
- Sees collapse of the tun, their merger into private land, as inevitable outgrowth of population growth and rising cost of land. In any case, as the soldiers themselves(?) sold or rented their land, and the people working it are merely replaceable tenants, there is little hope of tracking down the "real" owners and seizing it back.
    
- 寺田: 偽閩 gave the best land to temples. In early Wanli, tax schedules for 民, 官, 寺田 were unified. The Jiajing era went too far in "scraping gold off the Buddha's face".
    

**雜課:**

- 魚課 was fully commuted to silver in Hongzhi 7. 龔文莊 has a 記
    
- 市舶稅: In the Song, foreigners came to Quanzhou to trade; 大食 had the most valuable cargoes, but was distant and its ships came rarely. 三佛齊 often brought 大食 goods, as well as its own spices and pearls (Srivijaya). Lists all the major importers. Notes that 大食 is the early Ming 爪哇 and Tang 訶陵國; they are thought to now trade via Luzon.
    
- Ends by noting that the 雜課 were largely abolished with the Single Whip and replaced with silver charges for military expenditures. 977
    
    -> Similarly, all tributes were replaced with silver payments in Longqing 3 (He Kuan?). 979
    
- He's overall conclusion: Taxes of the early Ming were far harsher than in ancient times. The Single Whip improved things, in the empire as a whole and not Fujian in particular. (海內以為大便，不特閩中。) However, by He's time tax officials had once again become abusive, including towards privileged classes (~~歷中考降~~ 歲貢 serving as village tax-collectors in absence of a strong local gentry. (Though the fresh jinshi appointees ~~patron client bonds~~ of major county seats are even worse!)
    
    -> He seems to criticize the examined scholar-officials, who are loyal to central metrics of revenue collection, and favor a healthy stratum of local gentry to defend local society against rapacious taxation. Views the state's need for funds as inherently conflictual with local interests. Specifically critiques additions to quotas "增加額" - old censuses must be defended!
    
- Zan: Poem arguing that the goal of good government should be increasing the population, which rests on providing enough food; government should refocus on letting the people grow and prosper rather than harming them.
