**封疆大吏與社會變革**

* Pang Shangpeng was born in Nanhai in 1524 to a family of fairly educated wood merchants. He was educated after 20 in Shunde, with his most important teacher being the Jiangxi-born Chen Yong 陳雍. He completed the palace examination at 239th place in the 3rd rank in 1553, at 29, with Xu Jie as one of his two chief examiners, and Zhang Juzheng (although one year younger!) as another examiner; "尚鵬與居正之間，從此便也有了師生之誼." 11
* After a successful period as Zhixian of Leping county in Jiangxi, Pang was promoted (relatively early in his career) to xunan yushi of Zhejiang. His time there was interrupted by the death of his father in late 1561, and when he returned to service from his mourning period, the Grand Secretariat had come into the hands of Xu Jie's "faction," and Pang was quickly reinstated as xunan yushi of Zhejiang, which role he took up in summer of 1565.
* First two _fu_ (in all of China, or just counting Zhejiang?) to start combining and silverizing corvee and in-kind taxes were Huzhou 湖州 and Jiaxing. 21
	* Huzhou: In 1541, an official named Zhang Duo 張鐸 combined _guantian_ and _mintian_ into a single payment scheme (i.e. eliminated differential tax rates) to make it clearer what was owed on each piece of land.
	* Jiaxing: In 1547, the official Zhao Ying 趙瀛，following the reforms of one Wang Yi 王億 in Suzhou, also eliminated the division between payments on _guantian_ and _mintian,_ as well as combining miscellaneous services (such as transport of tax grain) into silver payments which went to hiring of professional labor.
* General trend for tax equalization to be protagonized by 知縣，知府, and then implemented more broadly by xunan, xunfu. On the case of Shaoxing Yuyao's 周鳴: “君至，遂白於撫安守巡，群公并可之" (20).
* Quote from **劉志偉，《在國家與社會之間》**：“換言之，與其說均平是里甲正役的貨幣化，還不如說是由附加在里甲正役之上的法外科斂產生出來的新稅種。”  30
* He Qiaoyuan wrote a biography of Pang Shangpeng in his Mingshancang. Mentions the first predecessor for the _junpingfa_ in Zhejiang was one 朱英, who established a "兩役法," according to which each _lizhang_ was responsible for 2 all-_li_ events during his 10-year term: in the fifth year, he was to forward money collected for local government expenses, called the 里首錢; and in the 10th year he brought his people together to the local _縣廷_ to "審諸役，曰均徭." Since Zhu Ying was _yushi_ in the Zhengtong and Jingtai reigns, then if he really enacted this policy, rudiments of the _junpingfa_ began to exist in Zhejiang from as early as the 1430s-50s.
* Pang Shangpeng's 1566 memorial describing his favored reforms:《節冗費定法守以更里甲疏》
	* In his first lines, he once again refers to the _wokou_ problems as the root of Zhejiang's financial difficulties: "兩浙自兵興以來，公家之賦稅日繁，閭閻之困苦已機."
	* Pang Shangpeng's goal in petitioning to the court was to ensure that his reforms would not be undone by his predecessors; the resulting imperial proclamation not only declared the reforms permanent, but ordered them to be extended to every fu, zhou, and xian in Zhejiang. (35-37)
* A Kangxi era Shaoxing gazetteer summarized the reforms made during Pang's time in a relatively easy to understand way (38-40). First, a yearly account should be made of the following year's expected expenses, while a survey should be done adjusting the number of _ding_ (with land being "converted" to _ding_) for each _hu_ so that each _li_ and _jia_'s fair share of the calculated annual expenses could be fixed. By focusing on _ding_ and demanding payment in silver, it theoretically reduced opportunity for inequalities in payment, while the need for accounting prevented officials from inventing spurious charges as the year went on. He also ordered exact requirements of 吏役 and 夫馬 to be spelled out in advance and posted at the office of the officials responsible, and provision of food be made to recompense levied laborers. However, over time this well-intentioned system fell into disuse as higher officials failed to inspect accounts often enough to enforce it.
	* Deng's summary: “不難看出，龐尚鵬的均平法改革內容主要包括以下幾個方面：一是折田為丁，以丁銀為基本計算單位，量出為入，按年審編，以便家喻戶曉；二是刊刻由貼，里甲執由貼交納均平銀兩，“投櫃封鎖，記薄存照," 以杜絕舞弊；三是官為買班，；里甲供應通行載革；四是官府支用，例為會計，嚴禁法外加派，違者重治."
	* To Deng, these reforms both carried the spirit of the _yitiaobian fa_ and were an important predecessor to Pang's own efforts to implement that reform in Zhejiang.
* 十段錦法: Pang's earliest _fuyi_ reform in Zhejiang, undertaken in the first month of his arrival in 1565. Also called 提編均徭. The Ming Shi description: "其法，以銀力差排編十甲，如一甲不足，則提下甲補之，故謂之提編" (42). The term and basic reform were not created by Pang; Liang Fangzhong says that it began to appear in Zhejiang and Fujian after 1552, as a way to pay for military expenses due to the wokou problem. At the time, local officials were aware that the additional silver taxes (levied on land) were a substantial burden to the populace, but considered them necessary to deal with the _wokou_.
	* Pang wrote in his 厘宿弊以均賦役疏: "切惟民間大患，莫甚於賦役之不均；賦役之不均，實由於優免之太濫." The goal of his reform was to make each jia of each li responsible for equivalent amounts of taxes, with failure of one jia to pay passing the responsibility to their neighbors.
	* 優免: Exemptions
	* 厘: 整理; 宿弊: Long-standing/habitual corruption
* 均徭法 and the Single Whip: The purpose of the 均徭 was simply to equalize 差役 requirements. The phrase dated to the Tianshun reign, and Zhejiang was supposed to have implemented a reform by this name during the Chenghua reign (~early 1470s); however, He Qiaoyuan attributes a “兩役法" which he says was also called _junyao_ to one Zhu Ying, who was _yushi_ of Zhejiang in the Zhengtong/Jingtai reigns. However, implementation was uneven and local officials seem to have added frequent extra expenses (e.g. the 火耗). The _yitiaobian fa_ was supposed to have revived and fixed this earlier reform.
	* A candidate for earliest person to bring the phrase 條鞭 to Zhejiang was one 張振之, who was _zhifu_ of 處州 (modern 麗水市) around 1560. Zhang wrote a 《全浙條鞭》which is now lost.
	* In 1561, the _zhixian_ of Pinghu in Shaoxing, 顧廷對，implemented a "均徭" reform which involved registering total labor levy requirements, converting their value to silver, and then levying this silver amount equally by land and men. Pang Shangpeng arrived for his first circuit in Zhejiang around the same time, and the Tianqi-era Pinghu gazetteer credits Gu Tingdui with inspiring Pang's later spreading of this reform across Zhejiang. 46-47
	* However, a Guangxu-era gazetteer claims that Zhang in turn learned about this reform from Suzhou, even requesting a copy of the Suzhou tax registers to learn how to copy their methods.
	* "和原來的所謂“均徭”法相比，顧氏的條鞭法比較突出的有兩點：一為定額化，一為徭役征銀，“從縣田丁糧內均派帶征，”即以丁糧為標準征收役銀."48
	* The major difference between Zhang Zhenzhi's reform and Pang Shangpeng's later _yitiaobian fa_ was that Zhang still had taxpayers themselves bring their payments to government offices rather than sending official tax collectors; but this latter element was already present in Yuyao (Shaoxing) by 1564.
	* In both Pang Shangpeng's own writings and in later gazetteers, Pang's experience inspecting Shaoxing appears to be the largest influence on his eventual decision to implement the _yitiaobian fa_ more broadly (and to call for its extension to other southern provinces); the package of reforms he championed was particularly similar to that found in Yuyao (49-53).
	* Wang Shizhen's tombstone inscription for Zhang Zhenzhi: "授處州推官，甫至而守入覲，丞，媯俱闕，公遂行守事。孳孳問民瘼吏弊而參伍法，比公廳蕭然，大猾載三尺，而治條鞭之法最為便，單弱郡邑相顧莫敢先，公毅然行之。有盧尚書貴甚，族子憑籍為奸私，莫敢呵問，公捕而立至於理。" Deng Zhihua glosses this inscription as saying that Zhang Zhenzhi did not introduce new ideas about how to reform taxes, but rather was the first official with the bravery to stand up to the locally powerful and pass common-sense reforms to benefit the common people. 52
* Five reasons given by Pang Shangpeng why the _yitiaobian fa_ benefits the people: 1. It prevents inequalities, bankruptcies, and shortfalls caused by unevenly onerous levies and inaccurate tax registers; 2. It makes it possible to know one's exact tax burden well in advance; 3. In the past, tax exemptions were frequently granted, and this was taken advantage of by the wealthy, who split up their land into many small parcels registered to poor tenants or children in order to claim exemptions, which practice was to be prevented by thorough surveys and a decennial resurvey, along with limits on exemptions; 4. In the past, because some labor services were considered easier and others harder, it was easy for minor officials to extort bribes to change taxpayers' obligations, but the transition from differentiated labor services to uniform silver payments, along with the removal of tax registers from the hands of the _lizhang,_ would remove this source of corruption; 5. the older system differentiated between those with much land, who had heavier overall payments, and those with little or no land, who were more often forced to travel far to labor in person, while the new system removed this distinction + resultant habitual inequalities, in part by (almost) entirely getting rid of the need to travel for labor service.
	* Deng quotes a later source saying that Pang's reforms did not succeed on at least count 3, as rich households still found ways to hide their landholding, but it seems to have been a well-received measure anyways.

Chapter 5: 巡撫福建：龐尚鵬與張居正

* After 6 years out of office, in 1576 Pang Shangpeng was restored to government service by Zhang Juzheng and immediately made xunfu of Fujian, with a mandate to implement the _yitiaobian fa_ there. After one and a half years, Pang was again cashiered in 1578, this time (according to the 明史) because he wrote in support of officials who had been punished for criticizing Zhang's exemption from the mandatory mourning period for his parents.
* Deng spends a while excerpting Zhang's letters to Pang's predecessors on Fujian's _wokou_ issue. In one from 1573, Zhang criticizes Yu Dayou as a 老奸, while a 1575 letter informs Liu Yaohui to be ready to capture remnants of Lin Feng's fleet after a defeat in Guangdong.
* The _wokou_ were seen as adding to Fujian's confused and unequal tax situation which dated back to the Hongzhi reign or earlier (144).
	* According to one Cai Qing 蔡清 (Quanzhou, 1453-1508)
* In 1565, the Fujian _xunfu_ Wang Daokun 汪道昆 levied silver charges on the population to pay for soldiers' pay: “民間每丁征銀四分，米一石征銀八分." Deng says there were already precedents for this in Fujian, and the 丁四米八 merely represented an increase.
* Regarding the efforts needed to break through opposition to the Single Whip, Deng mentions that while Pang was in Zhejiang, he had to impeach a _zhixian_ who refused to enact reforms, while Pan Jixun made arrests of powerful opponents at the very beginning of his time as _xun'an_ of Guangdong. 148
	* Deng says Pan's 均平里甲法 “其實即是條鞭法的前身")
* There were powerful critiques made of the _yitiaobian fa_ on ideological grounds in the Longqing reign, especially on the grounds that it "suppressed the root and benefitted the branch (抑本利末)" in the words of Zhu Ruyan. One influential critic was the 戶部尚書 official 葛守禮, whose 寬農民以重根本疏 made two basic arguments: first, that the _yitiaobian fa_ would benefit the south while being inappropriate for the economic conditions of the north; and second, that it would benefit artisans and merchants while being harmful to farmers. The Longqing emperor personally approved Ge's memorial on the matter. Xu Jie was also a notoriously firm opponent of both land resurveying and the single whip. 148-149.
* Cites a Wanli 4 letter to Xiang Cheng (Huguang _xunan_) in which Zhang says that the single whip reform is particularly helpful to the south rather than the north. 149
* Pang Shangpeng was recommended for office a large number of times during his six-year forced retirement (150), and was in close contact with a "密友" of Zhang Juzheng's, 李幼滋. "以李氏與居正之關係，尚鵬在家之動向，居正當能聞知。但這是否就於後來居正起用尚鵬有直接關係，尚不得而知，有待今後考證..." 151
