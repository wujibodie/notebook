Intro
- Begins with a survey of the anti-maritime trade policies of the Ming and Qing; considers everything to have been continuous with the precedents set by Zhu Yuanzhang. 
- "明朝政府对海阳管理往往并没有即成的政策，而是处于不断的摸索和调整之中，体现出对客观性史的适应。" 2 
	- Following the wokou troubles of the mid-late Ming, coastal provinces were increasingly empowered to set their own maritime policies; in practice this tended to put power in the hands of "family and place-based connections." 
- Of Fujian's "three circuits and five bastions" (三路五寨), the most important was Zhao'an county's Tongshan fortress, which was strengthened by the 1530 establishment of Zhao'an county and by Yu Dayou's repeated arguments for investment in its defenses. 2-3

Chapter 2 
- Argues that Zhao'an, being at the intersection of Southeast Asian and Japanese trade routes, as well as the border of Guangdong and Fujian, was for a time the "center of China's maritime trade" 
- Extensively quotes the Chouhai Tubian, 福建事宜 section
- Very interesting quotes from Yu, Zhengqi Tang Ji, on 115-116, describing the opulence of merchant communities along Zhao'an Bay; Yu takes a rather hostile tone towards them. Notes that 男不耕田，而食必粱肉；女不蠶紙，而衣必錦綺，莫非自通番接濟爲盜行劫中得來。
- Zhao'an was a major center for piracy and banditry as people passed over the Fu-Guang border; Wang claims that Wu Ping and Zeng Yiben were Zhao'an people. 116
- Quotes a Tianxia Junguo Libing Shu passage about Wu Ping, which is rather flattering towards him, even calling him an 英雄 and saying he was the only pirate who frightened Qi Jiguang. Wang says that this narrative was drawn from earlier difangzhi but does not appear in the Qing-era Haicheng, Zhao’an, or Zhangzhou gazetteers, suggesting it was censored for being too close to the 倭亂者的立場; but he didn’t check the Chaozhou gazetteer. 120
- The origins of Yuegang were associated with a militant leader named 張維, who was credited (according to the Haicheng xianzhi) with being one of the first locals from the 8th and 9th du to build ships and sail abroad around 1554; he then became the leader of the “24 generals” at 草坂城. In 1564 the Haidao 周賢 and Tongzhi 登士元 were credited with defeating Zhang Wei and forcing him to surrender, opening the road for Haicheng to be made a county. 121 
- Discusses Zhu Wan’s proposals to create a county out of Yuegang, and suggests that they failed due to local opposition as well as  wariness about the expense of a new administrative center; Zhu repeatedly emphasized that strict baojia was the best, most cost-effective tool for oversight of the coast. 
	- Wang believes that Zhu Wan was mistaken in thinking that greater local oversight could suppress coastal trade, since Fujianese society was too dependent on it to give it up; the shift from Zhao’an to Yuegang proves that crackdowns in one place would just lead to smugglers migrating elsewhere. (However, I have serious doubts about Wang’s analysis that a real “shift” happened entirely due to state oversight, since Yuegang was probably important before the 1550s, and Zhao’an was made a county already in 1530.)
- Proof of Haicheng’s rapid growth: the city walls were expanded in 1570, 1582, and again in 1595. (Cites Lin Renchuan, p. 150)
- On the 二十四将，focuses on the failures of the Zhu Wan era attempts to cheaply regulate Jiulong Delta society via baojia and an 安边馆 in Haicang (a station intended to prevent illegal shipping). These measures were insufficient to patrol the whole coast, failed to get local participation, and were suborned by corruption of the officials responsible. 133-135
- Periodizes the "24 Generals" uprising in four stages: "fermentation of the uprising" (1557-1561), "eruption" (1561), "recruitment" 招抚 (1561-1564), and finally "re-emergence and pacification of rebellion" (1564). Note that the major officials involved in this are the local haidao and zhifu, not the provincial-level forces. 135
	- Main sources are the essays of Xie Bin from the Haicheng Xianzhi, and Zhu Wan's Piyu Zaji. 
	- Cites the Haicheng Xianzhi (舆地志，建置) which compares the 24 Generals to "化外": 结巢盘踞，殆同化外". 
- Only after the 24 Generals were defeated did Wang Daokun and other officials finally succeed in getting Yuegang turned into a county; implication is that the rebellion finally convinced the court of the necessity of increasing oversight over the region. 139
- Cites a line from Tan Lun's 条陈善后未尽事宜...疏 arguing that coastal Min people rely on fish, shrimp, and Guangdong's rice boats, and without the 外洋 they have no way of living -- so if their coastal trade is banned they will have no choice but to break the law. 146
Chapter 3 
- Wang emphasizes that, once the basic policy of opening Haicheng to maritime trade was adopted, the details were developed from the bottom up and then approved by the court. The original opening of the haijin (which Wang does not provide any details on besides what Zhang Xie says) was "not to develop overseas commerce, or to collect taxes for military salaries." It was purely designed to stop the constant piracy problems; essentially an admission of defeat against Zhangzhou smugglers. Thus the main concern of the court was that the new system's implementation should avoid further political risks.
- The Wanli Zhangzhou Fuzhi contains a detailed description of the beginnings of maritime taxation at Haicheng, attributing the first experimental tax station there to a Longqing 6 proposal by Luo Qingxiao (as prefect of Zhangzhou). After a few years of successful collection, the tax station was made permanent and institutionalized with regular schedules for collection under Liu Yaohui in Wanli 3. (149-51)
	- Two patrol stations at Haomen and Songyu checked incoming vessels. Taxes were levied on foreign goods (pepper, sappanwood, ivory are named), and merchants were taxed a proportion of the value of such goods purchased upon leaving the town, following the precedent of the border customs stations with Jiangxi, while ordinary goods of domestic consumption were not taxed. 及商人買貨回橋，俱照贛州橋稅事列，酌量抽取，其民間日用鹽米魚菜之類，不必概抽。
- The main texts regulating the Haicheng customs office were drawn up in 1575 by the Haifang Tongzhi Shen Zhi 沈植 and approved/promulgated by Liu Yaohui. This was also the first time the court formally approved the establishment of a customs office in Haicheng, though one had been operating informally (as noted) since 1572. 151
	- Shen Zhi's 海禁便宜十七事 banned the practice of "cramming winter," in order to avoid connections with wokou. 
- Wang sees the importance of these regulations, fixing the amount of maritime customs to be dedicated to military salaries, as lying in their being the Ming's first-ever attempt to turn private maritime trade to public benefit. 152
