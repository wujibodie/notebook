據時酌議兵食以保固海防疏
- Long memorial outlining Liu's immediate recommendations after first full year in Fujian.
- Autumn 1573: estimates the 1574 coastal-defense budget at 150,600 taels, but even if every scheduled surtax (丁二米四, temple rents, salt surcharge, tax-deed, etc.) is collected the province will still be 40,000 taels short. Proposes to close the gap—without new levies on an already exhausted populace—by (1) temporarily earmarking the current “tenth-year” tax-deed proceeds, (2) borrowing one year of the 22,200-tael salt surcharge normally sent to the northern frontier, and (3) drawing 30,000 taels from the imperial treasury “in peace to avert future peril,” just as the court earlier advanced 400,000 taels after the 1562 fall of Putian. Concurrently asks to fill the three recently demobilized “guest-army” battalions with 3,000 Zhejiang mercenaries, restore five decommissioned warships per coastal squadron, and defer further cost-cutting so that troops “do not fight on empty stomachs.” Warning that the pirate fleets of Lin Feng (off Taiwan/琉球) and Zhu Liangbao (Guangdong border) are both poised to strike Fujian, Liu requests discretionary authority to mobilize additional reserves once revenue is secured.
請給閥防駐倒信地以重責成以杜姦弊疏
- asks the throne to cast and issue an official seal for the Zhangzhou Prefectural Assistant Surveillance Commissioner for Coastal Defense (海防同知) and, at the same time, to authorize moving that officer’s seat from the prefectural city to Yunxiao鎮 – a choke-point one day’s march from the garrisons of Tong-shan, Xuan-zhong, Liu-ao and Zhao-an. Because the coastal-defense official must personally verify monthly rations, inspect ships under construction and countersign all pay and supply documents, the present three-to-four-day journey overland (or two-week detour by sea when tides block the route) invites delay, surcharging and fraud; local bandits also exploit the jurisdictional seams between counties. Yun-xiao already contains a confiscated compound that can be converted to a yamen at modest cost; once the seal is delivered the commissioner can reside there year-round, pay troops on the spot, keep accounts under tight seal, and act concurrently as military-police intendant for the border zone, thus tightening control of the Guangdong–Fujian pirate corridor without raising new taxes or troops. (Essentially, recommends moving military oversight to the coastal valleys nearer the problematic Chao-Zhang border.)

奉旨建言民情疏
- Liu Yaohui’s memorial of early 1574 observes that after years of borrowing and juggling every available Fujian tax item to finance coastal warfare the province finally holds its own store of salt commutation silver and standing quotas; he therefore asks that, instead of forcing these modest tolls on ports, mines, temples and market boats to keep flowing to Beijing, they be permanently reserved in the provincial treasury as a special “seaward contingent fund,” to be drawn on for soldiers’ pay and hull repairs whenever fleets sail, but otherwise left in local circulation so that merchants and villagers are not bled anew each time the drums beat.

- Wanli 4.5.23. Liu Yaohui forwards the provincial accounts for the new bronze-cash mint ordered by the throne in 1576. Using 1,265 jin of brass (bought, along with other materials, from 9.4 taels of silver from county-level “retained” revenue 存留銀), Fujian’s new Fuzhou mint has already struck 10,400 coins worth 10.4 taels, yielding a clear profit of 0.97 tael; scaled up, a 100,000-coin run would net nearly 10 taels and a million coins almost 100 taels. The coins average 1.5 qian weight (slightly above the ministry’s 1.4-qian model), making them heavy enough to deter private casting yet still interchangeable with older cash. Liu therefore asks that the sample 10,000 coins now enclosed be approved and that full production proceed, its seigniorage to be recycled as capital for still larger mintings so that “mother-and-son” circulation will expand indefinitely, supplying both provincial expenses and popular currency without extra levies.

(Note: Geng Dingxiang was promoted to Xingbu Zuoshilang in Wanli 13, so that would date this letter after Zhang Juzheng's death.)
- The line 明公在南则南重，今过北方学者又为明公重于南也。seems to be a reference to Geng representing the "southern" faction now weak after Zhang's disgrace; places the weight of the whole 方 on Geng's shoulders; but he also cautions him that he's far from his friends among the shi. 
- 宛陵承别，日夕思所以副德教嘱勉，未能抵家月余，即促先孺人变，泉毁中百礼敝坏，故于明公未得专致起居，并所教《实鉴新录》述次，得细与究味之，乃见作者之意为世道政本，大有裨益，其文且足行也。是秋即转寄去养旦兄，烦为缴复记室，今果何如？想已授梓人传布人间矣。近有自白下来者，审闻荣陟尉卿，奉内召已多日，欣慰可言！明公在南则南重，今过北，方学者又为明公重于南也。第已辞文院，陟廷尉，则是于左右晏然，近于士人疏矣，岂知沃上固所以附疏，其转干又更易易耶？时会清夷，善类显遂，独明公尤兀然系天下望。使明公知所以责华之意，则思所以副天下士人者，不容有一念一行少懈也。记志学别时，不肯曾以《抽赋》奉教，今觉此意尚未是。**孟老好辩，岂不能默？孔不暇席，岂故曰劳？值时不可耳。乃知濂溪翁此赋有所感于事而作，其要在于微刑政，亦如孔孟之欲汲汲易世也。曾真抽耶？直不巧耳**。近以忧郁中忽契此意，具陈为候，不尽欲言。

與譚二華: 
- Opens with typical flattery of Tan's brilliance and fame etc. etc. Notes the great sweep of Tan's campaigns, which reach the mountains, oceans, and plains. Expresses clear gratitude to Tan as a patron of his earlier career; reference to serving under Tan for three years (referring to 3 years as Fujian Xunfu while Tan was Bingbu Shangshu?).  Notes that he (Liu) is so foolish his wife is always nagging him. Expresses the wish to repay Tan under the surface protestations of humility. Seems likely this was written in 1577, to thank Tan for support during Liu's impeachment and eventual reappointment to Jiangxi. 
- 伏惟門下清朝儒碩，名世人英。道中神交，來秘傳丁黃石；德由天縱，明台曜于紫宸。幕府威賢，才羣策畢展；廟算多勝算，夷夏傾心。神運煙中，風馳域外；梯山航海，盡入荒牧底定之勳。畫閣雲臺，將千載著祈常之烈。道侔周畢，功掩范禪，猶乃謙讓作則，得士而真之懷；休休有容，進人必加諸膝。即其駑駘本品，亦荷顧盼殊恩。投鍼三年，未失一志；感遭愛者剎冤竹再生，遵舊服者喜召棠猶在。惟慈存釅，咸受訓誨；豈意微勞，遽徼懋賞。緣門下後投授之跡，先推轂之心，故卒使魚目混于靈蛇，而罷足等子仙鹿也。顧慚才小，寔非稍祠之靈；尤負抽多，難謝妻非之口。含近而計遠，豈善謀身；夫安而即危，誠非巧宦。使微日月獨彰于幽蔀，寧免康莊偏哭于窮途。感激高深，鍍鑄心膂；怠報德之無期，陳詞增恧，僅寸心之見諒，雖黜猶榮。
