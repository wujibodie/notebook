- Key conclusion: The Song commercial taxation net completely withered away in the Ming. Of over 2000 commerce-tax stations in the southern Song, around 1000 were still used in the early Ming, but thereafter this number dropped dramatically. "然而在明朝建立以後的二百年中，這些稅課司局紛紛被裁撤，到了萬曆初年，全國僅剩 111 所。" Guangdong and Fujian only had 1 such post each, in the provincial capitals (so *none* in Minnan). 
- Main explanation for the difference between Song and Ming is that in the Song, operation of the local customs stations was under oversight of zhou/xian level officials, whose performance reviews depended on the revenue brought in from those stations, which was retained for local use -- local officials were thus incentivized to deeply understand and foster local commercial conditions. By contrast, in the Ming these stations' revenues were directly forwarded to the capital, so were poorly administered and tended to be sidelined or removed entirely by local admins. 66-7
- Also blames the removal of the capital to Beijing: "其次，從地理環境的角度看，明代北京與商業化程度高的長江中下游和東南地區相距遼遠，陸運成本高昂，水運僅有一線運河相通，遠不如兩宋都城 —— 開封和杭州 —— 與國內其他城市圈之間的交通便捷，這一制度之外的因素也給明廷管轄全國稅課司局增添了困難."
