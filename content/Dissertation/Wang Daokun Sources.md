More recently, Wang Daokun has been the subject of a number of critical reappraisals, biographies, and reprintings, but even these, whether focused on Wang as a literary giant or a source of historical detail, have tended to be very localist in character -- primarily sponsored by Anhui publishing presses, they largely seek to rehabilitate the fame of this local son. Few of these reevaluations engage with the period of time that Wang spent in Fujian; but these years, from 1561-66, were among the most pivotal of his career. It was in Fujian that Wang first became a noted expert on border defense, and there that he first collaborated with his long-time partners Tan Lun and Qi Jiguang (perhaps his closest friend in the bureaucracy). Wang Daokun's impact on Fujian itself was equally as profound: not only did he participate in the great wave of reforms that helped see the province through the wokou crisis of the 1560s, he was also the province's standout literary figure of the age, penning dozens of poems on local scenery. His attempts to rework the old *biansai shi* form to the environment of the southeast coast -- from frigid steppe and searing deserts to misty coasts and verdant cliffs -- rank as some of the most distinctively "Fujianese" poetry of the Ming, and are important sources in their own rights for the history of the province in this time.
