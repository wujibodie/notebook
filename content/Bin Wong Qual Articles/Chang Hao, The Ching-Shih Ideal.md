
- Problem: "Jingshi" was a broadly-defined term used by people of very different perspectives. "One of several interchangeable symbols embodying a a complex of concerns and ideas centered around the problem: how to set the society and the world in order?"
- Begins with a discussion of the "moral-psychological" "Neo-Confucian" understanding of Jingshi, where the primeval unity of human nature and Heaven is interrupted by material barriers, and so the goal is to purify individual consciousness to reach the level of the sages and the Sandai; moral utopianism which tended rather to legitimize than criticize the status quo institutions. Cosmological utopianism counterbalanced the transcendental strain in Neo-Confucianism, in Lu-Wang emphasis on the "autonomy of an intuitive moral mind."
- "Cosmological myth" tends to redirect project of "ordering the world" towards individual cultivation, since the "moral effort to purify individual self... was also the hinge for inducing the macroscopic order."
- There also existed a tradition of Jingshi as "pracitcal statesmanship," resulting from a blend of responses to practical crises of the dynastic order with "the impingement of the spiritual-moral perspective of Confucianism." Practical statesmanship "did not share the euphoric tendency so prominent in the tradition of moral statesmanship." 46
	- Gu Yanwu as example of a figure who implicitly accepted the importance of consequences and utility even within a mostly "Neo-Confucian" moral framework.
	- A line of thinkers who accepted the practical need for coercion and profit-seeking, including Wang Anshi, Ye Shi, Yan Yuan, and Wei Yuan, "whose political thought was dominated by their interest in financial and military matters." Zhang Juzheng would surely fit in here, though the category seems drawn extremely broadly. 
- A tradition of "institutional statecraft" interested in the evolution of secular political forms beyond the narrative of decline from the Sandai. Critiques of Song/Ming despotism did not produce calls to actually abolish the monarchy. Instead, development of advocates for a "mixed system" combining elements of the *junxian* and the *fengjian*, as in Gu Yanwu's proposal that prefectural magistrates be given hereditary tenure. Calls to buttress the bureaucratic system by devolving responsibilites to large-scale kin groups and voluntary associations also fit into this "mixed system" paradigm.
