- Basic starting point: look at Qing local governance from perspective of its internal bureaucratic procedures rather than the standpoint of “social control." 
	- Main source is the 福建省列, a procedural manual compiled in Fujian by the local administration with the earliest surviving example dating to 1873-4, though the earliest recorded cases go back to Qianlong 17. 
	- It includes cases 案 on *internal* topics including: examinations 公式, granaries 仓库, money and grain 钱粮, annual accounts 奏销, exchange (of officials) 交代, expenses 解支, salaries 俸禄, supplemental yanglian 养廉, donations 捐款, family pensions 恤赏, military salaries 兵饷, postal services 邮政, labor levies 差务, and promotion/demotion 铨政. 
		- Of these, the mails (45), granaries (25), exchange (23), and yanglian (21) have the most dedicated cases. 
	- And *public* topics including: taxes 税课, grain price leveling *pingtiao* 平粜, voluntary granaries 社仓, population 户口, land ownership 田宅, encouragement of land reclamation 劝垦, regular taxes on the *hang* 当税, exam halls 科场, salt 盐政, monetary policy 钱法, iron 铁政, regulation of boats 船政, coastal defense 海防, construction and maintenance 修造, punishments 刑政, tax transportation 捐输, tax collection 征收, banditry suppression 缉匪. 
		- Largest number of cases for punishments (121) and boats (36).
	- Sees the regularization of expenses in the yitiao bianfa as making it impossible for local governments to respond to unexpected costs; in Fujian the problems of military salaries and piracy meant that the fiscal system was already in collapse by the end of the Ming. Each county had original quotas 原额 for expenditures in various categories which carried over to the Qing, with very little money earmarked for miscellaneous or unexpected expenditures. In the Qing large parts of these quotas were further appropriated by provincial or central authorities. 
	- Sees a continuous shriveling of the scope of local government from the Kangxi era on, as measured for example by the number of non-official employees hired by local yamens; since most of these laborers were indispensable to basic functions, their removal from official budgets forced officials to pay their salaries out of their own pockets. 
		- "上述政府雇员原为地方官员的主要行政助手，裁撤之后仍不可或缺，因而只能由地方官员自行设法雇募，这大概就是清代幕友盛行的基本原因". 26
		- In general the only county-level functions to receive more funding in the course of the Qing were those associated with education and customs, such as teachers' salaries and public rites; of course such expenses did nothing to improve the day-to-day functioning of government. 这些举措固然反映了乾隆时代重视文教的倾向，但并未加强地方政府的行政职能，也不可能使地方财政经费紧缺的状况得到改善。
	- There was a shift in the middle of the Qianlong reign away from local jurisdiction over use of "excess" revenues towards mandatory remission of such revenues. Loss of the biggest source of discretionary funds for local projects, and loss of incentive to collect "yanglian". 
	- By the 19th century local finances were so chaotic that the ordinary procedure of having incoming officials verify that their predecessors' accounts were in order became impossible; since new officials were personally responsible to make up deficits in the accounts, they developed the habit of simply clearing old account books and starting from scratch on arrival. Fujian thus became famous for having an unusually large number of "open cases," matters which were simply left unresolved after the arrival of a new officials, and in some cases were left open for six or seven terms. 
		- A Fuzhou Zhifu: "伏查各属交代，竟有一县未结五、六案之多，迟延六、七任之久，实为他省所未有" 27
	- Cites a Minzhe Zongdu of Daoguang 9, 孙尔准, as noting that food costs and thus wages in rural Taiwan were higher than elsewhere, meaning that expenditures for runners and militiamen necessarily needed to increase from the standard quota; but since it was politically impossible to spend more than the allotted amounts, he had to instruct local Taiwan officials to pay for these expenses with off-the-books income, particularly “就廉摊捐," surcharges on existing taxes. When extension of this practice was banned in Fujian in Tongzhi 1, there were 18 such surcharges being collected. 31
	- Because most *yanglian* had to be spent paying old debts, there was never adequate local revenue for new disbursements, hence why by the late Qing Fujianese officials almost always refused to perform the ordinary transfer accounting, and each departing official left behind a "mountain of unresolved cases." 31
		- At least one member of the local gentry, 陈寿祺, commented that this situation made local officials unavoidably reliant on bribes: "大邑廉俸仅千金，而岁费当数万缗。郡伯之例规，幕宾之修铺，驿传之供亿，贼囚之解送，其用至浩繁。计安所出?非资贿于民不可。"
