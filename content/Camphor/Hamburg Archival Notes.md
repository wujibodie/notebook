## 111-1 46734: Resignation of the Foochow Consul

1. **Resignation letter from G.W. Schwemann, 25/01/68.** Mentions that he has paid an agent of Dircks & Co. $20.50 to take over consular duties.
2. **Note summarizing #1.**
3. **Reply to Schwemann.** Hard to read, but mentions the $20.50 to Dircks should be made out to a **G. J. Siemsen.**
4. **Letter to Merck from Peers in Fuzhou, 8/10/68.** Also, a letter in English—apparently the British customs commissioner declines to send consular reports for the Hansa towns. Also requests the return of certain documents belonging to "certain North German vessels," including those of a Mr. Siemsen.
5. **Letter to Lübeck explaining the situation.** Ganz unlesbar と思う.
6. **Lübeck's reply, 1/12/1870.** The Senate is baffled, since a Hanseatic consulate was never established in Foochow. Expresses the view that it is no longer advisable to establish independent Hanseatic embassies, "**insbesondere in China**," and that *Bundesconsulaten* [Confederation consulates] are to be preferred.

## 46772: Milisch’s Proposal for Tamsui

**1. Milisch's proposal of a consulate, Tamsui, November 3rd 1865.**

> Eur. Magnificenz,
>
> Da jährlich hier und in Kiellung [Keelung] eine Anzahl Schiffe verkehren, worüber eine gute Menge die Hamburger & Bremer Flagge führen, erlaube ich mir Eur. Magnificenz die ergebene Anzeige zu machen, daß die freien Hansestädte hier auch kein Consulat besitzen. — Es würde mir, als Hamburger, zur Ehre gereichen, das Interesse dieselben als Consul wahrzunehmen und die (wehenden?) Flaggen die drei Städte hier und in Kiellung zu beschutzen.
>
> Hinsichtlich Kiellung erlaube ich mir noch zu bemerken, daß solches ein Hafenplatz ist, welcher Tamsui untergeben, da alle Schiffe welche daselbst ankommen hier (re-clamieren?) haben.
>
> Unter jetzigen Umständen müssen die Schiffe deren Flagge nicht vertreten [ist] ihre Papieren an hiesiges Chinesisches Customhaus abgeben, welches häufig zum Nachtheil derselben gereicht.
>
> Herr G. T. Siemsen in Hamburg und Herr Woldemar Nissen, Hamburg. Consul zu Hongkong, werden jederzeit mit (Anzeige?) Auskunft über meinen Charakter geben.
>
> In Entgegenstehung von gefälligen Antwort verbleibe Eur. Magnificenz, ganz ergebenst
>
> **James Milisch**

## 111-1 46774: Beschwerden der Chinesischen Regierung (The Milisch Affair)

1. **Letter from Lübeck to Bremen + Hamburg: 18/06/1869.**
    * Shares **Bismarck's letter on Milisch**. Expresses that the Chinese complaints may well be intended to harm Hanseatic commercial interests.
    * However, if Milisch is guilty it would be a violation of the **1861 treaty**.
    * Insist he should be made aware of the charges and allowed to defend himself.
    * Still, given the distances involved + the urgency of the situation, the Senate reluctantly recommends giving in to Bismarck while advocating for a *Bundesconsulat* on Formosa.

### Letter from Rehfues to Bismarck, 25/08/69
* Reports that he has received countless complaints from the Chinese authorities and summarizes the case as he sees it:
    * **Horn**, who had spent some years among the "savages," together with **Milisch**, established a treaty with tribes on the north coast.
    * They agreed to cooperate against "**gemeinsame Feinde**" [common enemies], which could only be the Chinese.
    * They gathered a large group of natives under this treaty and took them to occupy **Kamalan** [Kavalan], taking "necessary steps" to protect their two colonies ("wie sie dieselben nannten").
* The Chinese authorities considered these settlements to lay on the boundary of their territory but, not wanting to attack the Europeans, just sent "endlose Berichte" [endless reports].
* Rehfues confirms that he immediately warned Milisch to cease his illegal activities lest he "**daraus anstehenden Nachtheile selbst zuzuschreiben habe**" [have only himself to blame for the resulting disadvantages].
* Explains that he contacted **Sir Rutherford Alcock** to inform him that an Englishman was involved.
* Ends by saying he has given so much detail because he expects that Milisch will have written to Hamburg defending himself.

### 18. Milisch's Defenses
* 3 letters plus copies of the treaty + "perpetual lease" with the natives.
* **Milisch to Rehfues, 16/06/1869.** Starts with 5 documents:
    1. Lease with chieftains.
    2. Agreement with Kabarang [Kavalan] chiefs to cultivate the land.
    3. Letter from Horn to Vice-Consul William Gregory.
    4. Reply from Gregory.
    5. Sketch of N. Formosa.
* **Milisch's Argument:**
    * The colonies were founded in aboriginal territory, where the Chinese authorities have no legal rights, and that he and Horn are engaging in legal economic activities.
    * Insists that if the Chinese wish to take over these lands, they will have to buy them at a fair price.
    * Insists that at very least he must be compensated for his initial investment. Claims he + Horn have invested over **$20,000 (!)**.
    * Says that work on the colony has ceased, leading to discontent + violence among the workers.
    * Milisch describes the lands of **Tua Lam O** and **Lam O** as flat, well-watered valleys suitable for rice, sugar, tobacco, and indigo, surrounded by mountains rich in metals, **camphor**, and other trees.
* **Milisch to Rehfues, 15/09/69.**
* **Berlin (?) to Hamburg, 07/04/70.** Letter introducing the files forwarded to Hamburg by Rehfues.

### Agreement between Horn/Milisch + Five Pepowhan [Pepohoan] Chiefs
* H+M agree to provision and defend 150 families for 6 months until the colonies are self-sustaining.
* Total population of 1,000 "men or families."
* The natives agree to "obey all orders that may be given" by H+M.
* Signed at **Ta-lam-ok**, 01/02/69.
* **Lease:** The local chiefs lease 2 valleys from the seashore "as far inwards as it is practicable to clear or go."
* They also agree to help defend the colonies + to assist shipwrecked sailors.
* The rent is **4 pigs, 1 bag gunpowder, 8 lbs lead, rice and fish monthly**.
* Certified by Br. Vice-Consul on 24 May 1869.

### The Aftermath
19. **Note from Siemsen to Merck** enclosing passage of the *Customs Gazette* reporting **Horn's death by drowning**, Feb. 14, 1870. The shipwreck occurred on 12th Nov 69. Surviving "Malay" + Chinese sailors were ransomed for $220.
20. **Reply from Merck? 3/3/70.**
21. **Note to Merck**, w/ business card, from **Dr. Arnold Schetelig**. Attempts to summarize a Chinese document provided to him, but which he finds too difficult.
22. **Ditto.**
23. **Excerpt from Swinhoe's "Notes on Formosa"** along with Merck's notes. Attempts to resolve the question of whether China is the legitimate authority over all of Formosa, or just a part.
24. **Blue note starting Syndic Merck.**
25. **Senate of Hamburg to Bismarck, 30/05/70.**
26. **Milisch's papers.** Includes letters to Hamburg, correspondence w/ Formosan customs, and copies. Longest letter is to Merck, 10/07/70.
27. **The commercial treaty with China.**
28. **China Overland Trade Report, 27.12.68.** "Difficulties in Formosa."
    * The writer states that troubles began when **camphor worth $6,000** purchased for **Messrs. Ellis & Co.** was seized as monopoly of the Daotai. [Marginalia: **怡和洋行** - *Jardine Matheson & Co.*]
* ...and not released even after intervention from **LeGendre** + **Jamieson**.
* **Liang Daotai** then refused to allow the Catholic missionaries to rent property in Taiwan-fu, and allowed an "outrage" against them.
* He allowed rumors against the missionaries to spread, such that a **Catholic chapel at Koa-kan was burned in April**, along with a Protestant one at Pit-ow.
* **William Pickering**, an agent of Ellis, was attacked while trying to inquire about the camphor, and had to flee to Tamsui.
* In late October, Ellis & Co's house in Taiwan-fu was sacked.
* At this point **Anping Fort was occupied**, and after brief battles the Viceroy of Fujian agreed to dismiss the offending officials, cancel the camphor monopoly, reaffirm the right of missionaries to work everywhere in the island, and pay various indemnities ($2000 to the Catholics).
