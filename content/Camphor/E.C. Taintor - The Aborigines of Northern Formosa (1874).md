- Taintor visited Danan'ao in Jan-Feb 1869 in a group probably including James and Alexander Horn, Milisch, himself, a "Spanish Mexican" (Herce??), a "Goa Portuguese" (Dos Santos?), a Malay, and a contingent of Chinese and Kavalans. 
	- "My own visit to the savages on the east coast, which was made in January and February 1869, arose partially out of circumstances connected with the colonization scheme alluded to. Very shortly after my visit, the scheme ended in a disastrous collapse, and a few months later the foreigner who had been its active leader was drowned near the southern end of the island." 6
- Describes some of the songs and dances he witnessed the Pepos performing, including an epic song about their dispossession: "In another, and perhaps the most pleasing song, they chanted in a low, plaintive voice the story of the wrongs they have suffered at the hands of the Chinese, who have driv en them from their homes, seized their lands, and killed their people." 18
- Complains at length of the fleas the travelers encountered, and notes in passing that they slept on beds of camphor chips to get some distance from the ground. 
- Describes at length the celebrations accompanying one treaty of friendship with the shengfan, sealed by a pig roast and wine: "The most acceptable pr esent to make to the savages, and in fact an indispensable one to gain their good will, is a pig." 26
- Taintor translates sections of the Gemalan Tingzhi on 33-36. It seems much of the foreigners' understanding of the district actually came from this gazetteer. He quotes the following on the practice of aborigines signing leases with a fingermark: "There are 36 tribes of tame aborigines scattered over the Komalan district. They are simple and dull by nature, and the Chinese, by giving them a measure of wine or a foot of cloth, can induce them to sign the lease of large tracts of land. As they cannot read, they cannot know the contents of the lease, which they sign by impressing upon it a finger wet with ink; and they are thus completely at the mercy of the Chinese."
