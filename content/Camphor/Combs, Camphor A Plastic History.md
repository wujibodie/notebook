- The "Camphor War" treaty did not actually end the camphor monopoly system, which was already being opened earlier in 1868. E.C. Taintor, the Qing maritime customs comissioner for Tamsui/Keelung, said that in the summer lots of camphor bought in the producing districts were brought to a foreign merchant to Tamsui "under armed escort," as the Qing "monopolists" harassed the export convoys, but ultimately around 7,300 piculs of camphor were exported from Tamsui in 1868 vs ~1,300 the year before. He's talking about Milisch's business as the first to break the camphor monopoly! 67-68, citing E.C.
