
- Founded by Graf Arthur Butler von Clonebough (various spellings, e.g. Clonmore), the younger son of a count living in the South Bavarian town of Haimhausen. The Butler von Cloneboughs descended from the Irish mercenary Walter Butler, who was rewarded by Emperor Ferdinand II with a former domain of Wallenstein's after joining in the latter's assassination. (Walter Butler appears as a villain in Schiller's Wallenstein.)
	- See *Die Wallenstein-Trilogie von Friedrich Schiller: Walter Buttler in Geschichte und Drama*. FLORIAN KROBB
- By the 19th century the Butler von Cloneboughs were well-integrated into Bavarian elite society, with a number of them serving in the Bavarian officer corps; what could have induced Arthur to leave for an uncertain merchant's life on the other side of the world?

- Huang Fusan article on Butler's involvement in Liu Minchuan's camphor monopoly.
- In December 1898, while travelling along the "Pattokwan" trail (probably 八通關，from 南投 to Hualien), [Dr. Stoepel](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=uc1.b4506676&seq=9&q1=kampfer) stayed with Herrn Greiner, a former agent of Butler's in that region who inherited his camphor stills when the latter left Taiwan; he tried to operate them on his own account, but suffered a loss when his main labor crew was massacred by headhunters. 
	- Greiner, who apparently felt he had good relations with the natives, expressed a sense of betrayal, especially since no other coolies were willing to work for him after this event.
	- Gives a few details of Greiner's life: born in Baden, but restless and dissatisfied, he first did 4 years of military service in Kehl, then joined the Foreign Legion in Algiers, in the service of which he partook in the attack on Jilong in 1885; then he decided to desert, first going over to Liu Mingchuan's service, then finding work with "Graf Buttler." He also married and had children with a Chinesin.
