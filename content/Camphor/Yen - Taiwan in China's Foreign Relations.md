V. Consular Establishment on Taiwan
- Eulenberg's mission opened Tamsui and Taiwan to German traders on September 2, 1861. The British soon added Banka and Keelung to the deal, but it's unclear if that applied immediately to Germans. 99-100
- In July, 1865, articles advocating for Prussian annexation of Taiwan appeared in the Norddeutsche Allgemeine Zeitung; Yen attributes these to Eulenberg; specifically names Su'ao as ideal capital of a Deutsch-Indens in the "herrenloses Land" of the indigenous east. 
	- Vice-Consul Gregory agreed that the aboriginal half of Formosa was "herrenloses Land" and that "if the Chinese government wishes to prevent the appropriation of Saw-o Bay by a foreign power, it might be well for it to establish there some unmistakable sign of its own dominion." B.F.O. 228/398
- Horn-Milisch case on 106-109. Explains it as an attempt by foreign merchants to break the camphor monopoly by "cutting trees" themselves.
