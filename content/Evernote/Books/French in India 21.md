Haudrère

* The French India Company was the earliest of the European east India companies to suspend its business, in 1769.
* The French cargoes tended to consist of 80%+ precious metals, with each ship carrying 4-6 million livres tournais. This cargo was mostly made up of silver piastres, which had to be smuggled from Spanish America, usually via Louisiana, St Domingo, or French smugglers visiting Chile and Peru. A certain number of French trading voyages stopped at Cadiz to obtain piastres legally.
	* Also obtained silver via Bayonne (through the Basque Country) and Amsterdam

Ames

* Conflict between Colbert's mercantilist aims in founding the CIO and Louis XIV's "aristocratic" emphasis on territorial aggrandizement in Europe -> early gains were wasted in the Dutch War
* Beginning of the _Compagnie des Indes Orientales_ c. 1664-1670 under Caron was focused on squeezing into the Indian Ocean trade dominated by the VOC.
	* Factories were founded in Persia and Surat, but the French lacked knowledge of the trading environment
* Colbert attempted to overcome initial difficulties by sending a large squadron to India in 1670. The French attempted to negotiate an alliance w/ Portugal along the same lines as the Anglo-Portuguese alliance, by which the English were granted Tangier and Bombay in return for protecting Portuguese ships from the VOC; but the French never gained any material concessions
	* The English also refused an alliance out of concern that the CIO could become a competitor
* The VOC was well-informed of French plans before the Persian Squadron set out in March 1670. The squadron itself was delayed for a full winter on Madagascar, and when they finally encountered a Dutch fleet off Cape Comorin, they were prevented by Caron from attacking it.
	* Although the French fortified a position on Ceylon, they were never ordered to go on the offensive, in part due to Louis XIV's shifting of priorities to the European theatre + unwillingness to spend on reinforcing the Indian Ocean fleet. In the end Louis XIV ordered four of the six men-of-war in the fleet to return to Europe
* The squadron seized the town of St. Thomé in Golconda, and resisted an attempt by the Qutb Shah to retake it. Eventually La Haye was forced to surrender after a combined Golcondan-Dutch siege in 1674.
	* Ames blames the collapse on lack of a bourgeois political bloc which could pressure the French monarch to support the CIO consistently.

Manning

* For artists of the 18th Century, Asia evoked "in contradistinction to America and Africa... the products of human cultivation, art and ingenuity." 1
* The major empires of the Indian Ocean were weakening in the early 1700s in the face of religiously-inflected tribal violence: the Ottomans were expelled from Yemen by the Zaidis and suffered raids by the Najdi followers of al-Wahhab, even as they faced war with Russia and Persia -> trade at Basra became riskier and less profitable. The Safavid dynasty was destroyed by the Afghans in 1722 and as a result Bandar Abbas was cut off from the Persian heartland, and Muscati pirates dominated the Gulf. The Mughals held on to their territory but suffered from Marathi and Afghan raids and the devolution of power to regional authorities, especially in Bengal and Hyderabad; the central power was weakened and suffered immensely when Nadir Shah sacked Delhi in 1739. As a result the trade in Bengal was secure but that of Surat declined (to the advantage of the French).
* European commerce in Asia was dependent on respondentia loans from local merchants (loans on trading cargoes which were not repaid if the vessel was lost)
* General recession in Indian trade from ~1700-1770, but different regions fared differently; the Coromandel-Burma/Siam/Aceh route prospered after 1730, with European cities like Porto Novo, Pondicherry, and Madras all doing well. Manila's trade with India also reached a peak between 1680 and 1700.
* The French factory at Chandannagar (Bengal) trade with Gujarat, while that at Pondicherry was focused on the east: textile exports to Manila were highly important, while the French rarely had enough capital to be competitive in Canton.
* "The national chartered companies formed in the seventeenth century to trade with the Orient were the product of a common European climate of economic and commercial ideas, the solution to the difficulties of raising capital for voyages of long distance and duration. Although 'the decision to adopt a monopolistic form of organization was derived on the basis of conditions in Europe rather than those in Asia,' the similarities between the companies were chiefly a result of the conditions in the countries of Asia in which they traded: their differences came from the individual characteristics of the societies out of which they sprang."
	* Sees the character of the CIO as coming from its connection to a centralized aristocratic monarchy (as opposed to the EIC or VOC)
	* The CIO's administration was located in Paris, while its port and market were in Lorient
* The chief merchants involved in the Compagnie c.1715 were a group coming from St. Malo in Brittany, whose capital originated in American fishing and resulting smuggling of silver from Mexico. Reference to a Cadiz connection - think of Haudrere
	* "Their ships sailed the north Atlantic to the fishing grounds off Newfoundland; their traditional trade route was to Cadiz in Spain, supplying the needs of the Spanish colonies in America. This
	* trade had been extended during the wars with Spain to dealing direct with Latin America and the Caribbean. These interloping merchants, not quite pirates, had cut through Spanish control of American trade to bring European cloth and goods in exchange for silver. "
	* The St. Malo group gained the CIO concession in 1715, but John Law withdrew it in 1717 to lay the ground for his own company
* In general the CIO was always subordinate to the crown, and lacked the support of interest groups which could pressure the king in its favor; private merchants lacked a say in the policies of the company and were correspondingly unenthusiastic abt investing
* contrast to the English system, where merchants were closer to royal power, and the Dutch system, where the different Dutch regions had direct representation in the Heeren 17.
