治安策 - 賈誼
夫樹國固，必相疑之勢。 Essay opens with the claim that if feudal lords are given powerful positions, they will naturally vie to replace the emperor. No matter what ties the emperor uses to bind the lords to him, if strong enough to serve as a threat, they will always eventually turn on their master and each other. 

* Goes on to give history of enfeoffment of the various kings, and warns how they will eventually forget their initial ties to Liu Bang as generations pass.

屠牛坦一朝解十二牛，而芒刃不頓者，所排擊剝割，皆眾理解也。至於髖髀之所，非斤則斧。夫仁義恩厚，人主之芒刃也；權勢法制，人主之斤斧也。 - A different take on Pao Ding, as well as a pragmatic reconsideration of the value of 仁義 vis-a-vis force. The ruler glides along the hollow spaces with the fine, accurate, and intuitive tool of benevolence and justice, but turns to the reflective, rational, and violent axe of authority when he reaches knots or grain. Part of the purpose of the searching goodness of virtue is to reach and identify joints that must be broken open. 

* Goes on to offer a detailed plan for which feudal lords to destroy when, and why. 

欲天下之治安，莫若眾建諸侯而少其力。The basic principle for reorganization after the powerful kings have been defeated. Notably, a lack of power among the lords will make them (seemingly) morally superior: 力少，則易使以義；國小，則亡邪心。An acknowledgement that morality (at least among the politically powerful) is less dependent on inner differences, and more on objective circumstance? 
Ends with an extended disease metaphor; the powerful kings are like infections （瘇 = “dropsy of the leg”) in the limbs of the state, and once one begins to attack, the court will be too weak to stop the others. Therefore preventative action is needed to destroy to infected areas _before_ the crisis is reached (失今不治，必為錮疾，後雖有扁鵲，不能為已）. 

* would be interesting to do a study of prudential, farsighted, preventative diagnosis in politics, as companion to study of crisis
