Mokros
"Curated transparency" - Qing court released information about bureaucratic goings-on to facilitate official coordination, while allowing a certain check on imperial overreach, but was far from reporting entirely "transparently." Cases of published "remonstration" cultivated the image of a court open to critique, while of course not allowing for the dissemination of out-of-bounds views.

Information scandals

Reading court gazettes was a part of the "self-training" of _jingshi_ intellectuals, but in the 19th century readership grew enormously, largely on the back of expectant, out-of-office, and novice officials.

Idea of a "bureaucratic public," accustomed to up-to-date shared body of news.

Q.: How does the agenda of the state get coopted or compromised in its transmission to actual readership? What does the story of the Beijing gazettes tell us about the relationship between state and officialdom? To what extent does the community of news-readers constitute a self-conscious "public sphere"?
