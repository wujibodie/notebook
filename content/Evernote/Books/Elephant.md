The elephant has already been evolving on earth for 10,000,000 years, their appearance displays how life is alternately passive and active, directional and directionless, in responding to the environment. Their horizontal bones gradually became shorter, their vertical bones gradually became longer, their molars went from few to many, their enamel went from thick to thin, the lips of their trunk and their tusks became very slightly longer in 10,000 year by 10,000 year increments. Elephants’ bodies are time itself.
Once, elephants were this forest and these mountains’ spirit, their enormous and rarely murderous bodies were the incarnation of mercy, their pair of tiny eyes, flashing wisdom, gave hints of feeling and intelligence.
People once worshipped elephants, believing that elephants knew about the course of humans’ fates; at that time they still believed that people were the most insignificant animals, and the ones most lacking in the ability to communicate with spirits. But that time has passed.

The first time he went to the zoo and saw Lin Wang, Mubanzhang did not in his wildest dreams expect Lin Wang would remember him. Although elephants are enormously wise, he was not at all sure if elephants also had deep memories. Especially since they had already been parted for quite a while and separately experienced not a few things. Unexpectedly, when he was standing outside the elephant’s pen quietly calling “Ah Mei, Ah Mei,” Lin Wang came walking out of the arched gate and gave him that sort of call too low to be heard, but which made the air vibrate with buzzing. After that Lin Wang extended his long trunk and sniffed Mubanzhang’s head, shoulders, chest, and even approached his genitals (this is an important place for elephants to recognize each other), and then brought his soft trunk to rest on Mubanzhang’s palm missing three fingers.

In my process of novel-writing, I early on understood that fictional stories and non-fictional human lives must be intermingled, and so any written works have parts that can be doubted. In novels, taking anything as “real” is a dangerous thing. For example, in that novel I made the narrator “I” the son of an electronics store owner, but in reality my family owned a suit store, and later on we also sold jeans. Novels do not at all depend on facts, all novelists understand this. Nevertheless novels will occasionally also contain “pillars of truth.” For example Meme’s letter mentioned that “lost bicycle;” from my perspective, that just so happens to be that novel’s “pillar of truth.”

I still remember that after my father went missing, there was a long time when I didn’t dare to go back to Chonghua Road, where the now-demolished marketplace had stood. Directly after I received Meme’s letter, I gathered the courage to go back to Zhongshan Hall — the place where my fictional bike had disappeared. In fact that terrace had already been torn down and reconstructed, and I suddenly inspected the lie which I had created; this was really too ridiculous.

Maintaining the independent character of novel writing at least can give my life a space to turn around in, where I can say to myself and to any other people: “All this is just a novel, it’s fake, fictional, not real, but nevertheless it is a symbol or a metaphor.”

I know that whenever a writer asks you for your opinion on their works, what they want is always praise and not criticism, that question sentence is fake 100% of the time. ... Nevertheless, I know that there are some people who write novels who have a taboo on asking whether novels are “real,” and since I didn’t know if Sabrina cared about this point, I decided to ask in an indirect way.”
