Dream Interpretations

* "In a similar vein, Zichan expounds on how he placated the vengeful ghost of Boyou, a Zheng nobleman murdered in a power struggle between different clans. Boyou appears in the dreams of the Zheng people vowing to kill his enemies, and his threats come true. Zichan reinstates Boyou’s son, who is then in a position to offer sacrifices to his father. He explains, “When a ghost has a place to return to,118 it will not become a vengeful ghost. I provided it a place to return to” (_ZZ_ Zhao 7 .9, p. 1292). The dream is the locus for ghosts and spirits to manifest their discontent; it is a moment of ritual disorder marked by uneasy tension between the realm of the spirits and the human world. Dreams can be suppressed, however, because all things, including ghosts, can be assigned their proper place and mollified through the performance of sacrifice. Again we have the motif of con- trol and order.
* The restoration of ritual equilibrium         suggests the potential continuum between dreaming and wakeful states, analogous to the fluid boundaries between the realm of the spirits and the human world. Boyou can be a powerful ghost, Zichan explains, because his clan was in charge of the government for three generations and the things they used were abundant, elaborate, and refined (_ZZ_ Zhao 7.9, p. 1192). The po- tency of ghosts, including the power to manifest themselves in dreams,thus depends on their family history and the material conditions of their previous existence. The readability of dreams in _Z uozhuan_ is a function of continuities between past and present, dreaming and waking states: hence the insistent appeal to ancestors and beginnings. In admonitory dreams, advice or warning is usually given by a dead father or ancestor.” 238-239
	* Ghosts as public memory — a prominent figure is wrongfully murdered, and the people’s dreams are troubled until that figure is acknowledged openly, given public honors, and has had his family’s continuity assured.
