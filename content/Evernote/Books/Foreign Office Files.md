FO 371/9182 - Political situation in China: election of Tsao Kun as president of the Chinese Republic; situation at Amoy; civil war in Szechuan; civil war in Hunan; possibilities of hostilities in Shanghai

* "Situation at Amoy" runs from 67-70. August 1923
* An Englishman (or American?) named W.E. Davis writes for news from Amoy, as he has been unable to reach his daughter (a missionary) following news stories of bandit attacks on Europeans in Amoy. The consul writes back informing him that "although the situation at Amoy was still unsettled" the British Concession was evacuated by "Chinese soldiers" (Zang's?) and guarded by a British naval detachment on August 4, with no news of foreigners being harmed.

FO 371/10243 - China: Release of Sietas Plambeck property; political situation in China; intelligence reports; reunification; Canton; Pearl River; attempted assassination of minister for foreign affairs; Yangtse; Kiangsu and Chekiang hostilities

* Letter reproduced from October 11, 1923: "At Amoy a period of stagnation has set in. Frequent reports of projected attacks come through but so far all have ended in smoke. Tsang Chih Ping still remains in occupation of the Island, and seems determined to remain there." Sun Chuanfang has arrived in Fuzhou, and Zang and Wang are said to be working out whether to join forces or fight; the latter is said to have been "bought by Peking" and Admiral Yang is waiting to support him in an attack on Amoy.
* 44: 30 November 1923: "At Amoy things have been comparatively quiet, though occasional sniping occurs." Zang has left for Shanghai to consult with Lu Yongxiang.
* 100: Report by Fuzhou Consul Walter J. Clennel of 12 March, 1924 on the political situation of Fujian. "Ever since General Sun Ch'uan-fang became Tuli of Fukien in April last year, rumour has been busy with stories to the effect that the outwardly correct relations maintained between him and... General Wang Yung-ch'uan, veiled a latent antagonism that was bound, sooner or later, to break out with explosive force." "The two men are of very different types. General Sun, who is an avowed partisan of Marshall Wu P'ei-fu, has the manners and appearance of a cultured gentleman. General Wang, who has been a good many years in the Province and, throughout the various vicissitudes of the local Government, has always hitherto either adhered to or controlled the party in power, in the ultimate interest, it is understood, of his patron Marshall Chang Tso-lin, does not possess any refinement of manner or breeding. Both, however, have had some military education in Japan."
* Wang is said to control a so-called "Assassination Club" "an-sha P'ai", which claimed Zhou Yinren as one victim, and his troops are paid more regularly than Sun's. Wang has seized almost all of the province's salt revenues, and his troops occupy the major salt works, besides some in the hands of the Navy. Wang Yongyi, Wang Yongquan's brother, is Defense Commissioner of parts of Xinghua and Quanzhou, and is tasked with forcing opium cultivation on the locals.
* An incident precipitating a province-wide crisis occurred over January. A corporal of the Wangs' Salt Gabelle troops, who were infamous for "accusing villagers of having smuggled salt and fining them," was beaten to death by locals. On January 12th one hundred solders descended on the village in question and extracted a huge settlement, then proceeded to "Phang Bo" (?) to collect a tax either on opium cultivation or on kitchen stoves (accounts differed). A party of villagers resisted, the village elder was killed, and "a general melee ensued," where two additional villages sent parties to assist in the ambush, and some 70 soldiers were killed. The next day a punitive expedition was sent which massacred everyone in the village, said to number some 20,000 people. (The army claimed to have killed fewer people, but "Governor Sa" (?) suggested that deaths among fugitives from the 80 or so villages in the region were greater than deaths from the massacre itself).
* In February Wang Yongquan was convinced to contribute men, money, and boats to an "expedition" to northern Fujian, where Sun Chuanfang himself joined up with Zhou Yinren's redeployed army; Sun's flight was said to have shocked Wang, who thought he had assuaged doubts about his loyalty by his contributions to the expedition. A few days later Wang's detectives executed a passenger on a steamer from Amoy, said to be an emissary from Zang Zhiping; this execution was said to help make Sun's mind up to strike down Wang. At this point, Zhou Yinren intercepted and captured a convoy of materiel ordered by Wang from Tianjin, which was making its way through Yanping. Thereafter Zhou seized the Fuzhou arsenal, and Wang was forced to flee Fuzhou into Wang Yongyi's protection, with only a small portion of his men following him.
* In desperation, Wang is said to have offered to combine forces with Zang to counter-attack Sun, but Zang was said to have refused and instead accepted a "pardon" from Sun. "In any case, it has always been rather against Wang than against Sun that Tsang has been standing out." (!)
* Mentions that central Fujian is dominated by a "famous Robin Hood" 盧興邦 (?)
* Summary of the Amoy Consul's report of March 1923 simply describes the conflict between Zang and the Japanese ambassador over the Taiwanese jimin.
