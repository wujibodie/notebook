* Calls the Single Whip system "an extremely important turning point" and "the beginning of the modern land tax system." Even so, the Single Whip reform "was but a branch stream in the great current of that time," i.e. in the context of monetarization of the whole economy.
* The Ming dynasty always allowed commutation of 本色 grain into silk, coin, silver, etc., and silver was "not infrequently" used for this purpose even before the 16th C.
* Labor service was divided into two parts: that done by the person called himself, and that for which the taxee paid the government to hire a professional; the latter was called 銀差 and was paid in silver. Eventually people would also be allowed to hire their own replacements for manual labor 里差。
	* In general, the Lichai was considered more onerous and was exacted on the wealthier households (in part because they could be charged for any irregularities), while the Yinchai was levied mainly on smaller households.
	* The wealthier families were also charged with providing horses, carriages, and/or boats for the mail relay.
	* The households with the most adult male members were also those chosen for the 民壯 militia duties.
* The above system "deteriorated into irretrievable ruin" \[by what period? unclear...\] due to the collaboration of powerful/wealthy families and the rotating lijia heads to alter the registers to help push tax burdens onto the poor and connectionless.
	* Step one was the blurring of boundaries between government and private land, as some imperial relatives, nobles, officials, and soldiers encroached on neighboring private property, while others transferred their land to cultivators; "the result was that some of the private land was accorded the treatment of government land (such as exemption from taxes and labor service), and some of the tenants of the government could mortgage and sell the land is if it were private."
* In general the system as set up in the Hongwu reign was simply too complicated to accurately track the shifts in land use and ownership over the years; there could be as many as 1000 different tax rates within a single county, and the huge variety of forms of land transfer meant it was often impossible for the government to track who owed which taxes.
* Commutation made it easy for officials to rob the people, by charging artificially high rates and keeping a portion; often they even demanded commutation into silver in order to steal a portion.
* By the Jiajing reign, peasants were routinely oppressed by tax burdens and began to flee their lands en masse, even as the central government's revenue did not meet its expenses.
	* Even so, it was the labor service, not the land tax, which was most confused, and which "directly caused the adoption of the Single-Whip method." 15
* "The final fatal defects of the system were its excessive complexity and detail. Since the administrative personnel were not qualified, and, at the same time, the powers of supervision by the people who paid the taxes were exceedingly small, the more detailed the laws the greater were the opportunities for abuse. Therefore, the statesmen who initiated the reforms started principally with the simplification of the system of land tax and labor service." 19
* Two movements of simplification in the Zhengde and Jiajing reigns: First, the unification of all charges into one tax; second, commutation of all taxes into silver.
	* Movement of 均量/均則: re-registration of land with fewer categories of quality and tax rate; reduction to two or three rates; this reduced the room to abuse the system by reclassifying land to avoid taxes.  Often combined with re-measurement of the land. "Wherever the Single-whip Method was adopted, the chün-liang had already been in practice."
	* Combination of the Summer and Autumn taxes into one payment due in autumn; and incorporation of minor taxes on silk, floss 絹，fodder, etc. into the basic land tax. The miscellaneous labor services were also added into the 均搖; and more and more services were moved from the lichai to yinchai. E.g., during the Zhengde and Jiajing reigns many places started collecting taxes for postal services instead of requiring special service.
		* Fixed rates of silver commutation were set for most tax items. "After a sufficient length of time had passed, both the government and the taxpayer logically and easily forgot the original meaning of the commutation of taxes, and payment was effected entirely in terms of silver, at fixed rates." These rates lost touch with market fluctuations in commodity prices. 22-23.

The Single Whip

* Most important difference w/ preceding system: whereas the earlier Ming tax code took the household as unit, the Single-whip took the adult male 丁.
	* In order to avoid abuses, the Single-whip method dropped the idea of household "grades."
* "Teng T'sai-ch'iao" of Yuyao County (part of Shaoxing) pioneered a merger of the land tax and labor tax into a single silver levy on adult males and **land in 1567; his method was adopted in multiple Zhejiang counties (just the rest of Shaoxing?)**
