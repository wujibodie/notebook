* Three questions about development of lineages:
	* 1\. What precisely distinguished post-Song lineages from earlier descent groups? (“What were the crucial innovations linked with this type of descent group, and when did they appear?”)
	* 2\. Why did these changes appear? (“Was one change the crucial one, leading to the others, or were several independent changes in practices and attitudes involved?”)
	* 3\. What was the relationship between elite leadership and these new group formations?
* Qingming festival develops by the mid-Tang into crucial day for ceremonies at ancestral graves, partially with Buddhist influence, and this shift is only gradually and hesitantly accepted by Confucian ritual specialists in the Song; this is crucial because “for the development of group consciousness among sets of local agates, two innovations are particularly important: the inclusion of _early ancestors_ in rites, and the practice of everyone visiting the graves _on the same day_.” 23
* Takes approach that changes to ritual were primary, rather than ritual changes being motivated by economic/political concerns, largely due to chronology; new gravesite ceremonies appear before articulation of lineages as corporate actors.
* One of the key challenges which older model of intergenerational “communal families” living together and sharing property in common was that they rarely survived a member achieving office, because the demands of mobility and resistance to “communalizing” official salary would push the officeholder to split off. Lineage organization as a way to solve problem of communal organization in a way amenable to interests of wealthy and prominent members
	* Ouyang Xiu’s genealogy extended to five degrees of kinship: among other things, an attempt to _limit_ the scope of relatives who could make claims on the resources of prominent men
	* Fan Zhongyan’s lineage estate was partially modeled on Buddhist monastic organization (!) in its allotment of communal lands sufficient for maintenance of basic livelihood of all members + subsidies for rituals and students, while individual households retained their own property. Fan’s model was much-praised but little-imitated; most lineage “estates” were small and only covered ritual expenses.
		* Lineage estates as survival and modification of the centralized labor organization model of the monasteries
