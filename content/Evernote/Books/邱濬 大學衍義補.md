製國用
總論理財之道 上

Begins with quotation from Shangshu. Yu says that after he controlled the waters and Ji began cultivating grain, the people began trading their excesses to acquire what they lacked, and thus the people had grain and the lands were at peace.

* Cai Shen: "懋勉其民徙有於無，交易變化其所居積之貨也"
* "而山林川澤之貨又有無相通以濟匱乏” - need for trade across geographies; implicit criticism of rulers who monopolize non-agricultural natural resources. 
* Qiu quotes the Yijing to define 財 as that which gathers people. "懋遷有無化居” is the basis for all subsequent 理財之法。Exchange is the eternal means of managing wealth. 
	Qiu quotes Guanzi: "藏富於民,” store wealth among the people. Qiu wants to argue that this is compatible with 理財, and in fact the purpose of wealth management is to spread wealth among the people. 
	

Yu divided the land into three tax categories, higher, middle, and lower; Qiu says that later dynasties failed to revise these classifications (despite changes in productivity), and thus routinely overtaxed some while failing to get enough to cover expenditures. 

王制：Expenditures should be decided at the end of the year, after all income (grain) has been collected. The state should always have enough grain in reserve for at least nine years. 

	Qiu: In early times 塚宰 was 宰相. Modern policy is for the state to save one quarter of its income each year, with the goal of always having at least 9 years worth of savings for each 30 years of income. No change since the Shang dynasty. Goes on to describe the methods for accounting all income and likely expenditures at the provincial level on down. 
	
	(Ban Gu cites Jia Yi on the idea of saving 9 years worth of surplus from 30 years of income)
	
	Implicit assumption that central government is responsible for distributing famine relief. Zhu Xi disagreed (!), favoring the idea of local granaries managed by gentry (社倉). Alternative to Wang Anshi’s state-run 青苗 agricultural loans.
	

《周禮·春官》：天府祭天之司民、司錄，而獻民數、穀數則受而藏之。

	Commentary from Lin Zhiqi: Frequent census taking is the key work of ensuring peace. Guanzi said there should be 5 farmers for every 2 merchants and artisans; the ratio of 本 to 末 occupations is part of this census. Data on population and production vital to maintain wealth. 自古在昔先民有作，其所以經綸圖維，以富邦國、以生萬民者，其要實在乎此，孰謂其可忽而不思，以坐視夫民之窮哉?
	
	Qiu Jun: Since population rises and falls, and harvests are good or bad, the court needs to send officials to keep yearly accounts of every village. Practice starts from Zhou. Need to lower taxes if population lowers, and check expenditures if costs are eating savings; then bad harvests will not cause calamity and rebellion. 
		Richard: “reclaiming” the idea of government being well-informed and planning ahead. Need to argue that notion of 國計 is not inherently the same as  greedy expropriation 聚斂/興利. 
		

大學：君子先慎乎德，有德此有人，有人此有土，有土此有財，有財此有用。外本內末，爭民施奪，是故財聚則民散，財散則民居。是故言悖而出者亦悖而入，貨悖而入者亦悖而出。

	朱熹："Land” here means “the country.” The ruler must first have virtue to gain the country, and on gaining the country will not have to worry about goods or use. If the ruler focuses on wealth, he will fight with the people. If the ruler betrays his 命the people will betray him; if he steals and hoards, the people will seek to steal from him.
	
	The goal is for the ruler to realize that he and the people want the same things, and to use this to give the people the best possible well-being. 
	

生財有大道，生之者眾，食之者寡，為之者疾，用之者舒，則財恒足矣。

	朱熹：The way of sufficiency is to focus on _ben_ and to economize. Confucius said that the first work of governing is to ensure the people have food; after that expenditures must not exceed income. 
	
	Qiu: The way to gather wealth is exclusively through agriculture; the state needs to focus on encouraging production, and this is not the greedy way of Guanzi and Shang Yang. Four key words for management of wealth: 勤/怠，儉/奢。
	

仁者以財發〈（猶起也）〉身，不仁者以身發財，未有上好仁而下不好義者也，未有好義其事不終者也，未有府庫財非其財者也。

	孟獻子曰：「與其有聚斂之臣，寧有盜臣。」此謂國不以利為利，以義為利也。Zhu Xi says: 聚斂之臣 steal from the people, while 盜臣 steal from the ruler; thus a ruler who 以義為利 will prefer the latter. Cites Sang Hongyang, Yuwen Rong, as examples of people who hurt the people to gain government revenue. Quotes Lu Zhi on notion that the people are the base of the state, and 財 is their heart. Some rulers wrongfully value ministers who can gather wealth, without realizing it is being stolen from the people. 
	
	孟子曰：我能為君充府庫，今之所謂良臣古之所謂民賊也。
	
	金履祥: Use of petty people to gather wealth inevitably leads to loss of the peoples’ faith, and thus unrecoverable disaster. 
	
	Qiu: Analogy between accounts of the government and those of the family, with the difference that the government can take from individual families but families have no one to take from; government exactions thus cause direct suffering to individuals. Summary: basic attitude towards wealth is management of expenditures and encouragement of sufficiency for the common people. 
	

總論理財之道 下
子曰：「節用而愛人。」孟子曰：「無政事則財用不足。」

	Yang Shi: Wasteful expenditures end up hurting the people; loving the people starts from frugality. 
	
	Qiu: "愛之一言萬世治民之本，節之一言萬世理財之要."
	
	The state does not seek for wealth, only for political ends. This means it does not go beyond the standard taxes.
	

趙簡子使尹鐸為晉陽，請曰：「以為繭絲乎，抑為保障乎？」簡子曰：「保障哉。」尹鐸損其戶數。

	Hu Yin: Defense is more important than taxation. This is a story about how the state and people compete for resources; a good ruler will prefer to have government shortfalls than to squeeze the people. Examples of Han Wendi, Jingdi: low taxes and no worry of insufficiency. Tang Xuanzong. Dezong: High taxes, poverty of people and consequent poverty of state. 
	
	Qiu: If the state lets the people be, they will voluntarily work for and protect it; if it focuses on taxing all their necessities and overworking them, they will answer brigandage with brigandage (上以賊道待下，下亦以賊道應之). Argues that Lijia system means that most people (those not responsible for e.g. military service) are able to pursue private wealth and manage family affairs unimpeded (今損其戶數，則一夫應公家之征求，餘夫營私家之衣食，生理既厚).
	
	Richard: Jinyang was one of the most prosperous commercial towns of the Warring States period
	

唐陸贄曰：「地力之生物有大數，人力之成物有大限，取之有度、用之有節則常足，取之無度、用之無節則常不足。生物之豐敗由天，用物之多少由人，是以先王立程，量入為出，雖遇菑難下無困窮，理化既衰則乃反是。桀用天下而不足，湯用七十里而有餘，是乃用之盈虛在於節與不節耳，不節則雖盈必竭，能節則雖虛必盈。」

	Qiu：Economizing creates wealth out of poverty, and wastefulness creates poverty out of wealth. Recalls Lu Zhi’s historical examples, and mentions Lu’s use of Han Wudi to advise his own emperor that one can change course midway and save oneself. 
	

蘇軾曰：「為國有三計，有萬世之計，有一時之計，有不終月之計...

	Goal of economic management is to plan for the future such that reserves are always enough that no disasters (drought, flood, banditry) will affect the people. This means spending less than income and not eating into savings. 量入為出
	

蘇轍: 3 kinds of waste（冗）: wasteful officials, wasteful armies, and wasteful expeditures (court expenses?). If these are cut to a minimum, there will be enough resources. 

	Qiu: In the Ming dynasty, officials and soldiers are not a source of especially wasteful spending. The real problem is court expenses: gifts, decorations, and religious/purely frivolous expenditures. These could all be cut without losing anything, and none of them are acceptable reasons to eat into savings. 
	

Final comment: "財生於天、產於地、成於人，所以制其用者君也”. Only way to fulfill the mission of the state is to employ calculation and statistics to manage finances. Main role of ruler is to regulate expenditures. Ultimately ruler in position of head of household, except responsible for the good of every individual household in the world. These expenditures are too numerous and unregulated and thus have grown to unmanageable proportions. Claims that the Daxue holds up economic management as a crucial means to 平天下.

銅楮之幣 上

《管子》曰：湯七年旱，禹五年水。人之無勣〈（糜也）〉有賣子者，湯以莊山之金鑄幣而贖人之無勣賣子者，禹以曆山之金鑄幣以救人之困。

	The origins of money come from need to rescue people from disaster, not the pursuit of property. 
	

Guanzi: 3 ranks of currency. 

	Qiu: These currencies predate the three dynasties, and the role of the state is to equilibrate their values by choosing when to store and when to circulate them. 
	

Guanzi: The form of currency comes from the Quanfu of Zhougong. Commentators accept this idea. The Zhou system involved currency valued relative to gold (measure) and silk (store of value); the government ensured consistent balance between value of these three.

	Different government offices administered different parts of revenue; money was only used for minor day-to-day expenses. 
	

Zhou Jingwang was concerned money was too expensive, and so coined more valuable currency. However, this caused the people to turn towards earning money instead of focusing on agriculture, and poverty ensued. 

	Qiu says this was the beginning of 文 on money. 
	
	Similarly, the reduction of money to gold and bronze 半兩 dates to Qin Shihuang and start of private coinage came in the time of Han Wudi. Doubtful precedents
	

賈山曰：「錢者，亡用器也而可以易富貴，富貴者人主之操柄也，令民為之，是與人主共操柄，不可長也。」

	If the people have control of money, they will see the opportunity to go from poor to rich, and all will incline towards the pursuit of money. This is the mechanism by which chaos ensues from lack of firm state control over monetary policy. 
	

In Yuandi’s time, it was suggested to ban the use of money and switch to trading cloth and food. 

	This would be a mistake because cloth and food have real uses which would be harmed if they were used as means of exchange. It is necessary to use useless things to trade in order to avoid waste. 
	

Huandi’s time, a proposal was made to cast valuable coins to save the people from famine.

	Liu Tao: This is getting things backwards; the way to save the people from hunger is to reduce taxes and let them save themselves. 
	

Wu Sun Quan cast enormous coins which were too valuable for the peoples’ use; this was the beginning of later practices of states casting money which only rulers could find uses for, thus going against the basic principle of money (that it should be used to facilitate exchange of goods, not for profit). 

Southern Qi's Kong Si: There are two possible problems faced by currency systems: overly valuable currencies are rarely used and hard to accumulate, while overly cheap currencies are easily forged. There is no way to fully prevent counterfeiting, but the best solution is to mint coins of medium value (五銖) with difficult-to-copy patterns; but this requires the state to not skimp on copper or labor costs (not 惜銅愛工). 

	Qiu: 自古論錢法者多矣，惟南齊孔鋋所謂不惜銅、不愛工，此二語者萬世鑄錢不易之良法也。
	

Richard: Historically, Qiu Jun attempted to create a currency system where silver, paper money, and bronze coins all had a place. Silver was taken to be the standard of value, bronze coin was to be used for petty transactions, and paper money was to serve for large-scale commercial transactions; but his scheme was never implemented. 

銅楮之幣 上

In the time of Tang Xuanzong, it was debated whether to allow private mints. Liu Zhi memorialized that it is the role of the ruler to manage the relative values of currency and goods, so that neither farmers nor merchants suffer. Government must release money when money is too valuable and release goods when they are too expensive. Also points out that the only way for private mints to be profitable is if they adulterate their coins with lead, but this will harm everyone. 

	The goal of government is to help the people; it can do this by using 利 as a tool for 義, but must not allow pursuit of profit to become an end. Thus the regulation of values to benefit the people. 
	

Xianzong attempted to ban private fortunes over 5000 _guan_.

	Qiu praises this as an attempt to balance the value of money and goods. Old wisdom is that people who gather money try to circulate it; hoarding thus does not fall within the ideal system. 
	

In the Zhou of the Wudai, the government stopped casting coins, and bronze was slowly converted into utensils and Buddhist statues; when minting began again these bronze goods were collected for smelting. 

	Qiu praises the Zhou ruler for not hesitating to melt Buddhas to serve the people. 
	

Criticizes the practice (starting in Song dynasty) of minting coins with reign titles, since constant reissuing for each new emperor means coins have to be collected from the people. 

Wang Anshi ended a prohibition on the use of bronze \[hoping to stimulate mining\], but this meant a reduction in total bronze supplies as people smelted now-valuable coin for bronze implements and traded coins abroad. 

	Hu Yin: As long as it’s possible to earn profit by smelting bronze coin and trading it abroad, there will never be enough at home. 
	
	Qiu: Liu Zhi argued that the value of bronze is not intrinsic, but is set by demand. If the use of bronze is prohibited, demand and thus value will be low, so minting coins will be cheap. Wang Anshi was thus misguided in allowing competition for state use of bronze. 
	

《周禮》：小宰以官府之八成經邦治，四曰〈（八成中此其四也）〉聽稱責以傅別。

	Qiu sees this as the ur-text for concept of “空文" money. Seems dubious.
	

Also mentions Han Wudi’s attempt to use arbitrarily valued deerskin currency as a forerunner of paper currency.
