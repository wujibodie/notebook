State Formation Reconsidered
System and Process

* Makes the basic point that premodern (“traditional,” “feudal,” “agrarian”) moral and legal orders are often presented as having more systematic coherence and long-term stability than can really be assumed, because these “orders” were usually codified for the first time at the time of their disintegration, in order to make political claims; for example, the British colonial administration drew up “bodies of rights” supposed to apply in court for all Indian villagers everywhere. Seeing the 18th or 19th century legal codifications of “traditional rights” as reflective of a really existing “moral order” is thus an anachronism.
	* “In short, skeptical detachment is a vital instrument for the historian when faced with reconstructions of “systems” of thought and social structure in past and present that betray a more complete extent of coherence and closure than is justified in terms of the available evidence, and which may even be directly contradicted by the range and complexity of the latter.” 31
	* At the same time, “substantivism,” “the will to create order,” is a crucial part of each social complex. Proposes to “define and demarcate this substantive dimension”. 33
* Proposes to reject the idiom of individual “reformers” and discrete “systems” in favor of describing a process of steady and decentralized change as strategies and vocabulary shift in response to continuous processes of record-keeping and state/familial intergenerational reproduction build up a “library” of techniques for fiscal management and political administration. Crucial role of paper in facilitating growing complexity and development of scribal “cadres” at every level, down to that of village money lenders.
* “Administration and the processes of centralization were far from being questions of a few isolated and brilliant capital cities and great courts… they can best be understood in terms of a dispersed and many-headed set of recesses infused by the managerial activities of numerous household economies, small and large, a pool of experience from which household and court would select the combinations of elements considered suitable in each case.” 55
