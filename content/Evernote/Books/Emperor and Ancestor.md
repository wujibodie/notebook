83: In 1535, Guangdong Governor Dai Jing was preparing registration tables for "the area covered by the _lijia,_" which included marking the extent and location of each _li_ (called _tu_ in Guangdong), in preparation for a _junyao_ reform; the _junyao_ reform in general was credited to having been introduced by Assistant Administration Commisioner Jie Ji in the 1450s.

* Cites the 1535 and 1561 _Guangdong Tongshi_

97: Ding Ji, the magistrate of Xinhui, Guangdong, appointed in 1479, was known for shifting labor levies to cash payments: "Ding Ji was noted by Chen Baisha for having regularized payment of cash by leaders of the _lijia_ in lieu of corvée in Xinhui county. The reform was said to have been instrumental in alleviating arbitrary demands by yamen clerks. Chen's description of the reform indicates the extent to which _lijia_ corvée service had eroded by the end of the fifteenth century. However, he was also noted for putting into practice in the county the official rituals promulgated by the Hongwu Emperor in the early years of the Ming."

* Citing 陳白沙，陳獻章集，101-103.

107: "By 1565, as the result of a memorial by a very capable official from Nanhai county, Pang Shangpeng, imperial sanction was given to allow descendants of military households to register separately." 111: An official named Zhu Jian serving around 1440 had written a set of regulations for the practical management of labor services for government, in which he decried the inequalities and inefficiencies of the _lijia_ labor requisitions, saying that "it is necessary that tax and labor service be put on an equal footing. For example, in arranging a roll for messenger service, people should be placed according to the amount of tax they pay, and in serving as heads, labor service should be assigned according to the number of registered males;" he says that in his time, labor service is differential based on tax payments and number of registered males, and the tax liabilities and registered numbers of males are not accurate. **Faure characterizes Zhu's concerns as an attraction to "the idea that _lijia_ might be rescued  by fair distribution (_junping_)."**

* Faure's definition of _junping_: "In order to produce fairness, the local government offices had to be harnessed. In the first place, it was necessary not only to reduce the services that might be imposed but to itemize them. In the second place, it was soon possible to substitute money for some of these itemized services. In the face of the massive complexity of Ming taxation, equalization has often appeared to later historians to be the creation of another tax category, but it was really another step on the way to monetizing and routinizing labor service into what soon came to be known as the single-whip method of taxation".
* Supposedly by the term as  巡安 of Dai Jing, in 1535, "equalization had become a set of rules."
* Zhu Jian 朱監 朱簡斋公奏議, 1713
* Also mentioned in _Guangdong Tongshi_ of 1561 (Huang Zuo comp.)
