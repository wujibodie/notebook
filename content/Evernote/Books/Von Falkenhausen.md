Weekly written response to assigned readings due each Sunday evening, ending with one discussion question for class.

Guest lecture on the Tanguts in Week 3 

Focus on disparate caravan stops rather than linear routes - considerations of each entrepôt’s local history, political affiliations, local market hierarchies, with consideration that most trade occurs between immediate neighbors rather than with more distant centers. In general, north-south orientations is more central in the histories of most entrepôts, with the long-distance luxury trade entering into the daily life of these centers as a more occasional filigree.

Prehistory of Eurasia - Week 3
First Lapis lazuli found in the Chinese core lands dates to no earlier than the Han Dynasty - strong evidence that the trading networks of Central Asia were not firmly connected until the reign of Han Wudi.

* Horse-riding groups were not found on the Chinese borderlands until the 1st millennium BCE, but even as late as the 3rd century BCE horse-raising nomads in the region still relied on carts or chariots, at least for elites.

Kucha as main site of Tocharian B, Agni main site of Tocharian A, mutually unintelligible languages despite being relatively close to one another in the northern Tarim Basin. In the western cities of the Tarim Basin, other Indo-European (Iranic) languages were spoken (Tumshuqese in Aksu, Khotanese in Khotan)

* Tocharian is a linguistic isolate that represents a completely different branch of the Indo-European family than the Iranic branch; likely came from a population which split in the 2000s BCE and spent time in southern Siberia
* Sogdian also a Middle Iranic language, spoken in Bukhara, Kabudhan, Samarkand, and Panjikent
* Tocharian A and B were written in Brahmi script, an Abugida similar but unrelated to Sanskrit; this script was transmitted along with the Buddhist religion in the early centuries CE
* Sogdian script is an Abugida derived from Aramaic, with a few different varieties. Sogdian texts were also written in Syriac (largely Christian texts) and Manichaean script (Manichaean documents)
* Earliest Turkic writing is found in stone carvings of the northern steppe, known as the Turkic Runic alphabet, (fl. 600s-900s CE)
* Old Uyghur was based on Sogdian script, except written vertically right-to-left, presumably influenced by Chinese. Largely disappeared after the Uyghur kingdoms were wiped out by the Mongols, but the Mongolian writing system was closely based on it, and then Manchu was in turn based on Mongolian.
* Tangut is an entirely separate writing system, based visually on Chinese (square characters with radicals, though not pictographic or phonographic meaning), but without any clear relatives. Each character represents one syllable and was supposedly each translatable to a given Chinese character.
