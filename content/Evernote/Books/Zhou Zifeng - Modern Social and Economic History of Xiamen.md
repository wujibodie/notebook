* In the early years of the Republic, the population of Xiamen was 34% natives of Xiamen, along with 2% Guangdong and 1% Zhejiang natives. The majority of the populace thus consisted of people registered as natives of other counties in Fujian. 73-74
* The major sorts of _bang_ associations were local place - profession groups, such that e.g. Xinghua and Wenzhou people dominated the rickshaw business, the Tong'an clans ruled the docks, Fuzhou people were known for their "three blades" (tailor's scissors, barber's scissors, and cook's cleavers), Longyan people dominated dry goods stores, etc. The second type was the artisan guilds, tied together by master-apprentice relations.

Minguo-era Xiamen Shanghui

* In 1913, the Xiamen Chamber of Commerce had 435 members, with 42 board representatives (yiyuan), and met 117 times; compared to Fuzhou, the number of members was lower, but the board itself was larger and met much more frequently.
	* The Chamber's annual finances were routinely in the red, creating a situation where the Chamber as a whole was reliant on the members who were interested and able to pay its expenses from their own pockets. 109
	* The Chamber cooperated with Yuan Shikai's government, particularly in the collection of taxes; Huang Shijin played a major role in the collection of the "救國儲金" tax, with the Shanghui collecting 400,000 yuan for the purpose. However, when Yuan declared himself emperor in 1915, most board members fled to avoid further cooperation with his government.
* The Shanghui steadily became stronger in Xiamen politics throughout the 1910s, especially under the rule of Li Houji (who seized Fujian in June of 1917), as Li's need for steady income and lack of administrative capacity compelled him to allow the Shanghui a great deal of autonomy in exchange for their cooperation with forwarding tax monies. Also, the individual members of the Chamber came to positions of increasing social prominence due to the increase of their wealth and their highly visible participation in governmental and charity organizations.

	* Interested in the "Nanyang Gonghui" and "Shitu Gonghui" which Hong was involved in.
	* Took control of the preexisting association of dockworkers/import-exporters called the 公幫 and reorganized it in 1920 as the 商業益同人公會, with as many as 300 armed members and a number of charitable arms (firefighting, soup kitchens, clinics).
* The Shanghui also played the important role of mediating between the Huaqiao and local society, for example by contacting the Singapore Shanghui for help with charity for Tong'an after it suffered "military disasters" in May of 1924 (!), or by managing the funding of schools intended for the children of huaqiao.
* Zang Zhiping was first sent to Xiamen after his armies withdrew from fighting with Chen Jiongming on the border with Guangdong in May of 1918. On Zang: “臧世反對孫傳芳獨立。。。以廈門為皖系在閩的反孫據點，軍政開支多仰給於廈門，苛捐雜稅層出不窮。商會變為臧世的勒索對象."
* Zang's time in Xiamen had the effect of separating out the Xiamen elite "臧致平竭澤而漁的賦稅政策對廈門地方菁英起了分化作用。" The first mechanism for this was **_opium._** Hong Xiaochun in particular took on the farming of the Maxiang opium tax, which he estimated could bring in 300,000 yuan yearly.
* The Shanghui was contacted by the Haijun to negotiate Zang's surrender of Xiamen, and also to pay a settlement to Zang in compensation. Representatives of the Shanghui assisted the Navy in demobilizing Zang's remnant troops.
	* According to a 南鐸日報 article of 07/16/1924, Yang Shuzhuang asked the Shanghui to hold a meeting to discuss the fact that monthly military expenditures at Xiamen amounted to 100,000 yuan, while combined tax income only brought in 30,000.
* The Haijun carried over Zang's opium monopoly, but opened a new "Naval Provisions Branch Office" headed by one Zheng Rulin 鄭汝霖, who did not have good relations with local society, and merchant strikes in protest of commercial taxes occurred frequently from July 1924 through the end of 1925, with the _jimin_ taking part as well. After 1925 the Haijun, and particularly Lin Guogeng, attempted to establish close relations with the Shanghui. 117
	* Lin Guogeng also began attacking the privileges of the Taiwan _jimin_ at the same time, garnering further support from the merchants. "故自一九二六年至一九三二年間，海軍與商會漸次形成唇齒相依之關係，前者整頓治安和興辦廈門市政，滿足商人階級渴求社會穩定之心態；商會則向海軍提供項款與輿論支持。兩者結成地方資本家與軍人的政治同盟，抵抗來自地方建制以外（如北伐時期的軍閥張毅，及國民政府統治時期的廈門國民黨黨部）的挑戰。" 118
		* I would add the Japanese and Taiwanese jimin, as well as potentially the huaqiao, to the list of outsiders excluded by the Shanghui-Navy alliance.
	* On March 2, 1928, 4 Korean _jimin_ students in Xiamen were seized by the Japanese police and held in the Taiwan Gonghui for supporting Korean independence. The local Guomindang operatives immediately moved to begin anti-Japanese protests and boycotts, with attacks on merchants who sold Japanese goods, strong-arming the Shanghui to go much further than its members wished; by this point the Shanghui was following rather than directing social movements in Xiamen. Protesters went as far as to throw bombs at shops that sold Japanese goods, and to seize violating shopkeepers and parade them in cangues.
* After the KMT took Fuzhou in November 1926, the Fujian Haijun declared allegiance to the Nationalists, and thereafter the three major powers in Xiamen were the local Guomindang, the Zhang-Xia Naval Commander (Lin Guogeng), and the Xiamen Shanghui. The Naval administration under Lin Guogeng resented the intrusion of Nanjing's representative, Zhang Guohui. 124-125
* Over time, although the Guomindang purged the Communists and helped suppress labor movements after 1927, the Shangui found itself aligned with the Haijun in opposition to local KMT activities. The Shanghui's major functions at this time were:
	* 1\. To mediate conflicts between merchants
	* 2\. To maintain financial stability and prevent bank runs, for example by passing emergency regulations after the bankruptcy of two qianzhuang 建通，積金 in 1928
	* 3\. To protest excess taxation. Between 1928 and 1930, the Shanghui made formal protests against 13 new taxes implemented by the KMT
	* 4\. To respond to famine. In 1929, due to grain taxes passed in Zhangzhou, rice prices rose precipitously in Xiamen, and the Shanghui stepped in to prevent exports and ask the naval administration to abolish the rice tax so as to lower prices back to their original levels.
	* 5\. Expressing support, especially through its contacts with other Shanghui elsewhere, for the Guohuo movement, and especially in pushing for “國佈國煙國酒"

* In 1930, the 369 members of the Shanghui were divided into their respective professional Hui, which Zhou separates into five categories: Finance (35 members), Long-distance Traders 貿易 (textiles, firewood, department stores, 122 members), Jiao Merchants 郊商 (Rice, paper, "north" and "Quanzhou" merchants, 19 members), Services (Sanitation, medicine, trucking, warehousing, etc., 77 members), and Food (Flour, pork, fish, sea products, butchers, 105 members). The number of board representatives from each branch was proportional to the total membership of each professional guild. At this point (after the Shanghui was pressured to reform by the KMT) the financiers were no longer the dominant party in the organization.
