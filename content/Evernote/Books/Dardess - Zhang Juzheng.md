“As a boy from a lowly military family, Ming local and regional officials identified him very early on as a child prodigy with a glorious destiny awaiting him. The boy’s response to the encouragement of his elders was to try hard to fulfill their predictions, by reading, writing, and studying intelligently the entirety of China’s formidable archive of philosophy, literature, and history. Zhang never sought to single out any particular line of orthodoxy. It was all potentially useful. He drew at random upon all of it—Confucianism in its many forms, Legalism, Buddhism, or the non- denominational historical record—to build up a giant mental catalogue of ammunition that could be tapped as needed to meet any exigency.“ 18
