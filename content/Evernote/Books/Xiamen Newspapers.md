西報述日本嗎啡營業之發達 - 1915/09/15

* Claims that Japanese sales of morphine, mainly manufactured in Japan itself, are now the most profitable branch of business for the Japanese in Manchuria, and sales also occur in Shandong; the primary route is for morphine manufactured by the Taiwan government to enter China via Fujian. Claims that the Japanese have monopolized morphine smuggling into China ever since a customs crackdown on all but specifically packaged and labelled morphine in 1909. From that time on, Japanese chemists in Scotland (Edinburgh, Glasgow) and Berlin made large purchases of morphine, which they shipped via the Siberian railway; following the outbreak of WW1, they switched to steamship lines. Ends by concluding that, as the Manchurian morphine market is already producing huge profits for the Japanese, the near future of the Shandong and Fujian routes is surely very promising.

The Xiamen docks are in need of repairs, with many Xiamen ships needing to stop at Hong Kong or Fuzhou for maintenance, but the government is in no position to pay for these repairs, so there are calls for merchants to put up the capital. 閩省烟苗㧞除消息 - 1919/03/11
Li Houji's army has revealed extensive poppy planting in Tong'an and Maxiang, and have begun operations to tear up poppy fields under the direction of newly-appointed commander Zang Zhiping. A number of prominent merchants have been implicated in covering up poppy planting, namely Hong Xiaochun, Cai Yucun, Chen Shaowu 陳少梧, and one Lian Zirui 練子瑞, who has been arrested. It is said that in Tong'an alone, five wives have hung themselves after their husbands lost everything in the sweep. How much heavier the guilt on the merchants who incited this illegality!

* A follow-up report on 1919/04/03 clarifies that the merchants involved are Cai Yucun and Chen Shaowu in Tong'an, and Hong Xiaochun and Lian Zirui in Maxiang.

閩省之禁烟談 - 1921/10/27
Two inspectors have been sent by the central government (Jin Yunpeng premier) to Fujian to investigate the state of opium prohibition; 王大亨 arrived in spring, while his brother 王大貞 arrived in September, and immediately sent underlings to investigate local conditions and enact measures for prohibition.
In Zhangzhou, the Commander of the Second Division, Zang Zhiping, is running an opium monopoly (鴉片公賣) under the name of the 利生公司, nominally headed by Zang’s subordinate Liu Changsheng 劉長勝. The “company” sells opium paste in copper tins split up into three categories and marked with the stamps of the Lisheng Company. 25 shops are registered to sell this brand of opium and pay a monthly fee of 5 yuan for the privilege. Total monthly income of 125 + 3750 + 15750 = yuan. The opium paste was imported from Xiamen (who manufactured it?) under military escort. Anyone seen carrying opium not branded Lisheng is liable to suffer confiscation and fines as high as hundreds or thousands of yuan. Zang is also running a gambling tax, which is farmed out to the former Zhishi of Longxi, one Wang Yinsun 汪蔭蓀; 28 licensed gambling halls within the city pay a daily (!) fee of 25 yuan each, plus extras; monthly income from the gambling tax amounts to some 20,000 yuan. Wang Dazhen’s inspection has forced an end to all of this, and the opium merchants involved in the scheme have all fled to Xiamen (!!!).

* The opium den license fees are nominal, and the really money is earned by selling paste to them, whereas in the case of the gambling halls it is the license fees themselves which act as the main “tax”.

閩南之兵戰與匪禍 - 1921/12/28

李厚基與臧致平之暗鬬 - 1922/02/17

* Li Houji’s policies in Fujian have long been limited by Wang Yongquan in the north and Zang Zhiping in the south. Zang has become increasingly insubordinate, refusing to send a single soldier to Li’s campaigns against inland bandits, and instead consolidating his hold over Quan-Zhang. When Li attempted to send his officer Tang Guomo to Xiamen, Zang flatly told him that the price of admission was a war with the Second Division. Zang has been purging, berating, and physically beating Xiamen officials considered loyal to Li. When Zang finally sends a detachment to “aid” Li, he orders them not to fire a shot, and they largely refuse to enter any action (“其餘第二師軍隊均袖手作壁上觀”). People question whether Zang’s behavior is due to secret compacts with Li’s enemies, or meant to pressure Li for back payments of salaries, or simply intended to be irritating. In response to Li receiving a new detachment of 3500 men sent from Zhili, Shandong, and Jiangsu, Zang is said to have begun secret negotiations with Lu Yongxiang and Liu Guanxiong 劉冠雄 of the Haijun for a joint attack on Li; Li for his part is desperately attempting to win the Navy over to his side.

時局變動中之閩局/張吳搆兵中之態度/粵海軍變動之影響 **-** 1922/05/17

* Li, Zang, and Wang are quickly shifting plans in light of the Zhili-Fengtian War, Chen Jiongming’s ouster by Sun Zhongshan, and the defection of a part of the Fujian Navy to Sun even as much of the rest is in the north fighting for Wu Peifu.

李厚基突然赴漳之內幕/接受臧致平軍隊 - 1922/06/13
On June 2, Li Houji suddenly entered Xiamen with a set of troops, and after a few days of confusion, it became clear that he had "promoted" Zang to chief of staff, relieving him of his army. From the 3rd to the 5th Li was welcomed and fêted by the Shanghui (!), and on the 6th he went to Zhangzhou to divest Zang of his army. The reasons for this event are rumored to relate to events of the military conference held in May: first, during discussions on whether to side with Zhili or Fengtian, Zang aroused suspicions by appearing overly inclined to help Zhili (?); second, Zang was said to be in communication with one Du Xigui 杜錫珪 (Yang Shuzhuang's predecessor as grand admiral, noted for assisting Wu Peifu in the Feng-Zhi War), who sought to become Governor of Fujian on the principle of “Fujianese governing Fujian” (杜錫珪謀為閩督、以達閩人治閩之目的、臧密與杜通).
Upon receiving his orders to quit himself of his army, Zang is said to have been furious, and to have showed no sign of moving.

臧致平抵厦後之行動/態度暗昧 - 1922/11/06
Zang Zhiping was arrested by Li Houji, but managed to escape to Shanghai, where he gathered support from the general 王永泉 and the Minnanese community in Shanghai while making overtures to Sun Zhongshan. Wang rebelled against Li, and Zang was able to return to Xiamen on October 21, where he was greeted by his former supporters. The merchant community is described as being thrown in turmoil by the news, with some pleased and others terrified depending on their former relations with Zang. However, forces loyal to Li Houji remained in the area until 11/09 (see "臧致平佔領厦門之滬訊" on that date)

Describes Zang's 閩南自治軍's attack on Quanzhou, which has largely been seized. Fujian is described as returning to normalcy, with commerce on the mend and the banks able to issue sufficient money. This information is provided by the Fujian 同鄉會 in Shanghai.

廈門 - 1923/03/21
Describes the opening of the 自來水公司, with a board made up of 黃秀烺, 黃迺川, 吳蘊甫, 邱世定, 黃奕住, 黃世金, 黃仲訓, 葉心鏡, and Huang Shijin presiding as chairman. The company is said to issued stocks worth 574,000 yuan, and is still inviting more stockholders. Huang Shijin is in talks with the American Asia Construction Company and with Siemens (who eventually win the contract, per Ong). Both Huang Shijin and Huang Yizhu are said to have invested 100,000 yuan each on top of the 574,000 yuan worth of shares.
Note: Huang Yizhu was a famous investor and philanthropist from Nan'an, who earned his fortune in Indonesia before moving to Gulangyu. He also helped found the Zhongnan Yinhang, the Xiamen Telephone Company, etc. His real estate company had the largest capitalization of any real estate firm in Xiamen c. 1930, per Ong, 147. He also owned the single largest number of buildings of any overseas Chinese investor (160 properties).

* 吳蘊甫 was a Taiwanese citizen who shows up in the Jikan as a 巨富 trader in ground sugar, rice, and beans； member of the big Wu family
* 黃秀烺 was a Philippines Huaqiao with roots in Dongshi who moved to Xiamen and founded the Bingji Yinhang 炳記銀行. Died in 1926
* 邱世定: comprador for Taigu (Butterfield & Swire)
* 黃仲訓: A scion of a successful Hanoi huaqiao who built a real estate empire and lived in a famously luxurious mansion on Gulangyu.

閩南局面將有變化 - 1923/10/14
蜀生
The situation in Minnan is extremely complex, and has taken a major turn within 3 days of the presidential election in Beijing (of Cao Kun, on October 10), as Sun Zhongshan has resolved to send 100,000 dollars to the Min Army, while Zhejiang has sent a mixed brigade under General Pan Guogang as well as 2000 rifles and 2000 new recruits to Xiamen to prepare a joint assault on Chen Jiongming in Zhangzhou. Of these allies, only Zang is clearly showing signs of activity, having pressed new taxes on the merchants, raised loans with the city docks as collateral, paid for thousands of new uniforms, and begun exhorting troops about the need to retake the mainland.

Sun Chuanfang has been absent from Fujian for 3 months, during which time Wang Yongquan's armies have had substantial success, and a rift is said to be growing between the two. Two new generals sent from Beijing, Liang Hongzhi and Liu Chuanshou, are both "段係人物," and thus have better relations with Wang. Wang has five brigades, about equal to Sun's forces, and his territories in Quanzhou and Xinghua bring in about 3 or 4 million dollars per year; Sun is thus inevitably lead to fear Wang as a rival. Wang however is pinned down by formidable enemies, namely Huang Dawei, who in September forced Wang to cede the opium tax revenues of Maxiang and Tong'an to his _min_ army. Huang is attempting to eliminate Wang Xianchen, who in turn has fled to Longyan to be taken in by Zhou Yinren. 臧軍渡海反攻情形　王永泉逃滬後消息 - 1924 /03/23
Zang's army has crossed the water to attack Wang Yongquan in Quanzhou. Li Qingbo is one of the officers leading the attack on Wang's coastal forts. This attack has set off a cascade of reprisals, mutinies, and reorganizations among the warlord armies of southern Fujian. Zhangzhou has been emptied of soldiers as the armies there either rush to defend Wang or have become involved in a rebellion by one Su Shi'an in Longyan. Just outside this conflict, the armies of Sun Zhongshan and Sun Chuanfang are preparing to sweep in and contend for control of Fujian.

厦門通信 陳軍並未入閩　同安形勢緊張 - 1924/04/15
蜀生
Shusheng reports that formers rumors about Chen Jiongming invading Zhangzhou are false, based on reports from Zang's army. Furthermore, there are signs that Zang and Chen may be in the process of making a treaty to ward off Sun Chuanfang, as certain "important people" in Shanghai are said to have urged Zang to do. The chambers of commerce of Xiamen, Zhangzhou, and Shima sent a joint letter to both Chen and Zang, as well as a number of other local military leaders, requesting an end to the fighting which they say has wearied the people and damaged business.

Meanwhile, the army of Zhou Yinren has been stockpiling ammunition and digging trenches in Quanzhou, around Anhai and Magang, in order to defend against Sun's army, which is said to have advanced to Tong'an. Zang Zhiping meets with a minor officer called Yang Huazhao (楊化昭), and convinces him to join his army, arguing that he is fighting for Fujian autonomy ("即席開軍事會議、臧提出本軍宗旨、㊀擁護段合肥反對曹吳、㊁扶助閩人自治、不在福建攫取地盤"; Yang is convinced, perhaps in part because Zang agrees to pay his soldiers' salaries, and so Zang gains a corps of 5,000 experienced troops.

厦門海軍財政問題解决 財政委員會之黑幕 海軍自辦鴉片公賣及雜捐 - 1924/05/19
蜀生

* After the navy drives out Zang Zhiping, the Xiamen Chamber of Commerce (led by Huang Xiaochun and Cai Yucun) offers to pay the naval salaries, estimated at 60,000 yuan per month; Shu Sheng sees this as a trick to get the navy to agree to allow the merchants to keep running the opium monopoly bureau. Yang Shuzhuang seems to agree, suggesting that the merchants only contribute 20,000 yuan per month, for three months only. The merchants negotiate, claiming that income from current taxes are insufficient to pay even the lower number, and reminding Yang of his promise to lower customs duties, suggesting that only the opium monopoly will allow both sides to benefit; Yang counter-offers that the navy will take over the opium suppression bureau itself, allowing it to cancel smaller customs duties entirely. 厦門著名漢奸 黃世金被捕解省 黃助敵作惡有據 被捕後人心大快 - 1937/11/05
At time of arrest, Huang is 68 years old and the director of the Xiamen Telephone Co. and chairman of the 自來水公司。He was also a former head of the Shanghui and consultant to the Taiwan Bank. In 1916 he was said to have saved the Taiwan Bank in Xiamen by depositing 100,000 yuan of his own money as well as soliciting help from the Chamber of Commerce to counter a run, and so gained the trust of the Japanese ambassador, who was said to always heed Huang's advice. Even Zang Zhiping grew distant from Huang after discovering his role in secretly arming the Taiwanese bands which fought with Zang's police. Afterwards, Huang did not take part in anti-Japanese boycotts and even particularly hired Taiwan jimin for his companies. He owned millions of yuan worth of properties, but escaped paying taxes by registering himself as a Japanese subject. He was finally arrested after the beginning of the Japanese invasion, presumably to be executed.
