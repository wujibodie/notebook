Chapter 2

* In the early Ming Dynasty, Nan Zhili had more than twice the tax grain burden of any other province, and Suzhou fu alone had a higher quota than all of Zhejiang (the province with the next highest burden). The registered population was ~11 million, only slightly larger than Zhejiang (10.5 million) or Jiangxi (9 million). The grain tax burden per _hu_ for Suzhou’s ~500,000 _hu_ was thus more than 20 times that of Zhejiang. (8-9, 12-13) Nan Zhili was only 3rd in quantity of registered farmland.
* Describes the slow decline into irrelevancy of the initial 魚鱗冊，黃冊 registers, which hit Nan Zhili differently due to the higher proportion there of guantian. Proportion of guantian in all registered land was far higher in the major tax cow prefectures of Nan Zhili (Songjiang 85%, Suzhou 66%) than anywhere else (except a few prefectures in Huguang). As the “rent” on guantian was far higher than the land tax on mintian, this both partially explains the unusually high tax burden in Nan Zhili and was a principle cause for smaller landholders to give up registration of their land in various arrangements with larger landholders, who then had the influence to get that land off the books.
	* Sale price of guantian said to be 1/10th that of mintian, making it a bargain for those who believed themselves capable of removing it from tax rolls…
	* Contemporary observers noted almost immediately after the move of the capital to Beijing that transportation expenses were making the tax burden unbearable to the south. In Xuande 4, a portion of southern tax grain was ordered to go instead to granaries along the Yangtze, but this practice seems to have been abandoned over time.

	* Zhou Chen divided the problem of population flight from tax registers into 7 categories:
		* 1\. 大戶苞蔭: Wealthy households swallowed up land from poorer households, either through payment or bullying, and then enrolling these people as servants, removing them from the lijia rolls.
		* 2\. 豪匠冒合: Artisans from SuSong tended to group in the 2 capitals, with whole extended families crowding into the “households” of single registered artisans in those capitals in order to escape tax burdens.
		* 3\. 船居浮蕩: Families in the watery regions of Suzhou, Songjiang, and Wuhu increasingly moved onto boats and took up lives as itinerant traders and fishers, escaping taxation by constantly moving their residence.
		* 4\. 軍囚牽引: Recruiters attempted to fill up missing spots on army rolls in the border regions “trick the poor” into serving as replacement soldiers. Seems to suggest people might be captured by force.
		* 5\. 屯營隱占: People take advantage of the coastal forts, giving up their own names and taking up the identities of soldier families, or farming secretly on garrison land, or becoming laborers for military garrisons, etc, in any case disappearing off the lichai rolls.
		* 6\. 鄰境蔽暱: People fled to neighboring counties or villages, especially those with poor grain production and thus low taxes, and either disappeared entirely from the registers or giving themselves lower quotas; suggesting that good lands are being abandoned, professions are given up, and family altars are going without sacrifice.
		* 7\. 僧道招誘: Self-explanatory, people becoming monks or moving to farm monastic land to escape taxation.
	* Early in the Ming Dynasty, the court attempted to solve the problem of tax evasion in Nan Zhili mainly through periodic tax forgiveness, but as the situation continued to worsen, they began sending officials to investigate. Zhou Chen was the first such official to make thoroughgoing reform proposals. At the same time other officials were sent to Jiangxi, Huguang, Zhejiang, Henan/Shanxi, and Beizhili/Shandong. This was the beginning of the 巡撫 system (or at least of permanent assignment of xunfu to each province), and the initial remit of this set of officials was focused on the investigation and reform of the provincial tax situation.

Chapter 3

* Describes Zhou Chen’s basic protocol as making interviews with commoners “in the fields,” while also asking the advice of prominent local gentry (賢達之士) and calling for recommendations from talented lower-level officials to be passed up to him.
	* Zhou Chen was sponsored for his post by 楊榮, and had the support of all 3 of the 三楊.
* Zhou Chen’s first step after his appointment was to clean up and resurvey the land registers. He ordered a complete resurvey of all fields and the editing of any false information in the fish-scale registers. Land found to be illegally occupied was redistributed to the entire _li,_ and boundary markers were set up to avoid lawsuits.
* On reaching Jiangnan, Zhou Chen was given a mandate to lower tax schedules on certain kinds of guantian (抄沒田) but not on the “old” guantian, which had the highest rates. J@
